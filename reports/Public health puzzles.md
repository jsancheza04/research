# Birth outcomes and syphilis defy Colombia's health gains

## 0. Bottom line and scope

Patterns that rested on false premises, fell outside 2001-2026 or are already well explained were excluded, leaving **36 puzzles: 18 in Colombia and 18 in other developing countries**. The most surprising and least explained patterns sit at birth and in sexually transmitted infection. In Colombia, low birth weight (LBW) rose to a record **11.3% in 2024** while poverty fell, and LBW among term births also rose. Cesareans became the most common mode of delivery in 2023, with department rates ranging from **5% to 76%** that maternal characteristics do not explain. Gestational syphilis roughly **quadrupled** while congenital syphilis stayed flat at about five times the elimination target. Brazil, Argentina and Chile show the same syphilis surge at the same time, in very different health systems. Outside Colombia, the strongest cases are India's child and women's anaemia, which rose while every determinant improved (and was then dropped from NFHS-6), and the Dominican Republic, where maternal mortality rose with near-universal hospital delivery. Two threads run through the table. First, several headline trends may be **measurement artefacts**: finger-prick haemoglobin, vaccination denominators, registry incentives, rapid-test rollout and coarse gestational-age bins. Separating artefact from real change is a publishable first chapter in each case. Second, Colombia's **2024-2026 insurer takeovers**, covering about 24 million affiliates, are a common shock to tutelas, syphilis, unit closures, TB and vaccination, and all of these outcomes are observable in public data. For a PhD in applied development microeconomics, the five most promising puzzles are Colombia's LBW rise, Colombia's cesarean geography, Colombia's syphilis split, India's anaemia reversal and the Dominican maternal-mortality paradox. Every figure below comes from search-engine snippets, so none is ready for a paper without checking the primary source.

- **What counts as a puzzle.** A pattern counts if four conditions hold. Its core fact must be supported by at least one cited snippet. Its key movement (a rise, stall, reversal or divergence) must fall within 2001-2026. It must contradict theory, diverge from comparable places, or reverse a trend. And its existing explanations must be rated None, Speculation, Descriptive or Partial causal. Labels: [Established] means stated in a cited source snippet (still search-only); [Likely] means inference supported by several facts; [Speculative] means conjecture. In the table, the "Facts" column is [Established] at snippet level unless flagged; "Why it is a puzzle" is [Likely] inference; hypotheses and variation are [Speculative] inference throughout.
- **Search scope and limits.** Five research passes covered maternal/newborn/child health, chronic disease and nutrition, and health systems and infections, each for Colombia and for other developing countries. Together they ran about 135 queries in Spanish and English (Appendix). Only general web search was used. pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov and dane.gov.co were blocked, and NBER, IZA, IDEAS, Google Scholar, SciELO and BanRep were reached only when they surfaced in general search. **No full text was opened, so every figure, quote and link is "search-only."** "No causal paper" means "none surfaced in this search."
- **What remains unverified.** Items still to confirm: department-level cesarean and LBW series for 2023-2025; whether DANE microdata carry exact gestational weeks or day and hour of birth; the MMEIG 2000-2023 estimate for the Dominican Republic; WUENIC series for Colombia, the Philippines and Brazil; the timing of Brazil's SI-PNI registry change; the 2017 change to Brazil's congenital-syphilis case definition; the design of the Cuenta de Alto Costo (CAC) risk-adjustment mechanism; the status of ENSIN 2024; the formal status of the 2026 EPS liquidation order; and every item marked **(nv)** (not verified) in the tables.

## 2. Cross-cutting themes

Several rows may be facets of one phenomenon. Each claim carries a label: [Established] for facts in the notes, [Likely] for well-supported inference, [Speculative] for conjecture.

### (a) Measurement artefacts are a research question in their own right

At least six of the strongest puzzles turn on how a statistic is produced. In India, a paired study found **capillary anaemia of 59.2% vs venous 35.2%** in the same women ([Indian Pediatrics 2022](https://indianpediatrics.net/nov2022/837.pdf)), and the government dropped all seven anaemia indicators from NFHS-6, citing the method ([The Wire](https://thewire.in/health/nfhs-key-indicators-missing-government-clarifies)) [Established]. A level bias alone does not explain a rise between two capillary rounds, so the open question is what changed between rounds: devices, teams, or COVID-era fieldwork [Likely]. In Brazil, papers flag denominators from intercensal estimates, under-reported private doses and survey-registry gaps ([RESS 2024](https://scielosp.org/article/ress/2024.v33nspe2/e2023539)) [Established]. The hypothesis that the switch to nominal registration caused the 2016 drop has no source yet [Speculative]. In Colombia, WUENIC reports that the vaccination **target population fell 12% between 2023 and 2024** and that the 2019 "increase" came from the denominator falling faster than doses ([WUENIC](https://data.unicef.org/wp-content/uploads/cp/immunisation/wuenic_col.pdf)) [Established]. With births collapsing, coverage computed on projected cohorts will move for reasons unrelated to vaccination [Likely].

The syphilis puzzles have the same structure. A 4× rise in Colombian gestational syphilis with flat congenital syphilis is exactly what better screening would produce [Likely], and in Brazil rising acquired-syphilis trends correlate with rapid-test availability ([PLoS ONE](https://ideas.repec.org/a/plo/pone00/0275253.html)) [Established]. DANE's public birth microdata group gestational age as **"28-37" weeks, lumping week 37 with preterm** ([IHSN catalog](https://datacatalog.ihsn.org/catalog/7169/variable/F4/V342?name=t_ges)) [Established]. One 2008-2017 study counted ~19% of births "up to 37 weeks" ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/347564937_El_parto_pretermino_desde_los_registros_del_nacido_vivo_en_Colombia_periodo_2008_-_2017)), and the OECD's ~15% preterm figure for Colombia may reflect the same bin [Likely]. Finally, the CAC kidney registry grew **20-26% a year** while audits flagged **580,517 inconsistencies** ([Consultorsalud](https://consultorsalud.com/?p=344637)) [Established]. If the CAC's redistribution rewards reported cases, insurers have a reason to report more early-stage CKD [Speculative; mechanism design not verified]. The same logic covers Mexico's ENIGH question change (34.2% vs 44.1%), Venezuela's indirect estimates and Bangladesh's stillbirth-neonatal boundary. In each case, a paper that decomposes a headline series into instrument and behaviour is publishable and must come first, before any causal claim [Likely].

### (b) Colombia's 2024-2026 insurer takeovers are a common shock to many outcomes

Interventions in 2024-25 placed **more than 24 million affiliates** in state-run EPS ([El País](https://www.elpais.com.co/colombia/seis-eps-estan-intervenidas-por-la-supersalud-suman-mas-de-24-millones-de-afiliados-0316.html)). The Constitutional Court reversed the Sanitas intervention in June 2025, and a March 2026 presidential order to liquidate about seven EPS was softened the next day ([Vanguardia](https://www.vanguardia.com/colombia/2026/03/18/supersalud-intenta-calmar-la-alarma-tras-el-anuncio-de-petro-de-liquidar-las-eps-en-quiebra/)) [Established]. Several of the table's outcomes moved in the same window. Health tutelas hit **312,500 in 2025**, with the highest per-affiliate rates at Savia, Asmet, SOS and Emssanar, all of them intervened [Established]. Clinics closed obstetric units citing EPS debts of COP 18.9 trillion [Established]. MMR coverage fell from 92.8% to 89.1% [Established]. Drug-resistant TB nearly doubled [Established]. Diabetes control fell to 23.1% [Established]. Gestational syphilis kept rising, and SIVIGILA records the mother's insurer [Established]. The SaludCoop exit is a well-identified template: it raised mortality through congestion and narrower networks ([Serna](https://nsernab.github.io/Insurer_Terminations_and_Mortality.pdf)) [Established]. The 2024-26 shock differs because managers changed while enrollees stayed [Established].

The natural design is exposure by pre-period municipal market share of intervened EPS, staggered intervention dates, and the Sanitas reversal as an "un-treatment." Outcomes would be tutelas, congenital syphilis conditional on gestational syphilis, REPS closures, TB loss to follow-up, PAI coverage and CAC control [Speculative]. Two threats are serious. Intervention timing may be politically targeted, and the birth collapse overlaps the same years and independently drives unit closures and coverage denominators [Likely]. Placebo outcomes that insurers cannot easily move, such as dialysis starts or EEVV deaths, and a contributory-vs-subsidized split partly address this [Speculative].

### (c) Cesareans, low birth weight and prematurity belong in one frame

Four countries combine majority cesarean delivery with stalled or worsening birth outcomes [Established]:

- **Colombia:** cesareans at 47.4% in 2022 and most common in 2023 ([DANE](https://www.dane.gov.co/files/operaciones/EEVV/bol-EEVV-Nacimientos-IVtrim2023.pdf)); LBW at 11.3%.
- **Brazil:** ~57% nationally and 80% in São Paulo private-insurance births.
- **Egypt:** ~72% in 2021.
- **Dominican Republic:** 58%, with rising maternal mortality.

Globally, preterm birth was flat at 9.8-9.9% over 2010-2020 ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news/item/06-10-2023-1-in-10-babies-worldwide-are-born-early--with-major-impacts-on-health-and-survival)) [Established]. A scheduled cesarean at 37-38 weeks produces early-term births, not preterm births. In DANE's 28-37 bin, however, a week-37 cesarean appears as "preterm," so part of Colombia's apparent preterm excess may be an artefact of obstetric scheduling combined with coding [Likely]. This does not explain the **1.5-point rise in term LBW** between 2014 and 2023 ([DANE](https://www.dane.gov.co/files/operaciones/EEVV/2024/19-dic-2024/bol-EEVV-Nacimientos-2024pr.pdf)), which needs a separate channel: composition from the birth collapse, food prices, or multiples [Speculative]. Brazil is the place to test the mechanism, because SINASC records exact gestational weeks, Robson group, hospital code and the timing of Parto Adequado entry [Established in notes as data availability; access to be confirmed]. Results could then be carried to Colombia with residence-occurrence movers and EPS liquidations as shocks [Speculative]. The Dominican case adds maternal mortality: if iatrogenic risk from repeat cesareans (haemorrhage, accreta) drives the rise, deaths should concentrate in direct obstetric causes at high-cesarean hospitals [Speculative].

### (d) The Latin American syphilis surge calls for a comparative design

The surge is synchronized across four systems [Established]:

- **Colombia:** gestational syphilis 5.1 → 21.8 per 1,000 (2014-2024), congenital flat at ~2.5.
- **Brazil:** acquired syphilis at a record ~120 per 100k in 2024; gestational rising and congenital falling since 2019; São Paulo gestational 15.4 → 71.4 per 1,000.
- **Argentina:** 56.1 → 93 per 100k (2019-2024), with Córdoba at 220.3.
- **Chile:** 19 → 31.4 per 100k (2010-2019), with congenital peaking in 2021.

PAHO reports rising prevalence among tested pregnant women in 18 of 26 countries ([PAHO 2023](https://paho.org/sites/default/files/2023-01/2023-cde-3-etmi-plus-prev-elim-cong-syphilis.pdf)) [Established]. Synchrony across a tax-financed SUS, a fragmented Argentine system, high-income Chile and Colombia's insurer model argues against purely national causes such as one country's penicillin shortage or one migration flow [Likely]. In Roraima, Venezuelan migration contributed to congenital counts but was not the main driver ([Rev Epidemiol UNISC](https://seer.unisc.br/index.php/epidemiologia/article/view/18537)) [Established].

A comparative design would harmonize three indicators across countries: gestational per 1,000 births, congenital/gestational ratio, and latent/primary share. It would then use each country's distinct shock: Brazil's staggered rapid-test rollout and the 2017 case-definition change (nv); Colombia's Venezuelan inflows (shift-share, as in Ibáñez et al.) and the 2024 insurer interventions; Argentina's provincial dispersion [Speculative]. If detection drives the trend, the gestational series should jump at rollout and then plateau, the latent share should rise, and congenital/gestational should fall everywhere. If true incidence drives it, acquired syphilis among men should rise first and continue [Speculative].

### (e) Coverage without outcomes

A fifth, looser family links rows 5, 7, 19, 25 and 31. Near-universal hospital delivery coexists with rising Dominican maternal mortality. Universal Colombian insurance coexists with record tutelas and Chocó's MMR above 300. Insurance expansion in India, the Philippines and Indonesia coexists with sticky out-of-pocket spending. Africa's HIV chronic-care platforms coexist with flat hypertension control [Established as facts]. The common question is why expanding access does not move outcomes. The candidate answers (quality, benefit design, rationing by insurers or providers) can be tested with the same tools in each setting [Speculative].

## 3. The 5 most promising puzzles

### Colombia's low birth weight rise, including at term

LBW rose from 9% (2014) to 11.3% (2024pr) while poverty fell and prenatal care improved. Term LBW rose 1.5 points, and the burden is highest in Bogotá and the contributory regime, which reverses the usual gradient [Established]. Existing explanations are descriptive correlates, altitude (constant, so it cannot drive a trend) and migrant odds ratios of ~1.2, which are too small to produce a two-point national rise [Likely]. The first analysis is a shift-share decomposition of EEVV births 2008-2024 into within-cell changes and composition (department, age, parity, regime, nationality, multiplicity), split by term status and delivery mode, with fetal-death microdata to test survival and registration selection [Speculative]. All of this uses public DANE microdata. Altitude bands and SIPSA food prices then give exogenous variation for the term component [Speculative].

### Colombia's cesarean geography

Cesareans rose from 25.7% to the most common mode of delivery by 2023. Department rates span 5-76%, and a multilevel model shows the Caribbean excess (OR 3.88) survives adjustment for maternal characteristics [Established]. The only explanations are a cross-sectional private/public prevalence ratio and a list of possible contextual drivers; no economics paper tests supplier-induced demand in Colombia [Established within this search]. The first discriminating test compares mothers who give birth outside their department of residence (place vs person), and checks whether the excess concentrates in low-risk births and on weekdays, if DANE records day of birth (not verified) [Speculative]. EEVV and REPS are public. EPS liquidations (Saludcoop 2015, Coomeva 2022, Nueva EPS 2024) supply payment-term shocks [Speculative].

### Colombia's gestational-congenital syphilis split, in comparative perspective

Gestational syphilis quadrupled to 21.8 per 1,000 (2024) while congenital syphilis stayed at 2.5, five times the 0.5 target. The steepest rise is in Bogotá, and Venezuelan cases are a minority of the total [Established]. The best causal paper (Ibáñez, Rozo & Urbina) covers acquired syphilis only and ends before the post-2021 rise; everything else is descriptive [Established]. The first analysis tracks the congenital/gestational ratio and trimester at diagnosis by department-year against rapid-test adoption, then estimates gestational syphilis among Colombian nationals against shift-share migrant inflows, with mother's EPS × 2024 intervention as a second shock [Speculative]. SIVIGILA 740/750 microdata are partly public, and Brazil's SINAN provides a comparison case [Established].

### India's anaemia reversal and the NFHS-6 omission

Child anaemia rose from ~59% to 67.1% and women's from ~53% to ~57% between NFHS-4 and NFHS-5, while IFA consumption, sanitation and schooling improved. The government then dropped haemoglobin from NFHS-6, citing capillary sampling [Established]. The only decomposition attributes the change to covariates that improved, and capillary bias explains a level, not a rise [Likely]. The first test uses team and enumerator fixed effects, NFHS-5 Phase 1 vs Phase 2 fieldwork, the shape of the Hb distribution and men's anaemia as a placebo, to measure how much of the rise is instrument [Speculative]. NFHS-4/5 microdata are public through DHS, and NFHS-6 will provide a non-Hb comparison [Established/Likely].

### The Dominican maternal-mortality paradox

Maternal mortality rose about 36% between 2000 and 2020 (PAHO: 107.3 per 100,000) with near-universal hospital delivery and 58% cesareans. Guyana, Suriname and Venezuela show the same combination [Established]. The literature is one 2003 descriptive paper and one 2023 ecological study with counter-intuitive signs [Established]. The first analysis splits the rise by cause (direct haemorrhage and sepsis vs ill-defined codes) and by the maternal share of all deaths of women 15-49, to separate better certification from real deterioration. Hospital-level cesarean rates and Haitian-origin shares follow [Speculative]. ENDESA and ENHOGAR-MICS are public, the WHO Mortality Database is public, and hospital-level bulletins exist as PDFs (microdata not verified) [Established/Speculative].

### Also interesting

- **CKD registry (row 6):** compare the stage mix of new cases with dialysis starts, and run an event study of EPS interventions on reporting vs control. This is a clean incentive-design paper on CAC public data.
- **Tutelas (row 7):** build an EPS × department panel from Defensoría reports against Supersalud financials and judicial digitization (Ley 2213/2022), to test the rationing vs legal-supply channels.
- **Mexico after Seguro Popular (row 9):** recode ENIGH 2024 to CONEVAL criteria to measure the questionnaire effect, then use staggered IMSS-Bienestar adherence for a DiD on OOP and infant mortality.
- **Brazil's vaccination decline (row 10):** test the 2016 drop against the municipal timing of the registry switch and against the 2022 census denominator revision.
- **Colombia's vaccination denominators (row 11):** recomputing municipal coverage with EEVV births is a fast, policy-relevant measurement note.
- **Obstetric closures (row 13):** combine REPS closure dates with IPS payer mix to separate fertility-driven from arrears-driven closures, then measure travel distance using residence vs occurrence in EEVV.
- **UPF tax (row 14):** use the staggered 10/15/20% schedule and taxed vs untaxed substitutes in CPI micro-prices to measure pass-through and substitution.
- **Philippines, HIV and immunization (rows 15-16):** use local condom ordinances and the 2018 testing-age change (nv) for HIV, and admin vs NDHS card coverage for the pre-Dengvaxia collapse.

## Conclusion

The pattern across the table is that the puzzles survive in the gap between indicators that agencies publish separately. Colombia reports cesareans, LBW, gestational-age bins and vaccination denominators in separate bulletins, and nobody puts them together. The same holds for India's capillary survey and its venous benchmarks, and for Brazil's syphilis series and its testing rollout. The strategic implication for a PhD is a three-chapter structure built on one public registry per country [Likely]. The first chapter is a measurement paper on what part of the headline trend is instrument: the 28-37 bin, denominators, rapid tests, registry incentives. The second and third use the shocks the measurement chapter clarifies, chiefly Colombia's insurer takeovers and Brazil's staggered rollouts. That order protects against building a causal design on an artefact, and it produces a publishable paper even if the artefact turns out to explain the whole trend.
