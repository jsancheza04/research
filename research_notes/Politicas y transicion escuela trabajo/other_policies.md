# Non-vocational policy changes in Colombia (1994–2026) that could move the youth NINI/NEET rate: a timeline and sources of variation

Scope: Colombia, 1994–2026, ages 14–28. Vocational policies (SENA, articulación, apprenticeship, Jóvenes en Acción) are deliberately excluded. Today = 2026-11-05.

**Method note.** These notes are based on about 27 web searches. **No pages were opened with WebFetch.** All links are therefore **search-only**: each comes from a search result, and its content is known only from the result snippet. Any fact taken from my prior knowledge rather than from a search result is flagged **"not verified"**. Statements labeled "Inference" are my own reasoning about what variation could be used, not findings from a source.

Motivating fact. Lasso-Valderrama & Vargas (Banco de la República, Borrador 1363) document a sharp rise in the NINI rate between ages 16 and 18, and find that NINI status is highly persistent into adulthood — [IDEAS/RePEc entry, Borrador 1363](https://ideas.repec.org/p/bdr/borrec/1363.html) (search-only). Universidad del Rosario (2024) reports NINI rates of 28.67% for ages 18–24 and 19.30% for ages 25–29 — [URosario Nova et Vetera](https://urosario.edu.co/periodico-nova-et-vetera/nuestra-u/aproximadamente-3-de-cada-10-jovenes-no-estudia-ni-trabaja-urosario-analiza-la-situacion) (search-only). **No search turned up a causal evaluation of any Colombian policy that uses NINI/NEET status as its outcome** (searched in Spanish and English). That gap is itself the opportunity.

## Q1. Schooling-side policies (secondary school): free schooling, Jornada Única, automatic promotion

### Takeaway
Of the secondary-school policies, two are the most promising for a NINI design:
- **The end of the 5% grade-retention cap (Decreto 1290 de 2009, effective 2010).** There is already a published school-level difference-in-differences study showing that it raised dropout among retained students in grades 9 and 11, right at the 16–18 age window. No study looks at NINI outcomes.
- **Nationwide free schooling for grades 0–11 (Decreto 4807 de 2011, from 2012).** It hit upper secondary (grades 10–11, typically ages 15–17) and was preceded by local free-schooling schemes with Sisbén cutoffs. No national causal evaluation was found.

Jornada Única has staggered rollout by school, but the existing evidence covers only test scores.

### Cited Findings

**1a. Free public schooling (gratuidad)**

- **National rule (Decreto 4807 de 2011).** Signed on 20 December 2011 — [MEN, Decreto 4807 de 2011](https://www.mineducacion.gov.co/portal/normativa/Decretos/293375:Decreto-No-4807-de-diciembre-20-de-2011) (search-only).
  - It regulates gratuidad for all students in state schools enrolled in grades transición through 11. Gratuidad means exemption from academic fees (derechos académicos) and from complementary-service fees.
  - It is financed from the "calidad" resources of the Sistema General de Participaciones (Ley 715 de 2001, arts. 16–17).
  - It was later compiled into Decreto 1075 de 2015 — [ICFES normograma, Decreto 4807 PDF](https://normograma.icfes.gov.co/docs/pdf/decreto_4807_2011.pdf) (search-only).
- **Timing and what changed.** A 2014 Oxford Business Group report says public schools became free through the end of upper secondary as of 2012, extending an earlier cutoff at the end of primary — [Oxford Business Group 2014](https://oxfordbusinessgroup.com/reports/colombia/2014-report/economy/discussions-continue-over-whether-to-open-the-sector-to-private-and-for-profit-institutions) (search-only; secondary source).
  - Not verified: the exact earlier national instrument (I believe there was partial gratuidad for some grades and populations in 2008–2011, but I did not confirm the decree number).
- **Earlier local free schooling (Bogotá Gratuidad, 2004).** Bogotá introduced fee reductions in 2004, targeted by Sisbén level.
  - Sisbén level 1 students received the reductions; level 2 and above did not.
  - In grades 10–11, level-1 students also had both academic and other service charges eliminated.
  - Barrera-Osorio, Linden & Urquiola (2007 working paper) evaluate it with a **regression discontinuity at the Sisbén cutoff**, using attendance — [Linden slides, Uniandes](https://economia.uniandes.edu.co/sites/default/files/imagenes/eventos/Leigh-Linden-pres.pdf); [3ie gap-map entry](https://gapmaps.3ieimpact.org/node/4317) (both search-only).
  - The headline estimates were not retrieved.
- **Existing evidence on the 2012 national policy.**
  - The only item found is a single-school case study from Barranquilla (2008–2012, simple regressions). It concludes that gratuidad is not directly related to dropout reduction and relates more to access than to quality. Not generalizable — [Redalyc, Zona Próxima 2015](https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/853/85344718003.pdf) (search-only).
  - I found no national quasi-experimental evaluation of the 2012 extension to grades 10–11, and none that measures NINI.

**1b. Jornada Única (full school day)**

- **Legal basis.**
  - Art. 57 of Ley 1753 de 2015 (Plan Nacional de Desarrollo 2014–2018) amended art. 85 of Ley 115 de 1994 to create the Jornada Única.
  - Decreto 501 de 2016 regulates it.
  - Decreto 2105 de 2017 later modified the decree's definition and duration articles (arts. 2.3.3.6.1.3–2.3.3.6.1.6 of Decreto 1075 de 2015) — [Decreto 501 de 2016, Rama Judicial SIDN](https://sidn.ramajudicial.gov.co/SIDN/NORMATIVA/TEXTOS_COMPLETOS/5_DECRETOS/DECRETOS%202016/DECRETO%20501%20DE%202016%20(REGLAMENTA%20LA%20JORNADA%20%C3%9ANICA%20EN%20LOS%20ESTABLECIMIENTOS%20EDUCATIVOS%20OFICIALES).PDF); [Decreto 2105 de 2017, Ámbito Jurídico](https://ambitojuridico.com/sites/default/files/BancoMedios/Archivos/d-2105-17%28mineduacion%29.pdf) (both search-only).
- **Staggered rollout.** Decreto 501 lets implementation be phased by grade, cycle, level, school, campus (sede), and rural/urban zone — same sources.
  - Not verified: the commonly cited goals of universal Jornada Única by 2025 (urban) and 2030 (rural); the search did not confirm them.
- **Evaluations (all on test scores, none on dropout or NINI).**
  - Hincapié (2016), IDB Working Paper 679, "Do Longer School Days Improve Student Achievement? Evidence from Colombia" — [IDB publication page](https://publications.iadb.org/en/metadata/12427/do-longer-school-days-improve-student-achievement-evidence-colombia); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/idb/brikps/7545.html) (search-only).
    - Design: within-school switches between half and full days (pre-2015 "jornada completa"), with school fixed effects and Saber 5/9 scores from 2002, 2005 and 2009.
    - Result: about +0.1 SD, larger in math, in 9th grade, and in poorer and rural schools.
  - Hincapié Correa (2017), Uniandes undergraduate thesis, on the jornada complementaria in Cundinamarca. It reports positive effects on dropout, but repetition increases — [MinCiencias record](https://redcol.minciencias.gov.co/Record/UNIANDES2_7548ce5917faa94ee0f3cc1910c14a27/Description) (search-only). This is not the national Jornada Única.
  - No study was found linking the post-2015 Jornada Única to teen pregnancy, crime, dropout, or NINI status.

**1c. Automatic promotion and its repeal**

- **Decreto 230 de 2002.** Schools had to promote at least 95% of students who finished the school year — [MEN, Decreto 230 summary](https://www.mineducacion.gov.co/1621/article-162264.html) (search-only).
  - Decreto 3055 de 2002 modified art. 9. It capped repeaters at 5% of students finishing the year and allowed repetition for unjustified absence from more than 25% of activities — [Función Pública, Decreto 3055 de 2002](https://www.funcionpublica.gov.co/eva/gestornormativo/norma.php?i=6828) (search-only).
- **Repeal (Decreto 1290 de 2009).** Decreto 3055 was repealed by art. 19 of Decreto 1290 de 2009 (same Función Pública source).
  - Under Decreto 1290, each school sets its own evaluation and promotion rules (SIEE) — not verified in search; from prior knowledge.
- **Causal evaluation (dropout).** Ferreira Sequeda (2020, Maastricht ROA/GSBE working paper), "The effect of grade retention on secondary school dropout" — [core.ac.uk PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/376908398.pdf); [Maastricht CRIS](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/the-effect-of-grade-retention-on-secondary-school-dropout-evidenc) (search-only).
  - The 2010 reform removed the 5% cap on retention.
  - Design: school-level difference-in-differences on administrative data, with treatment intensity measured by each school's change in retention. Pre-trends are reported to be parallel.
  - Results: retention raises same-year dropout among retained students. The effect is strongest at grades 9 and 11. There is little or no spillover onto peers' dropout.
  - The search results do not name Decreto 1290 explicitly; the link to that decree is my inference from the timing.
- **Companion paper (test scores).** Ferreira, Golsteyn & Parra-Cely, IZA DP 11604 (2018), uses the same reform: retained students improve in language but not math — same search, [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:unm:umagsb:2020037) (search-only).
- **Neither paper measures NINI status or labor outcomes.**

### Inferences
- **Free schooling 2012 (promise: MEDIUM).**
  - Possible variation: (i) pre/post by grade, comparing 10–11 with grades already free before 2012; (ii) intensity by the municipality's pre-2012 fee level or pre-existing local gratuidad, since some entidades territoriales such as Bogotá were already free for poor students; (iii) cohort exposure by age in 2012.
  - Weaknesses: a single national date, no clean cutoff, and fees were probably small for many poor students.
  - The Bogotá 2004 Sisbén-1 RD is a cleaner local design, but it is old and the GEIH sample for Bogotá youth near a Sisbén cutoff would be thin. Administrative data (SIMAT linked to PILA) would be needed.
- **Automatic promotion repeal 2010 (promise: HIGH).**
  - It directly shocked progression at grades 9–11, the exact age window where the NINI rate jumps.
  - The variation has already been operationalized: school-level change in retention.
  - Extending the Ferreira Sequeda design from "dropout" to "dropout into NINI vs. into work" would need linking SIMAT/C600 to PILA (formal work), or a cohort-by-municipality DiD on GEIH NINI rates.
  - A second, opposite shock exists in 2002 (introduction of the 95% rule), allowing a "reversal" design.
- **Jornada Única (promise: MEDIUM).**
  - Staggered school-level adoption from 2015/2016 suits a staggered DiD or event study using SIMAT school-by-year adoption.
  - Plausible channels to NINI: (a) incapacitation and time use, which lowers teen pregnancy (as in Chile's JEC literature — not searched here); (b) higher completion; (c) competition with adolescent part-time work.
  - Risk: adoption was endogenous to infrastructure and was concentrated in some entidades territoriales.

### Gaps
- No national impact evaluation of Decreto 4807/2011 was found. The exact pre-2012 national gratuidad instruments were not verified.
- The headline estimates of Barrera-Osorio, Linden & Urquiola (Bogotá) were not retrieved.
- No official DNP or MEN impact evaluation of Jornada Única was found. Data on adoption counts and coverage by year were not retrieved.
- The exact date of Decreto 1290 de 2009 and its year of application (2010 school year) come from the Ferreira Sequeda description and prior knowledge, not the decree text.

## Q2. Higher-education financing: ICETEX/ACCES, Ser Pilo Paga, Generación E, Matrícula Cero, Ley 2307 de 2023

### Takeaway
Ser Pilo Paga (2014–2018) and ACCES/ICETEX offer **sharp, published regression-discontinuity designs** with large enrollment effects. Generación E and gratuidad (2019–2023) offer Sisbén and estrato thresholds and a national rollout for public institutions (IES), but have **no causal evaluations found**. None of the studies measures NINI status. The policies mostly move youth at ages 17–19, right after secondary completion, where the NINI rate peaks.

### Cited Findings

**2a. ICETEX / ACCES (credit for low-income students)**
- Melguizo, Sánchez Torres & Velasco (2016, *World Development* 80: 61–77) — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:wdevel:v:80:y:2016:i:c:p:61-77); [EconBiz](https://www.econbiz.de/Record/credit-for-low-income-students-and-access-to-and-academic-performance-in-higher-education-in-colombia-a-regression-discontinuity-approach/10011603377) (search-only).
  - Design: **regression discontinuity**.
  - Results: credit raises enrollment, with the largest and clearly positive effects for the poorest applicants. Effects on dropout are "less compelling" but go in the expected direction.
- Not verified: ACCES launched around 2003 with World Bank support. The search did not confirm the start year.

**2b. Ser Pilo Paga (SPP)**
- **Eligibility thresholds and timing.**
  - First call 2014: Saber 11 score of 310 or above — [Las2orillas](https://www.las2orillas.co/?p=176172); [Uniandes SPP reglamento](https://decanaturadeestudiantes.uniandes.edu.co:443/sites/default/files/Reglamento_Ser_Pilo_Paga_1_0.pdf) (both search-only).
  - The cutoff rose to 342 in the 2016 call (third call) — [El Tiempo archive](https://www.eltiempo.com/archivo/documento/CMS-16731815) (search-only).
  - It was 348 in the 2017 (fourth) call, for the 27 Aug 2017 exam, plus a location-specific Sisbén cutoff — [Icesi SPP4 guide](https://icesi.edu.co/images/unicesi/docs/ser-pilo-4-guia.PDF) (search-only).
- **Scale.** The MEN reported the program reaching 40,000 beneficiaries (around the fourth call; year not confirmed from the snippet) — [MEN comunicado](https://www.mineducacion.gov.co/portal/salaprensa/Comunicados/363347:Ser-pilo-paga-llega-a-los-40-000-beneficiarios) (search-only).
- **Causal evaluation.** Londoño-Vélez, Rodríguez & Sánchez (2020), "Upstream and downstream impacts of college merit-based financial aid for low-income students: Ser Pilo Paga in Colombia," *AEJ: Economic Policy* 12(2): 193–227 — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:aea:aejpol:v:12:y:2020:i:2:p:193-227); [eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/content/qt1b46508m/qt1b46508m.pdf?t=pwztki) (search-only).
  - Design: two-cutoff RD on Saber 11 (merit) and Sisbén (need).
  - Immediate enrollment rises by 56.5–86.5% depending on the complier population, driven by high-quality private colleges. (This figure is from the dissertation abstract per the snippet; check the published article.)
  - A difference-in-differences analysis suggests spillovers to ineligible students through expanded supply.
  - The study also reports "upstream" effects on Saber 11 effort.
- **Other work.**
  - Penney et al., multi-score fuzzy RD: limited test-score effects, but significant differences in enrollment, persistence and test-taking — [CLEF WP 064 (2023)](https://clef.uwaterloo.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/CLEF-064-2023.pdf) (search-only).
  - A paper on SPP and rural youth access — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/col/000089/021545.html) (search-only).
  - "Intended and Unintended Impacts…" (Uniandes CEDE) — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/col/000089/015466.html) (search-only).
- Not verified: Sisbén cutoffs of 57.21 (14 main cities), 56.32 (other urban) and 40.75 (rural). These are from my prior knowledge of the AEJ paper; the search did not confirm them.

**2c. Generación E (from late 2018/2019)**
- **Launch and scale.** The MEN launched it as the replacement for SPP, targeting 336,000 students over the presidential term — [MEN press release](https://www.mineducacion.gov.co/portal/salaprensa/Noticias/377779:Gobierno-lanzo-Generacion-E-el-nuevo-programa-de-educacion-superior-que-beneficiara-a-336-mil-estudiantes-de-todo-el-pais-durante-el-cuatrienio); [El Universal, Oct 2018](https://www.eluniversal.com.co/educacion/2018/10/23/cuatro-cosas-que-tiene-que-saber-de-la-generacion-e-el-nuevo-ser-pilo-paga/) (search-only).
- **Equidad component (2019).**
  - Age 14–28, Sisbén score of 32 or below, admitted to a public IES, no prior university degree.
  - No Saber 11 threshold.
  - Sources: [MEN balance 2019](https://www.mineducacion.gov.co/1780/articles-402106_pdf.pdf); [MEN article 379028](https://www.mineducacion.gov.co/1759/w3-article-379028.html) (search-only).
- **Excelencia component.** Saber 11 cutoff reported as 359 in one source and 350 in another; the Sisbén cutoff is reported as 57 in one press source — **conflicting, not verified** — [Aprendemas guide](https://www.aprendemas.com/co/blog/mundo-educativo/todo-lo-que-debes-saber-sobre-el-programa-generacion-e-del-gobierno-colombiano-84253); [Semana](https://www.semana.com/trabajo-y-educacion/articulo/como-puedo-ser-parte-del-programa-generacion-e-en-este-gobierno/79149/) (search-only).
- **Financing.** CONPES 4013 (2020) covers financing for the program — [normograma copy](https://normograma.com/keralty/compilacion/docs/CONPES_DNP_4013_2020.htm) (search-only).
- **Evaluation.** Only a qualitative documentary review on retention was found — [CLABES/UTP](https://revistas.utp.ac.pa/index.php/clabes/article/download/3444/4129) (search-only). **No causal evaluation found.**

**2d. Matrícula Cero (2020–2021, temporary) and Ley 2307 de 2023 (permanent gratuidad)**
- **Matrícula Cero 2021.** In 2021, free tuition at public universities was announced as temporary, applying to the next semester. Generación E beneficiaries already had their studies paid — [La FM](https://www.lafm.com.co/educacion/matricula-gratuita-en-universidades-publicas-aplica-solo-para-el-proximo-semestre) (search-only).
  - Institutions issued their own implementing acts in 2021, e.g. [UTP Acuerdo 31 de 2021](https://media2.utp.edu.co/oficinas/10/acuerdo-31-matricula-cero-pregrado-utp-2021-ii_22-07-21_18-31-57.pdf); [Unicórdoba comunicado](https://www.unicordoba.edu.co/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/COMUNICADO-021-MATRICULA-CERO-1-1.pdf) (search-only).
  - Not verified: the 2020 local/departmental precursors and the national 2021-II decree number.
- **Ley 2307 de 2023.** It establishes gratuidad in undergraduate programs at public IES. President Petro signed it on 31 July 2023. Coverage is mainly youth from estratos 1, 2 and 3 — [El Tiempo](https://www.eltiempo.com/vida/educacion/el-presidente-petro-sanciono-la-ley-de-matricula-cero-en-educacion-superior-791963); [DMS Jurídica text](https://www.dmsjuridica.com/buscador_20179478954/legislacion/leyes/2023/08/23/ley-2307-de-2023/?hilite=2388&pdf=2633) (search-only).
  - The regulatory decree and the current eligibility rule (estrato vs. Sisbén) were **not verified**.
- **Evaluation.** No causal evaluation of Matrícula Cero or Ley 2307 was found.

### Inferences
- **SPP (promise: HIGH for a NINI extension of an existing RD).**
  - The two-dimensional RD (Saber 11 × Sisbén) is well documented and replicable: a methods tutorial reports 23,132 observations above the cutoff in the replication data — [arXiv 2301.08958](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08958) (search-only).
  - Linking ICFES and SPADIES to PILA would let one estimate whether eligibility moves youth out of "neither" status at ages 17–20 (not only from work into study).
  - Limitation: compliers are high-achievers, who are not the typical NINI.
- **Generación E Equidad (promise: MEDIUM–HIGH).** The Sisbén cutoff of 32 with no score threshold targets exactly the low-income population with high NINI rates. An RD at Sisbén 32, combined with the Sisbén III-to-IV transition, would need administrative data.
- **Ley 2307 / Matrícula Cero (promise: MEDIUM).**
  - Variation could come from (i) the department or municipality's pre-policy local gratuidad (some governments started in 2020, which creates staggered timing); (ii) estrato-based eligibility (an estrato 3 vs. 4 boundary); (iii) distance to a public IES.
  - Descriptive post-2023 changes in NINI rates for 17–20-year-olds by estrato are worth checking in GEIH.
- **ACCES (promise: MEDIUM).** There is an RD, but it is on credit applicants, a selected population.

### Gaps
- ACCES start year and score thresholds were not verified.
- SPP Sisbén cutoffs by area and the total beneficiary count (around 40,000) for 2014–2018 were only partially verified.
- Generación E Excelencia thresholds conflict across sources. Annual counts were not retrieved.
- The regulatory decree of Ley 2307 de 2023 and its targeting rule were not verified. Pre-2023 local Matrícula Cero timing by territory was not compiled.
- No study measures NINI/NEET as an outcome of any higher-education aid policy.

## Q3. Youth labor-demand incentives and military-service rules: Ley 1429/2010, Ley 1780/2016, Ley 2155/2021, Ley 48/1993, Ley 1738/2014, Ley 1861/2017

### Takeaway
There is a sequence of **age-targeted hiring incentives** with sharp age bounds (under 28; 18–28). Each is conditioned on net job creation:
- Ley 1429/2010 (payroll-contribution relief);
- Ley 1780/2016 art. 7 (no family-compensation-fund (caja) contributions for the first year for new hires aged 18–28);
- Ley 2155/2021 art. 24 (a 25% minimum-wage subsidy per additional worker aged 18–28, through about August 2023).

These create age-at-hire discontinuities, at 18 and especially at 28/29, usable in PILA. Separately, the **libreta militar** rules for men were relaxed step by step: Ley 1738/2014 (no longer required for an undergraduate degree), Ley 1780/2016 art. 20 (18 months to define military status after hiring), and Ley 1861/2017 art. 42. This gives a male-specific shock at 18. **No evaluation measures NINI/NEET status.**

### Cited Findings

**3a. Ley 1429 de 2010 ("Ley de formalización y primer empleo")**
- **Content.** Partial, gradual reductions in contributions to SENA, cajas de compensación and ICBF to promote formal job creation. Targeted groups include youth, female heads of household, women over 40, reintegrated ex-combatants, and workers earning under 2 minimum wages — [Externado boletín 13](https://beta.uexternado.edu.co/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/boletin13-nuevo.pdf); [Accounter, letra menuda](https://accounter.co/noticias/actualidad/titulares/conozca-la-letra-menuda-de-la-qley-de-primer-empleoq-y-saquele-mayor-provecho-a-la-iniciativa) (search-only).
  - Not verified: the specific youth provision (as I recall, art. 9: an income-tax deduction for parafiscales paid on new hires under 28). The search snippets did not quote the age bound.
- **Constitutionality.** Art. 5 (progressive payment of parafiscales) was upheld in Sentencia C-629/11 — [Accounter, C-629/11](https://accounter.co/normatividad/sentencias/progresividad-en-el-pago-de-los-parafiscales-y-otras-contribuciones-de-nomina-ley-1429-de-2010) (search-only).
- **Evaluations (all on formalization, none on youth or NINI).**
  - Farné, Baquero & Álvarez (Externado, 2011): short-run impact on informality was smaller than expected — [Externado boletín 13](https://beta.uexternado.edu.co/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/boletin13-nuevo.pdf) (search-only).
  - Universidad Nacional thesis, similar conclusion — [UNAL repository](https://repositorio.unal.edu.co/handle/unal/56592) (search-only).
  - Confecámaras: 290,703 firms benefited by December 2011 (same Accounter/Externado cluster).
  - There is also a CEDE paper — [IDEAS col/000194/015938](https://ideas.repec.org/p/col/000194/015938.html) (search-only; content not seen).

**3b. Ley 1780 de 2016 ("Ley Pro Joven" / empleo y emprendimiento juvenil)**
- **Art. 7.** Employers hiring new staff aged 18–28 at contract start do not pay caja de compensación contributions for those workers during the first year, provided total headcount increases. Art. 3 exempts "young small firms" from commercial registration fees — [Función Pública, Ley 1780](https://www.funcionpublica.gov.co/eva/gestornormativo/norma_pdf.php?i=69573); [Colombia Compra Eficiente text](https://relatoria.colombiacompra.gov.co/normativa/ley-1780-de-2016/?print=pdf) (search-only).
- **Art. 20.** Temporary employment for up to 18 months, during which beneficiaries must define their military status. It was later subrogated by art. 42 of Ley 1861 de 2017; the Constitutional Court (C-277/19) did not rule on the merits for that reason — [Ámbito Jurídico, Sentencia C-277/19](https://www.ambitojuridico.com/sites/default/files/BancoMedios/Archivos/sent-c-277-19.pdf) (search-only).
- Not verified: Ley 1780 also created "Estado Joven" (public-sector internships) and the public-employment rule on counting study as experience. The snippets did not confirm either.
- **Evaluations.** None found.

**3c. Ley 2155 de 2021 (Inversión Social), art. 24 — youth hiring subsidy**
- **Content.** A state contribution equal to **25% of one SMMLV per additional worker aged 18–28**, for employers who increase youth headcount.
- **Regulation.** Decreto 1399 de 2021 allows up to 12 monthly payments.
- **Duration.** It was valid until about August 2023. The law asked the government to decide on an extension, only for ages 18–28, after evaluating results.
- One source mentions a later extension allowing up to 24 payments. **Status after 2023 is conflicting / not verified.**
- Sources: [Decreto 1399 de 2021 (UGPP)](https://paef.ugpp.gov.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/DECRETO_1399_2021.pdf); [Forvis Mazars summary](https://www.forvismazars.com/co/es/acerca-de-nosotros/noticias-publicaciones-y-media/nuestras-publicaciones/actualidad-juridica-y-tributaria/2021/incentivo-a-la-generacion-de-nuevos-empleos); [Actualícese](https://actualicese.com/incentivo-para-la-creacion-de-nuevos-empleos-con-la-ley-2155-de-2021/); [Asuntos Legales](https://www.asuntoslegales.com.co/consultorio/los-beneficios-por-generacion-de-empleo-con-la-ley-2155-de-2021-3250260) (all search-only).
- **Evaluation.** No official or academic evaluation was found.

**3d. Military service / libreta militar (men)**
- **Ley 48 de 1993.** Not verified in this search session (from prior knowledge): it made military service compulsory for men from age 18 and required the libreta for public employment and degrees.
- **Ley 1738 de 2014, art. 2.** Expressly prohibits universities from requiring the libreta militar to award an undergraduate degree. The MEN states this does not automatically extend to graduate degrees — [MEN concepto](https://www.mineducacion.gov.co/1780/articles-354049_archivo_pdf_consulta_.pdf) (search-only).
- **Ley 1861 de 2017, art. 42.**
  - Proof of military status is required for public posts, private-sector employment and state service contracts.
  - However, public or private entities may not demand that the libreta be presented as a precondition for hiring.
  - People who must define their status have 18 months from hiring to do so.
  - In 2021, the Ministerio del Trabajo issued a circular urging employers not to request the libreta, especially for youth aged 18–28 entering their first job.
  - Sources: [Función Pública, Ley 1861](https://www.funcionpublica.gov.co/eva/gestornormativo/norma_pdf.php?i=194711); [Semana 2021](https://www.semana.com/economia/empresas/articulo/empleo-joven-libreta-militar-no-podra-exigirse-al-contratar-a-jovenes/202155/); [INCP 2021](https://incp.org.co/publicaciones/infoincp-publicaciones/informacion-para-empresas/laboral/2021/07/libreta-militar-no-es-un-requisito-para-contratar-jovenes/) (search-only).
  - A 2024 bill (PL 033 de 2024) would remove the requirement entirely. Its approval was not confirmed — [Gaceta del Congreso 1064/2024](https://sidn.ramajudicial.gov.co/SIDN/PUBLICACIONES%20PERIODICAS/TEXTO%20COMPLETO%20Y%20TABLAS%20DE%20CONTENIDO/GACETA%20DEL%20CONGRESO/GACETA%20DEL%20CONGRESO%202024/GC%201064%20DE%202024.PDF) (search-only).
- **Advocacy figure.** A bill cites 565,000 men aged 18–28 blocked from formal employment by the libreta (advocacy number, not an estimate) — [Las2orillas](https://www.las2orillas.co/?p=324430) (search-only); see also [CEJ 2022](https://cej.org.co/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/HACIA-UNA-ELIMINACI%C3%93N-DEL-SERVICIO-MILITAR-OBLIGATORIO-COMO-REQUISITO-DE-ACCESO-AL-EMPLEO-EN-COLOMBIA.pdf) (search-only).
- **Causal evidence.** One EAFIT master's thesis was found: Atis Ortega (advisor Brian Feld), "Effects of the abolition of military service on the youth labor market: evidence from Colombia" — [EAFIT repository](https://repository.eafit.edu.co/items/24ed7f33-e149-474b-be7c-7ea01700a8d4/full) (search-only; findings not seen).

### Inferences
- **Hiring incentives (promise: MEDIUM).**
  - Age-at-hire discontinuities: eligibility at 18 and loss at 28/29 for Ley 1780 art. 7 and Ley 2155. Time windows: 2016 onward for Ley 1780, and roughly Aug 2021–Aug 2023 for Ley 2155. These could be studied in PILA with RD-in-age, DiD across ages 26–28 vs. 29–31, or bunching of hires below 29.
  - Effects on NINI are likely small: incentives act on formal hiring at the margin, take-up appears low, and most NINIs are inactive women.
  - The 2021–2023 subsidy is the cleanest (dated start and end). It overlaps the post-COVID recovery, so age-group comparisons are essential.
- **Libreta militar (promise: MEDIUM–HIGH for men).**
  - Men-only rules give a natural control group: women of the same age.
  - Changes in 2014 (degree), 2016/2017 (hiring) and 2021 (circular) allow triple differences: men vs. women × age 18–24 vs. older × before/after.
  - Mechanisms: an employment barrier for male school leavers aged 18–24, and pressure to stay enrolled in order to postpone service.
  - Caveat: the NINI rate is concentrated among women, so this is a margin specific to male NINIs.

### Gaps
- The exact youth article of Ley 1429 (age bound, instrument) was not verified.
- The Estado Joven start and scale were not verified.
- Ley 2155 take-up counts and post-August-2023 status were not verified. No evaluation was found.
- Ley 48/1993 details (ages, exemptions, bachiller rules) were not verified here. Findings of the EAFIT thesis were not seen. No published causal paper on the libreta and youth outcomes was found.

## Q4. Adolescent work rules and minimum wage

### Takeaway
The **Código de Infancia y Adolescencia (Ley 1098 de 2006)** sets a minimum working age of 15. Work at 15–17 requires a written permit from a labor inspector, plus hour limits. This creates an age-15 and age-18 legal structure for formal adolescent work, though most adolescent work is informal. The **large minimum-wage increases** (16% for 2023, confirmed) are a national shock. Banco de la República work finds minimum-wage effects on informality are largest for youth, the less educated and women. No study links minimum wages to NINI rates in Colombia.

### Cited Findings
- **Ley 1098 de 2006 (adolescent work).**
  - Art. 35 sets a minimum working age of 15. Adolescents aged 15–17 need written authorization from the labor inspector (or the local territorial authority), requested by their parents (art. 113). Art. 114 governs hours.
  - A Ministry of Health guide states a maximum of 6 h/day and 30 h/week at ages 15–16, and 8 h/day at 17 (truncated; verify art. 114).
  - Art. 117 prohibits hazardous work under 18.
  - Under-15s may work only in exceptional artistic, cultural, recreational or sports activities.
  - The national authorization form is in Resolución 2438 de 2010.
  - Sources: [Gerencie](https://www.gerencie.com/menores-de-edad-requieren-autorizacion-para-trabajar.html); [MinSalud flow guide](https://minsalud.gov.co/Documentacin%20trmites%20y%20servicios/MenorTrabajador/Menores_flujo_escrito.pdf); [ICBF, Res. 2438/2010](https://www.icbf.gov.co/cargues/avance/compilacion/docs/resolucion_minproteccion_2438_2010.htm) (all search-only).
  - The hazardous-activities list was updated by Resolución 1796 de 2018 — [Rama Judicial](https://www.ramajudicial.gov.co/documents/573203/15248969/RESOLUCI%C3%93N+1796+DE+2018_actividades+nocivas+NNA.pdf/54a53ae6-1f90-428c-80a3-da31b90ec1b7) (search-only).
- **Minimum wage 2023.** Government, unions and employers agreed a 16% increase for 2023 (COP 160,000). Analysts flagged employment, not inflation, as the main risk — [Bloomberg Línea, Dec 2022](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/2022/12/16/salario-minimo-2023-empleo-en-colombia-quedara-contra-las-cuerdas-tras-alza/) (search-only).
  - Not verified: the 2024 (about 12%), 2025 (about 9.5%) and 2026 increases.
- **Minimum wage and youth: Banco de la República evidence.**
  - Borrador 1104 (2020): minimum-wage effects on informality are largest for less-educated workers, youth and women. A 1 pp rise in the minimum-wage-to-p70 ratio raises the probability of informal work by 0.16–0.21 pp — [Banrep Borrador 1104](https://www.banrep.gov.co/es/borrador-1104) (search-only).
  - ESPE paper on the macro effects of the minimum wage: a 1% real increase raises job destruction by 0.44 pp and lowers job creation by 0.56 pp. Employment falls about 1.85% in the youngest establishments vs. 0.84% in older ones — [Banrep ESPE](https://banrep.gov.co/es/publicaciones-investigaciones/espe/efectos-macroecon%C3%B3micos-salario-m%C3%ADnimo-Colombia) (search-only).
  - *Cuadernos de Economía* (UNAL): the minimum-wage gap creates substitution between young and older cohorts — [UNAL](https://revistas.unal.edu.co/index.php/ceconomia/article/view/45342) (search-only).
- **No Colombian study of the 2023–2025 increases on youth employment or NINI status was found.**

### Inferences
- **Ley 1098 (promise: LOW–MEDIUM).** The 2006 code tightened the formal rules for working at 15–17 (permits, hours). An age-15 discontinuity in work could be tested with GEIH or ECV. Enforcement is weak and most adolescent work is informal and family-based, so the effect on NINI status is probably small. Its main relevance is that it explains why formal labor-market entry is effectively postponed to 18. That is consistent with — but not proof of — part of the 16–18 jump.
- **Minimum wage (promise: MEDIUM).**
  - Use a "bite" design: variation across departments or cities in the minimum wage relative to youth median wages (Kaitz index). Interact the 2023 shock with bite × youth vs. adults in GEIH monthly data.
  - It is a single national date, so identification relies on bite heterogeneity.
  - It is relevant to the countercyclical NINI pattern and low-income youth.

### Gaps
- The exact text of art. 114 (hours) was not verified. No evaluation of the 2006 adolescent-work regime was found.
- No Banco de la República or academic paper on the 2023–2026 increases and youth was found. The size of the 2024–2026 increases was not verified in this session.

## Q5. Youth transfers: Jóvenes en Paz (2023) and Sacúdete (Ley 2231 de 2022)

### Takeaway
**Jóvenes en Paz** is a conditional transfer of up to COP 1,000,000/month for youth aged 14–28 who are poor or vulnerable in the Registro Social de Hogares, in prioritized (conflict and violence) territories, with a target of about 100,000 beneficiaries. Its geographic and RSH targeting provide variation, but no evaluation was found. **Sacúdete** is a framework law (strategy and governance), not a transfer, with little exploitable variation.

### Cited Findings
- **Jóvenes en Paz: legal basis and design.**
  - Created by art. 348 of Ley 2294 de 2023 (PND 2022–2026) and regulated by Decreto 1649 de 2023.
  - Ages 14–28; a conditional cash transfer of up to COP 1 million per month.
  - Beneficiaries must be in poverty, extreme poverty or monetary vulnerability, identified through the Registro Social de Hogares. The program is administered by the Ministerio de Igualdad y Equidad.
  - Target: about 100,000 youth in conflict-affected areas.
  - Sources: [El Tiempo](https://www.eltiempo.com/politica/gobierno/jovenes-en-paz-entregara-1-millon-mensuales-estos-son-los-requisitos-y-beneficiarios-837877); [El Espectador](https://www.elespectador.com/politica/requisitos-y-condiciones-para-aplicar-a-jovenes-en-paz-que-entregara-1-millon-mensual-noticias-hoy/); [DMS Jurídica, Decreto 1649](https://www.dmsjuridica.com/buscador_20179478954/legislacion/decretos/2023/11/22/decreto-1649-de-2023/) (search-only).
  - A draft DNP methodology for the program exists — [Ámbito Jurídico PDF](https://ambitojuridico.com/sites/default/files/2023-11/PROYECTONORMA-DNP-2023%28Metodologia-Jovenes-en-%20Paz%29.pdf) (search-only).
- **Legal challenges and operation.**
  - The Consejo de Estado admitted nullity suits against parts of the decree — [Consejo de Estado auto](https://gov.consejodeestado.gov.co/wp-content/uploads/2024/Auto%20J%C3%B3venes%20en%20Paz.pdf) (search-only).
  - In 2024, a tribunal refused to suspend payments — [Infobae, Sep 2024](https://www.infobae.com/colombia/2024/09/20/los-beneficiarios-del-programa-jovenes-en-paz-no-perderan-sus-pagos-tribunal-nego-propuesta-de-congresistas-que-pedian-suspender-las-transferencias/) (search-only).
  - Prosperidad Social clarified the age limits for payments in Feb 2025 — [Infobae, Feb 2025](https://www.infobae.com/colombia/2025/02/19/jovenes-en-paz-prosperidad-social-aclaro-hasta-que-edad-se-podran-recibir-los-pagos-del-gobierno-nacional/) (search-only).
  - A new payment round for more than 5,000 people was reported in June 2026 — [Infobae, Jun 2026](https://www.infobae.com/colombia/2026/06/01/el-gobierno-petro-iniciara-una-nueva-entrega-del-programa-jovenes-en-paz-mas-de-5000-personas-recibiran-las-tranferencias/) (search-only).
  - Media flagged irregular payments (for example, beneficiaries recorded as born in 1899) — [Pulzo](https://www.pulzo.com/nacion/exigen-auditoria-programa-jovenes-paz-pagos-nacidos-1899-PP3874042A) (search-only).
- **Sacúdete (Ley 2231 de 2022).**
  - Signed on 1 July 2022, it makes "Sacúdete" a state policy for youth aged 14–28.
  - Three phases (Inspírate, Enfócate, Transfórmate) focused on "3E": education, employment and entrepreneurship.
  - It creates an intersectoral commission chaired by Colombia Joven and requires nationwide implementation within at least one year.
  - Sources: [Función Pública, Ley 2231](https://www.funcionpublica.gov.co/eva/gestornormativo/norma.php?i=189389); [URosario PDF of the law](https://induccion.urosario.edu.co/sites/default/files/2022-10/ley_2231_de_01_de_julio_de_2022.pdf) (search-only).
  - No evaluation found.

### Inferences
- **Jóvenes en Paz (promise: MEDIUM, conditional on data access).**
  - Variation could come from (i) the list of prioritized municipalities, enabling a municipality-level DiD from 2024 onward; (ii) the RSH classification threshold (poor or vulnerable vs. not), enabling an RD if the microdata are obtainable; (iii) the age bounds 14 and 28.
  - Conditions reportedly include education/training activities, which would plausibly reduce NINI status mechanically during participation.
  - Small scale relative to the youth population, the short window (2024–2026), and implementation irregularities limit power.
- **Sacúdete (promise: LOW).** It is a governance framework with no transfer, dated cutoff, or measurable intensity found.

### Gaps
- Actual beneficiary counts by year and municipality, the list of prioritized municipalities, and the specific conditionalities were not retrieved.
- No impact evaluation (DNP or academic) of Jóvenes en Paz was found.
- Sacúdete implementation data were not found.

## Q6. Care constraints and family formation: De Cero a Siempre/CDI expansion (Ley 1804 de 2016), teen-pregnancy school policy, child-marriage ban (Ley 2447 de 2025)

### Takeaway
The **2011–2013 rapid expansion of comprehensive early-childhood care slots (De Cero a Siempre, CDIs)**, later made state policy by Ley 1804 de 2016, is the most promising care-side shock for young mothers. It is already exploited quasi-experimentally (variation in slots across municipalities and cohorts) for child outcomes. Maternal-labor evidence is thin (a master's thesis plus a simulation). **The child-marriage ban is Ley 2447 de 2025** (Diario Oficial, 13 February 2025), too recent for outcome data, but it is an age-18 rule shock. Teen-pregnancy school protection comes mainly from Constitutional Court case law (1990s–2000s) rather than a dated statute.

### Cited Findings
- **De Cero a Siempre / CDI.**
  - The ICBF/ELCA impact evaluation (Uniandes, 2017) uses ELCA 2010, 2013 and 2016. It exploits **the rapid expansion of comprehensive-care slots between 2011 and 2013**, with gradual variation across municipalities and cohorts — [ICBF, evaluación de impacto De Cero a Siempre (ELCA Uniandes 2017)](https://www.icbf.gov.co/system/files/evaluacion_de_impacto_de_cero_a_siempre_-_elca_uniandes_2017_1.pdf); [ELCA Boletín 12](https://datoscede.uniandes.edu.co/wp-content/uploads/Boletin-ELCA12.pdf) (search-only).
  - Maternal labor: Granda (2023, Universidad del Rosario master's thesis) finds the program raised women's labor-market participation and shifted some women from informal to formal jobs — [URosario repository](https://repository.urosario.edu.co/bitstreams/c5b39702-54af-4592-9924-7a4593e66572/download); [URosario Nova et Vetera](https://urosario.edu.co/periodico-nova-et-vetera/economia/puede-una-politica-de-cuidado-contribuir-a-la-autonomia-economica-de-las-mujeres) (search-only). The identification strategy was not verified.
  - Cicowiez et al. (2022) is a simulation, not causal, of expanded childcare transfers — [URosario research hub](https://research-hub.urosario.edu.co/display/n_387dfe8a-0c0d-474f-9f92-e1f325cc4476) (search-only).
  - Not verified: Ley 1804 de 2016 date (as I recall, 2 August 2016) and slot counts by year.
- **Teen pregnancy and schooling.**
  - Constitutional Court case law holds that a pregnant student cannot be removed from school, and that a school's convivencia manual cannot obstruct her studies: T-667/97, T-656/98, T-272/01 (and T-393/09, content unconfirmed) — [Corte Constitucional T-667/97](https://www.corteconstitucional.gov.co/relatoria/1997/T-667-97.htm); [DMS Jurídica T-656/98](https://www.dmsjuridica.com/buscador_20179478954/corteconstitucionalcronologico202454587/t-656-98/?pdf=4120); [DMS Jurídica T-272/01](https://www.dmsjuridica.com/buscador_20179478954/corteconstitucionalcronologico202454587/t-272-01/?pdf=7491) (search-only).
  - Ley 1620 de 2013 (Ley de Convivencia Escolar, regulated by Decreto 1965 de 2013) creates the Sistema Nacional de Convivencia Escolar, including sexual and reproductive rights education. The search did not show an explicit no-expulsion-for-pregnancy clause — [MEN, Ley de Convivencia Escolar](https://www.mineducacion.gov.co/portal//322486:Ley-de-Convivencia-Escolar) (search-only).
  - CONPES Social 147 de 2012 sets the national strategy and care pathways for preventing adolescent pregnancy — [UNFPA PDF](https://colombia.unfpa.org/sites/default/files/pub-pdf/28-CONPES%20SOCIAL%20147-2012%20Rutas%20de%20atencio%CC%81n%20para%20la%20prevencio%CC%81n%20del%20embarazo%20en%20la%20adolescencia.%20Atencio%CC%81n%20integral%20e.pdf) (search-only).
  - A 2011 Valle del Cauca study found 24.6% of pregnant schoolgirls dropped out, about 8 times the expected rate — [Colombia Médica 2011 (biblat)](https://biblat.unam.mx/hevila/Colombiamedica/2011/no3/5.pdf) (search-only).
- **Child-marriage ban: Ley 2447 de 2025.**
  - Published in the Diario Oficial on **13 February 2025** (No. 53.029).
  - It prohibits marriage and de facto unions (uniones maritales de hecho) where either party is under 18, nationwide. It amends art. 116 of the Civil Code, which previously let parents authorize marriage of minors.
  - The remedy is nullity (art. 7). It creates a national "Proyectos de Vida" program led by ICBF.
  - Sources: [Función Pública, Ley 2447](https://www.funcionpublica.gov.co/eva/gestornormativo/norma.php?i=258236); [Rama Judicial PDF](https://sidn.ramajudicial.gov.co/SIDN/NORMATIVA/TEXTOS_COMPLETOS/7_LEYES/LEYES%202025/Ley%202447%20de%202025.pdf); [La Silla Vacía fact-check](https://www.lasillavacia.com/detector-de-mentiras/enganoso/ley-contra-matrimonio-infantil-fue-sancionada-en-gobierno-petro-no-de-abelardo/) (search-only).
  - An ICBF implementation plan is reported — [ICBF](https://www.icbf.gov.co/gobierno-nacional-pone-en-marcha-plan-historico-para-erradicar-uniones-tempranas-y-matrimonios) (search-only).

### Inferences
- **CDI / De Cero a Siempre expansion (promise: HIGH for young mothers).**
  - Municipality-by-year slot expansion (2011–2013, and later) is a continuous-treatment DiD. Possible outcome: NINI status of women aged 18–28 with children aged 0–5, versus women without young children (triple difference).
  - It directly targets the care constraint behind female NINIs.
  - Needs ICBF slot data (Cuéntame, or the ICBF administrative "cupos" by municipality) merged to GEIH municipal or department identifiers.
- **Child-marriage ban 2025 (promise: LOW for now).** Too recent, and formal child marriage was rare relative to informal unions. Possible future design: an age-at-ban cohort DiD on early unions and school continuation for girls aged 14–17.
- **Teen-pregnancy school protection (promise: LOW).** Case law is diffuse and has no dated rollout. CONPES 147 (2012) piloted municipalities could provide variation if the pilot list is available (not verified).

### Gaps
- No rigorous published causal estimate of CDI expansion on maternal or young-women employment or NINI status was found. Granda's method was not verified.
- The Ley 1804/2016 date and slot series were not verified.
- The CONPES 147 pilot municipalities were not retrieved.
- Penalties under Ley 2447 and early-union prevalence data were not retrieved.
