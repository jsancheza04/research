## Puzzle 1 — Why do cesarean rates differ more than tenfold across departments, and why did cesareans overtake vaginal births in 2023?

### Takeaway
Cesareans rose from 25.7% (1998) to about 45–47%, and in 2023 (preliminary data) they became the most common mode of birth. Department rates range from about 5–9% (Vaupés) to more than 70% (Atlántico, Sucre, Córdoba, San Andrés). A multilevel study finds that department explains about 15% of the variance and that maternal characteristics do not explain the department effect. The private–public difference (PR 1.57) is documented, but no causal work isolates the mechanism (payment rules, insurer, physician supply, malpractice, demand), and the Caribbean "excess" is unexplained.

### Cited Findings
**FACTS: levels and trend**
- National cesarean share: 25.7% in 1998, 46.4% in 2015, 44.6% in 2020 (DANE birth records 1998–2020). The authors say "further methodologies are needed to tell whether the decrease is real" — [Evolution of cesarean sections in Colombia… legal standing (PubMed 36920899)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36920899/); same paper on [SciELO, Rev Colomb Obstet Ginecol 2023](http://www.scielo.org.co/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S0034-74342023000100015&lng=en&nrm=iso) and [PMC10057327](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10057327/)
- Public hospitals went from 26.2% (1998) to 42.9% (2014); private providers from 45.0% to 57.7% (2013). Prevalence ratio private vs public = 1.57 (95% CI 1.56–1.57) — [PubMed 36920899](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36920899/)
- DANE: between 2018 and 2022 spontaneous births fell from 54.6% to 51.1% of the total and cesareans rose to 47.4%. In 2023pr the cesarean became the most frequent mode of birth in the 2014–2023pr series. Putumayo had the largest rise (35.6% in 2014 to 55.7% in 2023); Bogotá 50.1% — [DANE boletín técnico Nacimientos IV trim 2023 (22 Mar 2024)](https://www.dane.gov.co/files/operaciones/EEVV/bol-EEVV-Nacimientos-IVtrim2023.pdf); [DANE comunicado IV trim 2023](https://www.dane.gov.co/files/operaciones/EEVV/cp-EEVV-IVtrim2023.pdf). *Discrepancy:* the same DANE bulletin series frames the comparison as "last five years" in one place and "last 10 years" in another (noted in the snippet).
- The rise is concentrated among mothers aged 35–44, per DANE's 2014–2023 comparison (snippet incomplete) — [DANE boletín IV trim 2023](https://www.dane.gov.co/files/operaciones/EEVV/bol-EEVV-Nacimientos-IVtrim2023.pdf)

**FACTS: geography**
- Rodríguez-López et al., *Colombia Médica* 52(3): e2044411 (2021), 2016 births, multilevel model (women nested in departments). National rate 45.5% (95% CI 45.4–45.6), range **5% to 76%** across departments. VPC = 15%. Caribbean vs Andean region OR = 3.88 (2.65–5.67). Department and region effects hold "independently of individual characteristics", and differences in maternal characteristics do not explain them — [PubMed 35431362](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35431362); [Colombia Médica](https://colombiamedica.univalle.edu.co/index.php/comedica/article/view/4411); [Redalyc full text](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/283/28370069005/)
- The same study lists possible contextual drivers: public/private nature of the institution, limited specialized staff, and "differential contracting rates" — [Redalyc](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/283/28370069005/movil/)
- MinSalud figures (2017 press): Atlántico 74.94% of live births by cesarean, followed by San Andrés, Sucre and Magdalena; Vaupés lowest at 9.40%. An earlier ministry report: Atlántico ">70%" vs Caldas "not over 30%" — [El Heraldo, 15 Oct 2017](https://www.elheraldo.co/salud/2017/10/15/atlantico-con-el-mayor-porcentaje-de-nacidos-vivos-por-cesarea-en-el-pais-412279/)
- MinSalud *Atlas de Variaciones Geográficas en Salud 2015*: Sucre has the highest standardized rate (904 per 1,000 births), Córdoba ranks 7th (826), Caldas is among the lowest. Under standardized rates Vaupés ranks 19th (568), so "lowest" depends on the measure — [MinSalud Atlas cesáreas 2015](https://www.minsalud.gov.co/sites/rid/Lists/BibliotecaDigital/RIDE/DE/PES/Resultados-generales-atlas-salud-cesareas-2015.pdf). *Discrepancy:* Vaupés is 9.4% crude (El Heraldo/MinSalud 2017) but 568/1,000 standardized (Atlas). The two cannot both describe the same population and year, so the Atlas standardization or its base should be checked.
- DANE 2021pr: San Andrés, Sucre and Córdoba delivered at least 70% of births by cesarean — reported in search synthesis of [DANE EEVV 2020 definitivas / 2021pr presentation](https://www.dane.gov.co/files/investigaciones/poblacion/pre_estadisticasvitales_IIItrim_2021pr.pdf) (search-only; confirm table)
- The Caribbean was already high in ENDS 2005: San Andrés 67%, Atlántico 41.5%, Sucre 37.2%. The Caribbean rise is therefore post-2005 and fast — [ENDS 1995–2005 cesarean analysis, SciELO](http://www.scielo.org.co/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S0120-81602009000300005)
- Within one contributory insurer's network (11,954 births, 2007), the cesarean rate was 35.33%. Variation across hospitals was linked to reimbursement; cesareans pay physicians more — [Value in Health Regional Issues, "Hospital Variation in Cesarean Delivery: A Multilevel Analysis"](https://www.valuehealthregionalissues.com/article/S2212-1099(15)00053-9/fulltext)

**Press / public framing**
- Framed as an "epidemic" of abuse: [Juan Gossain, El Tiempo, "La epidemia que faltaba: el abuso de las cesáreas en Colombia"](https://www.eltiempo.com/salud/el-abuso-de-las-cesareas-en-colombia-juan-gossain-497792) (date not shown in snippet); [Consultorsalud, "'Epidemia' de Cesáreas en Colombia"](https://consultorsalud.com/epidemia-de-cesareas-en-colombia/); [La Nación, "Cesáreas, ¿un procedimiento injustificado?"](https://www.lanacion.com.co/cesareas-procedimiento-injustificado/)
- Regional framing: [El Heraldo 2017](https://www.elheraldo.co/salud/2017/10/15/atlantico-con-el-mayor-porcentaje-de-nacidos-vivos-por-cesarea-en-el-pais-412279/) (Atlántico ranked first)

**Existing explanations and evidence quality**
- Financial incentives in private institutions: proposed by the *Rev Colomb Obstet Ginecol* 2023 authors ("incentivos financieros pudieran facilitar en el sector privado…"), with a cross-sectional PR — **Descriptive** — [PubMed 36920899](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36920899/)
- Department/region "place effect", staffing, and contracting rates (Rodríguez-López 2021): multilevel decomposition only — **Descriptive** (shows the effect is not composition, but does not identify the mechanism) — [Colombia Médica](https://colombiamedica.univalle.edu.co/index.php/comedica/article/view/4411)
- Hospital reimbursement and physician payment (single insurer, 2007) — **Descriptive / Speculation** — [VHRI](https://www.valuehealthregionalissues.com/article/S2212-1099(15)00053-9/fulltext)
- No Colombia-specific economics paper (CEDE, BanRep, IDEAS) testing supplier-induced demand was found. The international causal literature (e.g., [NBER w21022 on physician incentives and C-sections](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w21022/revisions/w21022.rev0.pdf); [Johnson & Rehavi, "Physicians Treating Physicians"](https://www.mit.edu/~erinmj/files/JohnsonRehavi_2013.pdf)) has not been applied to Colombia.

### Inferences
- **Why it is a puzzle:** The rise is not explained by maternal risk, and the regional gap is huge (Caribbean OR ≈ 3.9 vs Andean) and survives adjustment for maternal characteristics. Public hospitals also converged upward, which a pure "private profit" story does not predict.
- **INFERENCE — candidate hypotheses:** (a) supply-induced demand through EPS–IPS contracts (fee-for-service vs capitated or bundled "paquete parto" payments, cesarean priced above vaginal birth); (b) obstetrician scarcity or scheduling: a planned cesarean economizes on scarce specialist time, especially if anesthesiologists and obstetricians work across several clinics, as is common in Barranquilla, Montería and Sincelejo; (c) defensive medicine and malpractice exposure; (d) a regional social norm or maternal demand (aesthetic or fear-of-pain norms, "cesárea a petición"); (e) market structure: Caribbean delivery markets dominated by private for-profit IPS contracted by subsidized EPS, with vertical integration of EPS and IPS; (f) a hospital-ownership change after the 2000s liquidation of public ESE hospitals (e.g., ISS clinics), which shifted births to private IPS.
- **INFERENCE — discriminating variation:** day-of-week and hour-of-birth (cesareans clustering on weekdays and daytime signals scheduling or supply motives); December or holiday dips; the same mother's subsequent births (movers across departments: department of residence vs department of occurrence identifies place vs person); insurer fixed effects within the same hospital (EPS contract terms); hospital ownership changes (public ESE closures or privatizations; EPS intervention or liquidation by Supersalud, e.g., Saludcoop 2015, Coomeva 2022, Nueva EPS intervention 2024) as shocks to payment terms; obstetrician entry and exit (REPS capacity registry); multiples and breech vs low-risk Robson groups 1–4 (the excess should be concentrated in low-risk groups if non-clinical).
- **INFERENCE — data:** DANE EEVV birth microdata (public) contain delivery type (*tipo de parto*), birth weight, gestational-age bins, place of delivery, department and municipality of occurrence and of residence, health regime, maternal age and education, parity and multiplicity, and (not verified) day and hour of birth. They do not contain the insurer (EPS) or hospital ID publicly. REPS (public) gives facility capacity and ownership. RIPS/SISPRO cubes (registration needed) give procedures by EPS. Restricted DANE files may include the facility code (not verified).
- **Puzzle-ness: HIGH. Research promise: HIGH** (large n, public data, natural experiments from EPS liquidations and hospital ownership changes).

### Gaps
- Exact department-level cesarean shares for 2023–2025 (could not open DANE tables).
- Whether the 2015–2020 decline continued or reversed in 2021–2025 (DANE says 47.4% in 2022 and >50% of births in 2023pr; the exact 2023 and 2024 national figures were not captured).
- No evidence found on EPS payment rules for delivery (bundled vs fee-for-service) by region.
- Whether DANE microdata include hour and weekday of birth: not verified.

## Puzzle 2 — Why did low birth weight rise to a record ~11% while poverty fell and prenatal care improved, and why is it highest in richer places (Bogotá, contributory regime)?

### Takeaway
Low birth weight (<2,500 g) rose from about 9% (2014) to 11.0% (2023pr) and 11.3% (Jan–Oct 2024pr), the highest in the DANE series. Earlier, Colombia already went against the global trend (7.7% to 8.8%, 2000–2015). The "term-LBW is falling" claim holds for 2000–2014 and 2008–2018 studies, but DANE says term LBW **rose** by 1.5 pp between 2014 and 2023, mainly after 2021. So the rise is not only preterm-driven. The gradient is unusual: Bogotá is systematically highest, and in 2005–2009 the contributory regime had higher LBW than the national average. No causal explanation exists.

### Cited Findings
**FACTS**
- DANE: 11 of every 100 live births weighed <2,500 g in 2023pr, "the highest historical figure", vs 9 in 2014 — [DANE boletín Nacimientos II trim 2023](https://www.dane.gov.co/files/operaciones/EEVV/bol-EEVV-Nacimientos-IItrim2023.pdf); [DANE boletín IV trim 2023](https://www.dane.gov.co/files/operaciones/EEVV/bol-EEVV-Nacimientos-IVtrim2023.pdf)
- Record 11.3% for Jan–Oct 2024pr — [DANE boletín Nacimientos 2024pr (19 Dec 2024)](https://www.dane.gov.co/files/operaciones/EEVV/2024/19-dic-2024/bol-EEVV-Nacimientos-2024pr.pdf); [Portafolio](https://www.portafolio.co/economia/regiones/tasa-de-natalidad-cayo-en-2024-frente-a-2023-620031)
- DANE: among **term** births, the LBW share rises especially from 2021, up 1.5 pp between 2014 and 2023 — [DANE boletín Nacimientos (2023/2024 series)](https://www.dane.gov.co/files/operaciones/EEVV/2024/19-dic-2024/bol-EEVV-Nacimientos-2024pr.pdf) (search-only snippet; exact base year and table to confirm)
- Uniandes Observatorio de Salud Pública: LBW 10.5% in 2022, +6.3% vs 2021 and +26.4% vs 2005; 9.4% (2020pr) to 11.0% (2023pr) — [Boletín agosto 2024](https://medicina.uniandes.edu.co/sites/default/files/observatorio-sp/boletin-agoto2024-observatorio-sp-uniandes.pdf). Its July 2026 bulletin: LBW at 11.02% is the only indicator in its domain that worsened vs baseline (9.1%), and it is the "most urgent" priority (target 9%) — [Boletín julio 2026](https://medicina.uniandes.edu.co/sites/default/files/observatorio-sp/boletin-julio2026-observatorio-sp-uniandes.pdf)
- Earlier LBW rate per 1,000: 88.1 (2015) to 90.4 (2020), per the DANE EEVV 2021 bulletin — [DANE boletín III trim 2021pr](https://www.dane.gov.co/files/investigaciones/poblacion/bt_estadisticasvitales_nacimientos_IIItrim_2021pr.pdf)
- Global contrast: a *Lancet Global Health* meta-analysis reported by El Tiempo has Colombia LBW rising from 7.7% to 8.8% (2000–2015) while it improved globally — [El Tiempo](https://www.eltiempo.com/salud/bebes-con-bajo-al-nacer-peso-en-colombia-estudio-revela-cifras-363912). The prompt's figure of 7.3% for 2000 is **not verified**; El Tiempo gives 7.7%.
- Term LBW **fell** 2000–2014 "in Colombia and all its regions" — [Tendencia del bajo peso al nacer en recién nacidos a término… 2000–2014, Rev Chil Nutr 2020](https://scielo.conicyt.cl/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S0717-75182020000100022). Over 5.4 million term singletons 2008–2018, LBW prevalence fell slightly, but education-related LBW inequality **increased** — [Socioeconomic inequalities in birth outcomes, PLOS ONE 2021](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0255150)
- INS surveillance of term LBW (event 110): 2.8 per 100 live births (2017), 3.0 (2018), "no downward trend since 2013"; highest in Vaupés, Bogotá and Boyacá — [INS informe BPN a término 2017](https://www.ins.gov.co/buscador/Informesdeevento/BAJO%20PESO%20AL%20NACER%20A%20T%C3%89RMINO%202017.pdf); [INS 2018](https://www.ins.gov.co/buscador-eventos/Informesdeevento/Bajo%20Peso%20al%20Nacer_2018.pdf); [INS PE VI 2022: 3.4%](https://www.ins.gov.co/buscador-eventos/Informesdeevento/BAJO%20PESO%20AL%20NACER%20PE%20VI%202022.pdf)
- **Reversed gradient:** 2005–2009 LBW averaged 8.6%, but 9.2% among contributory-regime mothers, which is higher than the national average — [An analysis of low birth weight in Colombia, 2005–2009 (PubMed 25124129)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25124129). Bogotá "systematically" has the highest LBW share nationally (2026 Ediciones Uniandes book, which points to CO/SO₂/NO₂ pollution and psychosocial stress) — [El Tiempo on pollution and birth weight in Bogotá](https://www.eltiempo.com/amp/salud/el-aire-contaminado-que-respira-una-embarazada-en-bogota-puede-determinar-el-peso-de-su-bebe-advierte-investigacion-3541625)
- Preterm (<37 wk) share: 11.2% (2022); 2023pr term births 88.3%, implying about 11.7% preterm. Amazonas has the highest preterm share by residence (14.9%). One DANE snippet mentions a series maximum of 13.8% whose referent is unclear — [DANE boletín III trim 2023](https://www.dane.gov.co/files/operaciones/EEVV/bol-EEVV-Nacimientos-IIItrim2023.pdf); [DANE II trim 2023](https://www.dane.gov.co/files/operaciones/EEVV/bol-EEVV-Nacimientos-IItrim2023.pdf). Prematurity rose from 8.49% (2007, lowest) to a maximum in 2016 — [Fundación Canguro, Comportamiento de la prematuridad 2007–2016](https://fundacioncanguro.co/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Comportamiento-de-la-prematuridad-en-Colombia-durante-los-a%C3%B1os-2007-a-2016.pdf)
- **Data caveat (important):** DANE public birth microdata code gestational age in bins "<22, 22–27, **28–37**, 38–41, 42+". Week 37 (early term) is lumped with preterm — [DANE microdata catalog EEVV 2012–2013](https://microdatos.dane.gov.co/index.php/catalog/377/variable/F8/V591?name=MES); [IHSN catalog, T_GES](https://datacatalog.ihsn.org/catalog/7169/variable/F4/V342?name=t_ges). A 2008–2017 study using these bins counted 1,277,839 births "up to 37 weeks" out of 6,705,385 (~19%) and calls the preterm information "not homogeneous" — [El parto pretérmino desde los registros del nacido vivo 2008–2017](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/347564937_El_parto_pretermino_desde_los_registros_del_nacido_vivo_en_Colombia_periodo_2008_-_2017). PLOS ONE 2021 likewise notes gestational age is only categorical — [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0255150)
- OECD *Panorama de la Salud LAC 2020* (snippet via search synthesis): Colombia is the only country significantly above the LAC average, with about 15% preterm births — [OECD Panorama LAC 2020, "Nacimiento prematuro y bajo peso al nacer"](https://www.oecd.org/es/publications/panorama-de-la-salud-latinoamerica-y-el-caribe-2020_740f9640-es/full-report/preterm-birth-and-low-birth-weight_2c7f937f.html). **Not verified:** an English-language search did not confirm Colombia's position, and 15% may reflect the 28–37 bin issue.
- Venezuelan migrant mothers, 2018–2022: aOR 1.19 for LBW, 1.06 for preterm, 1.18 for small vulnerable newborns — [J Immigrant Minority Health (PubMed 41545813)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41545813/). 2016–2018 refugees: higher odds of preterm, LBW, VLBW (OR 1.30, 1.80) — [Health of Infants Born to Venezuelan Refugees (PubMed 33550477)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33550477/). Conflicting: no significant LBW difference after adjustment, attributed to selection through fetal loss — [Amuedo-Dorantes, García-Pérez & Pinto 2025, *International Migration Review*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/01979183251390571)
- COVID 2020: no significant change in preterm births despite fewer prenatal visits — [Changes in birth outcomes… COVID-19 2020, Colombia (PMC10175897)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10175897/)

**Press framing**
- "Record" LBW in the context of falling births: [Portafolio 2024](https://www.portafolio.co/economia/regiones/tasa-de-natalidad-cayo-en-2024-frente-a-2023-620031); [El Tiempo](https://www.eltiempo.com/salud/bebes-con-bajo-al-nacer-peso-en-colombia-estudio-revela-cifras-363912); [Canal Capital, "Estadísticas Vitales 2023 revelan cambios históricos"](https://www.canalcapital.gov.co/actualidad/estadisticas-vitales-2023-cambios-historicos-colombia)
- *Discrepancy:* [Radio Nacional](https://www.radionacional.co/actualidad/nacieron-145-mil-bebes-en-colombia-la-cifra-mas-baja-en-la-ultima-decada) states 12.6% for 2023, which does not match DANE's 11.0%. It may be a sub-period or subgroup.

**Existing explanations and evidence quality**
- Social determinants (low education, subsidized or uninsured, fewer than 4 prenatal visits, single, ethnic minority): INS — **Descriptive**. This does not explain a *rise* during a period when those risk factors improved — [INS, Factores asociados al BPN a término 2019](https://epidemiologiains.org/index.php/ren/article/download/57/73/133)
- Altitude (≈100 g less per 1,000 m), relevant to Bogotá (2,600 m) and Boyacá — **Partial** for levels, but altitude is constant and cannot explain the trend — [Gestión, altitude study](https://gestion.pe/mundo/la-altitud-provoca-que-los-bebes-nazcan-con-menos-peso-segun-un-estudio-noticia/)
- Air pollution in Bogotá — **Partial causal / Descriptive** (Ediciones Uniandes 2026) — [El Tiempo](https://www.eltiempo.com/amp/salud/el-aire-contaminado-que-respira-una-embarazada-en-bogota-puede-determinar-el-peso-de-su-bebe-advierte-investigacion-3541625)
- Venezuelan migration composition — **Partial**, with modest ORs (~1.2) that cannot by themselves produce a +2 pp national rise — [PubMed 41545813](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41545813/)
- Iatrogenic late-preterm births from cesarean and induction (single hospital, Cali) — **Speculation / Descriptive** — [Frontiers in Public Health 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2020.00200/full)

### Inferences
- **Why it is a puzzle:** LBW should fall with income, education, insurance coverage and prenatal care. In Colombia it rose, accelerated after 2020 (including at term), and is highest in the richest city and among the contributory regime.
- **INFERENCE — candidate hypotheses:** (1) **Measurement and selection:** better survival and registration of very small or extremely preterm births (more live births recorded instead of fetal deaths); increased institutional delivery with weighing. (2) **Compositional shift from the fertility collapse:** births fell about 15% in 2024. If the births that disappeared were disproportionately low-risk (e.g., second or third births to 20–34-year-olds) while older, first-time or ART-assisted births remained, LBW rises mechanically. The share of 35+ mothers rose from 10.4% to 12.7%. (3) **Obstetric practice:** scheduled cesareans at 37 weeks (or earlier) shift the gestational-age distribution left, which links Puzzle 1 and Puzzle 2. (4) **Multiples from ART** in high-income urban areas. (5) **Post-2020 maternal nutrition or food-price shock** (2021–2022 food inflation) for term LBW. (6) **Altitude × migration:** a larger share of births occurring in Bogotá or the highlands (not verified).
- **INFERENCE — discriminating variation:** a shift-share decomposition of the national trend into within-department and within-group changes vs changes in composition (department, maternal age, parity, regime, nationality, multiplicity); singleton vs multiple; LBW by cesarean vs vaginal; term LBW by altitude band (municipality altitude is exogenous); the 2021–2022 food-inflation shock by municipality market prices (DANE SIPSA); Venezuelan mother share by municipality and year; weekday-of-birth spikes in 37-week cesareans.
- **INFERENCE — data:** DANE EEVV microdata (public: weight in grams or bins, gestational-age bins with the 37-week caveat, delivery type, multiplicity, maternal nationality from about 2017, regime, residence municipality); fetal-death microdata (public) to test selection; SIVIGILA event 110 (term LBW); RIPS for ART procedures (restricted).
- **Puzzle-ness: HIGH. Research promise: HIGH.** A key first step is checking whether the restricted or newer DANE files carry exact gestational weeks (not verified).

### Gaps
- An exact national preterm series 2010–2024 with consistent <37-week definition (DANE tables not opened; the OECD 15% figure is unconfirmed).
- No study decomposes the 2014–2024 LBW rise.
- Whether DANE public microdata include exact weeks in recent years (the 2012–2014 dictionaries show bins only).

## Puzzle 3 — Why did exclusive breastfeeding fall between 2005 and 2015 while early initiation rose?

### Takeaway
ENSIN shows exclusive breastfeeding (<6 months) falling from 46.9% (2005) to 42.8% (2010) to 36.1% (2015), with a mean duration of only 1.8 months. Early initiation (first hour) rose to 72% over the same period. This goes against regional and global improvement and against rising education. The ENSIN 2024 national results were not found. The explanations offered (formula marketing, work) are untested.

### Cited Findings
- ENSIN 2015: 36.1% of infants under 6 months exclusively breastfed; MinSalud urged moving toward the WHO 50% target — [MinSalud, presentación ENSIN 2015](https://www.minsalud.gov.co/Paginas/Gobierno-presenta-Encuesta-Nacional-de-Situaci%C3%B3n-Nutricional-de-Colombia-ENSIN-2015.aspx); [INS news](http://www.ins.gov.co/Noticias/Paginas/En-Colombia-solo-el-36,1-de-la-poblaci%C3%B3n-practica-la-lactancia-materna-exclusiva.aspx)
- Down from 42.8% (2010) and 46.9% (2005); early initiation within one hour rose to 72% — [MinSalud ENSIN 2015 page](https://www.minsalud.gov.co/Paginas/Gobierno-presenta-Encuesta-Nacional-de-Situaci%C3%B3n-Nutricional-de-Colombia-ENSIN-2015.aspx); [El Tiempo, "Disminuyen cifras de lactancia materna en Colombia"](https://www.eltiempo.com/salud/disminuyen-cifras-de-lactancia-materna-en-colombia-155506)

## Puzzle 4 — Vaccination: post-2018 slide, a 2025 MMR drop, and extreme department dispersion. Are the coverage numbers even right?

### Takeaway
National coverage recovered partly after COVID (pentavalent about 90%, MMR at age 1 about 93% in 2023–24). MMR at age 1 then fell from 92.8% (2024) to 89.1% (2025), while pentavalent in Vichada is about 53–54% vs Bogotá 99%. WHO/UNICEF warn that reported coverage moves with the **denominator**: the target population was reported down 12% between 2023 and 2024. With births collapsing, administrative coverage based on projected cohorts is unreliable. This is part real puzzle, part measurement puzzle.

### Cited Findings
- MinSalud: in 2023–2024 tracer coverage grew; pentavalent about 90%, MMR at 1 year about 93%; age-5 coverage (DPT booster) lowest in 2023–2024 — reported via [Portafolio 2025](https://www.portafolio.co/economia/regiones/cobertura-de-vacunacion-mejora-en-2025-pero-persisten-rezagos-en-ninos-y-regiones-criticas-495236) and [MinSalud lineamientos 3ª jornada 2024](https://www.minsalud.gov.co/sites/rid/Lists/BibliotecaDigital/RIDE/VS/PP/PAI/lineamientos-tercera-jornada-nacional-vacunacion-plan-intensificacion-vacunacion-colombia-2024.pdf)
- MMR at age 1: 92.8% (2024) to 89.1% (2025) (ACEMI analysis). Pentavalent 3rd dose 91.6% nationally; Vichada 54.3%, Buenaventura 57.4%, Vaupés 76.8% — [Portafolio](https://www.portafolio.co/economia/regiones/cobertura-de-vacunacion-mejora-en-2025-pero-persisten-rezagos-en-ninos-y-regiones-criticas-495236)
- Así Vamos en Salud (2nd Annual Public Health Report): pentavalent in Vichada 52.94% (2025) vs Bogotá 99.32%; national average about 85% (per El Tiempo). Jan–Nov 2023 pentavalent was 10.5 pp below expected — [MinSalud lineamientos PAI 2024](https://www.minsalud.gov.co/sites/rid/Lists/BibliotecaDigital/RIDE/VS/PP/PAI/lineamiento-gestion-administracion-pai-2024.pdf)
- WUENIC 2025 revision: the 2024 estimate is based on DTP3 = 89%, with a "reported target population decline of 12 percent between 2023 and 2024". The 2019 reported increase came from "a greater relative decrease in the target population than in the number of doses administered". There has been no nationally representative household survey in the last 5 years — [WUENIC Colombia 2025 revision](https://data.unicef.org/wp-content/uploads/cp/immunisation/wuenic_col.pdf); [2022 revision](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/country-profiles/immunization/2023-country-profiles/immunization_col_2023.pdf)
- Schedule change: from 1 Jan 2023 the MMR booster moved to 18 months and the age-5 tracer became DPT. This breaks historical comparability — [Huila observatorio MMR](https://www.huila.gov.co/observatoriosalud/publicaciones/13275/cobertura-de-vacunacion-de-triple-viral-en-ninos-y-ninas-de-1-ano/)
- Editorial framing: [El Nuevo Siglo, "¿Qué está pasando con la vacunación?"](https://www.elnuevosiglo.com.co/editorial/que-esta-pasando-con-la-vacunacion)

**Existing explanations:** COVID disruption, rural dispersion, migration, PAI contracting and EPS crisis are mentioned in press and ministry lines — **Speculation / Descriptive**. No causal analysis of the department dispersion or the 2025 MMR fall was found.

### Inferences
- **Why it is a puzzle:** Department coverage ranges from about 53% to 99% with a single national PAI and centrally purchased vaccines. Moreover, the direction of change in 2025 (MMR down, pentavalent up) is odd if the shock were general access.
- **INFERENCE — candidate hypotheses:** (a) **denominator error:** coverage = doses / DANE-projected cohort. With actual births falling 12–15% a year in 2023–2024, projected denominators may be too high (coverage understated) or too low where migrants are born, so some "declines" may be artifacts. This is testable by recomputing coverage with EEVV births as the denominator. (b) The **EPS financial crisis and interventions** (Nueva EPS, Sanitas, etc., 2024–2025) disrupting vaccinator contracts. (c) Supply and logistics in Amazon/Orinoquía departments. (d) Vaccine hesitancy post-COVID. (e) Migration flows.
- **INFERENCE — discriminating variation:** municipality coverage recomputed with registered births; timing of EPS intervention by municipality market share; MMR vs pentavalent within municipality (an access shock should affect both; the 18-month schedule change affects only MMR).
- **INFERENCE — data:** PAI municipal coverage (MinSalud/SISPRO, public); PAIWEB nominal registry (restricted); EEVV births (public); Supersalud intervention dates (public).
- **Puzzle-ness: MEDIUM. Research promise: HIGH** (the denominator issue is a clean, policy-relevant contribution).

### Gaps
- A clean national 2018–2025 series of DTP3 and MCV1 from WUENIC (the file was not opened).
- No study found on municipal coverage vs EPS crisis.
