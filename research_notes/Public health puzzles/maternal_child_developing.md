## Puzzle 1: High coverage of skilled birth attendance, yet high or rising maternal mortality (Dominican Republic, Guyana, Suriname, Venezuela)

### Takeaway
The Dominican Republic's maternal mortality ratio (MMR) rose about 36% between 2000 and 2020 (PAHO: 107.3 per 100,000 in 2020) despite near-universal institutional delivery. Guyana, Suriname and Venezuela also combine ≥96% skilled attendance with MMR >120. The only explanation on offer is a vague "quality of care" story: one 2003 paper and one weak 2023 ecological study. This is the strongest puzzle in this set.

### Cited Findings
- **Size of the DR rise:** PAHO's *Health in the Americas* country profile estimates the DR MMR at 107.3 per 100,000 live births in 2020, a 35.8% increase on the 2000 estimate (implied baseline ≈79) — [PAHO Health in the Americas, DR profile (via ecoi.net)](https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/2121829.html).
  - WHO-based IndexMundi lists ~80 for 2000 — [IndexMundi](https://www.indexmundi.com/facts/dominican-republic/maternal-mortality-ratio).
  - Knoema: 69 in 2001 rising to 107 in 2020 — [Knoema](https://knoema.com/atlas/embed/Dominican-Republic/Maternal-mortality-ratio).
- **Discrepancy:** UNFPA's DR Country Programme Document gives 107 for **2018**, rising to 126 in 2020 and 169 in 2021 (COVID). It also cites a cesarean rate of 58% and calls the system "over-medicalized" — [UNFPA CPD DR](https://www.unfpa.org/sites/default/files/portal-document/DP.FPA_.CPD_.DOM_.7%20-%20Dominican%20Republic%20CPD%20-%20ENG.pdf).
  - These are national (UNFPA/MoH) series, not MMEIG modelled series. Researchers should compare MMEIG 2023 (published 2025) with the national surveillance numbers.
  - historysaid.com lists 76 (2000) and 121 (2020) and is unreliable — [historysaid](https://historysaid.com/dominican-republic/maternal-mortality/2020s).
- **Regional pattern:**
  - Guyana, Venezuela and Suriname show skilled birth attendance ≥96% but MMR above 120, which the OECD/World Bank *Panorama de la Salud: Latinoamérica y el Caribe 2020* links to possible quality-of-care problems — [OECD Panorama 2020](https://oecd.org/es/publications/panorama-de-la-salud-latinoamerica-y-el-caribe-2020_740f9640-es/full-report/component-15).
  - A study of WHO Mortality Database data (1997–2019) finds the largest increases in both the maternal mortality rate and the MMR in Venezuela, the Dominican Republic and Puerto Rico. Venezuela had the highest rate in 2019 (12.0 per 100,000 women) and the DR was second (7.9) — [PMC12291397](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12291397).
  - A joinpoint analysis of PAHO data finds significant upward MMR trends in Brazil, Puerto Rico and Venezuela — [Continental University CRIS](https://cris.continental.edu.pe/en/publications/trends-in-maternal-mortality-in-latin-america-and-the-caribbean-a/).
  - PAHO: Latin American maternal mortality fell 16.4% in 1990–2015 but **rose 15% in 2016–2020**. The region's ~17% reduction over 2000–2023 is the slowest of any region worldwide; the global figure is ~40% — [IPU/PAHO document](https://www.ipu.org/file/14941/download); [Jamaica Observer, 2025-04-10](https://www.jamaicaobserver.com/2025/04/10/paho-highlights-progress-reducing-maternal-mortality-caribbean-warns-persistent-challenges/).
- **Guyana:** a World Bank-derived series shows 133 (2013) falling to 112 (2020). That is high, not rising — [Macrotrends](https://download.macrotrends.net/global-metrics/countries/GUY/guyana/maternal-mortality-rate).
- **Paraguay:** the maternal mortality rate fell from 7.5 (1997) to 5.8 (2019) per 100,000 women — [PMC12291397](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12291397). **The seed claiming that Paraguay is rising is not supported.**

**Existing explanations:**
- **Miller et al. (2003), Int J Gynecol Obstet**, "Quality of care in institutionalized deliveries: the paradox of the Dominican Republic." The "paradox" label originates here. It argues that access to institutional care does not offset poor-quality obstetric care. Evidence: Descriptive (facility audit/qualitative; full text not read) — [Scilit record](https://scilit.com/publications/625bc1bbbe1e9813fa991262ebd20e71).
- **De Jesus et al. (2023), IJERPH 20(14):6413.** A cross-sectional ecological study of the DR finding that MMR rises with vaginal-birth rates and infant death rates, falls with single-mother birth rates, and is uncorrelated with hospital beds. Evidence: Speculation/Descriptive, an ecological correlation with counter-intuitive signs. The authors suggest near-miss/severe maternal morbidity outcomes — [PMC10379411](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10379411/); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jijerp/v20y2023i14p6413-d1198331.html).
- **JICA ex-post evaluation:** 98% hospital delivery in Health Region III, against a national MMR of 92 (2015). This is descriptive context only — [JICA](https://www2.jica.go.jp/en/evaluation/pdf/2020_1000402_4_f.pdf).
- **Press framing:**
  - Dominican Today (2025-10-19) ran a health story on the topic; the content was not seen in the snippet — [Dominican Today](https://dominicantoday.com/?p=248620).
  - The Jamaica Observer (2025-04-10) covered PAHO's warning — [link](https://www.jamaicaobserver.com/2025/04/10/paho-highlights-progress-reducing-maternal-mortality-caribbean-warns-persistent-challenges/).

### Inferences
- **INFERENCE: why it is a puzzle.** Standard development theory says facility births with skilled attendants are the main lever on MMR. The DR has had it for decades, yet MMR rose while GDP per capita grew strongly. "Quality of care" is a label, not a mechanism, and nobody has tested it causally.
- **INFERENCE: candidate hypotheses.**
  - **(a) Measurement artefact.** Better surveillance and death certification since ~2010 would raise *recorded* MMR. Discriminate by comparing the share of maternal deaths among all deaths of women 15–49 and the late-maternal/ill-defined codes over time.
  - **(b) Iatrogenic risk from very high cesarean rates (58%):** haemorrhage, sepsis, anaesthesia, and placenta accreta in later pregnancies. Test whether the MMR rise is concentrated in direct obstetric haemorrhage/sepsis and in hospitals with high cesarean rates.
  - **(c) Overcrowded public referral maternity hospitals.** A few large public maternity hospitals absorb rising volumes, including Haitian migrant women. Test with hospital-level deaths per delivery and changes in the migrant share of births.
  - **(d) Composition.** Haitian-origin mothers face language and legal barriers and arrive late. Use nationality fields in the DR birth/death registers.
  - **(e) Rising hypertensive disorders and obesity.**
- **INFERENCE: data.**
  - DR DHS (ENDESA 2007, 2013) and MICS (ENHOGAR-MICS 2014, 2019) are public via DHS/MICS. They give delivery mode, place, and sisterhood MMR (DHS 2007 only).
  - The DR Ministry of Health epidemiological bulletins (weekly maternal deaths by hospital) are public as PDFs; whether microdata exist is not verified.
  - The WHO Mortality Database (ICD-coded deaths) is public.
  - Guyana and Suriname have MICS rounds.
- **INFERENCE: research promise HIGH.** Hospital-level variation within one country, with quasi-experimental shocks such as maternity hospital openings and policy changes on Haitian migrants, offers identification. An "iatrogenic cesarean → maternal mortality" paper would be novel.

### Gaps
- The MMEIG 2023 (Trends in Maternal Mortality 2000–2023, published 2025) figures for the DR were not retrieved. It is unclear whether the modelled series still shows a rise.
- The full text of Miller et al. 2003 was not read.
- No study decomposing DR maternal deaths by cause and hospital over time was found.

## Puzzle 2: India's child anaemia reversal (NFHS-4 → NFHS-5), then NFHS-6 drops haemoglobin testing

### Takeaway
Anaemia among children aged 6–59 months rose from ~59% (2015–16) to 67.1% (2019–21) despite more iron-folic acid (IFA) distribution. NFHS-6 (fact sheets released 29 May 2026) dropped all seven anaemia indicators, citing doubts about capillary testing. Real deterioration or measurement artefact is an open question with high research value.

### Cited Findings
- **The rise:** child anaemia went from 59% (NFHS-4) to 67% (NFHS-5); some sources give the baseline as 58.6% — [BMC Public Health 2023 (PMC10436448)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10436448/); [IJCH letter](https://www.iapsmupuk.org/journal/index.php/IJCH/article/view/2449).
- Anaemia also rose among women (57% in NFHS-5), pregnant women and men. Assam had among the largest rises — [Factly](https://factly.in/data-nfhs-5); [The Wire](https://thewire.in/health/nfhs-key-indicators-missing-government-clarifies).
- **Supplementation:** the rise occurred even though IFA consumption rose significantly under the national iron programme — [BMC Public Health 2023 PDF](https://bmcpublichealth.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s12889-023-16398-w.pdf).
- **Existing explanation (2023, BMC Public Health), "Key drivers of reversal of trend in childhood anaemia in India":** a Fairlie decomposition on NFHS-4 and NFHS-5 names maternal education, maternal anaemia and socioeconomic status as key drivers. Evidence: Descriptive, with a decomposition of changes in covariates. It cannot explain a rise when education and wealth *improved* over the period (INFERENCE) — [PMC10436448](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10436448/).
- **Earlier period (Nguyen et al. 2018, BMJ Global Health), 2006–2016, when anaemia fell:**
  - The decomposition explains 49% of child Hb change.
  - Contributions: maternal schooling 10%, coverage of nutrition and health interventions 18%, SES 7%, sanitation 3%, meat/fish 3%.
  - Evidence: Descriptive/decomposition — [PMC6202996](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6202996/).
- **NFHS-6 omission:**
  - The fact sheets (released 29 May 2026) list 43 indicators dropped and 13 added; all seven anaemia indicators are in the "dropped" column — [OC Academy blog](https://www.ocacademy.in/blogs/nfhs-6-anaemia-data-testing-changes/); [The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/health/suppressing-data-nfhs-6-factsheet-drops-anaemia-cancer-hiv-indicators/).
  - The Health Ministry says haemoglobin testing was not done because of doubts about capillary (finger-prick) sampling. Anaemia will instead be estimated from ICMR's Diet and Biomarkers Survey (venous blood), and the missing indicators "will feature in the national report" — [The Wire](https://thewire.in/health/nfhs-key-indicators-missing-government-clarifies); [Daily Excelsior](https://www.dailyexcelsior.com/?p=2228074); [Newswav](https://newswav.com/article/missing-nfhs-6-indicators-not-dropped-will-feature-in-national-report-healt-A2606_i7tQXa).
- **Press framing:**
  - The Quint calls dropping the questions "a mistake" and argues capillary testing was known to be problematic — [The Quint](https://www.thequint.com/amp/story/fit/citizen-fit/anaemia-nfhs-6-questions-dropped-a-mistake).
  - The South First frames it as "suppressing data" — [link](https://thesouthfirst.com/health/suppressing-data-nfhs-6-factsheet-drops-anaemia-cancer-hiv-indicators/).
  - Outlook India asks why more than half of India remains anaemic despite interventions — [Outlook](https://www.outlookindia.com/national/why-more-than-half-the-population-of-india-remains-anaemic-despite-years-of-intervention).
  - ThePrint links the NFHS-5 rise to the economy — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/opinion/how-is-indias-economy-affecting-health-look-at-rise-in-anaemia-among-women/572904).
  - Congress president Kharge criticised the omissions (per The Wire).

### Inferences
- **INFERENCE: why it is a puzzle.** Every standard determinant improved between 2015–16 and 2019–21: wealth, maternal schooling, sanitation under Swachh Bharat, IFA coverage under Anaemia Mukt Bharat (launched 2018), and the PDS. Yet measured child anaemia rose 8 percentage points. The government itself attributes this to methodology, which is a testable claim.
- **INFERENCE: candidate hypotheses.**
  - **(a) Measurement.** HemoCue capillary drop variability, changes in device or cuvette lots, field team protocols, the altitude adjustment, and NFHS-5's fieldwork split around COVID (Phase 1 in 2019–20, Phase 2 in 2020–21).
  - **(b) Real change.** Diet quality, inflammation and infection, dietary shocks from demonetisation in 2016, possibly air pollution (PM2.5 is linked to anaemia in the literature; not verified here), and the haemoglobinopathy share in tribal areas.
  - **(c) Cut-off issues.** WHO revised Hb thresholds guidance in 2024 and Indian experts argue the cut-offs are too high for Indians; both points are recalled, **not verified**.
- **INFERENCE: discriminating variation.**
  - NFHS-5 Phase 1 vs Phase 2 states, pre- vs post-COVID fieldwork.
  - Within-district comparison of anaemia jumps against changes in survey agency or team ID (team identifiers exist in the NFHS microdata).
  - Comparison with the Comprehensive National Nutrition Survey (CNNS 2016–18, venous blood): CNNS reported much lower child anaemia (~41%, **not verified**), which supports the capillary-artefact story.
  - The Hb distribution shape: a whole-distribution shift vs fatter tails from measurement noise.
- **INFERENCE: data.** NFHS-4/5 microdata are public via the DHS Program, and NFHS-6 will likely follow. CNNS microdata are available on request (**not verified**). ICMR biomarker survey availability is unknown.
- **INFERENCE: research promise HIGH.** A measurement-economics paper can decompose a national headline statistic that shaped policy: survey team effects, device effects, and the distribution shift. The NFHS-6 omission makes this timely.

### Gaps
- The full decomposition tables of the 2023 BMC paper were not read.
- The CNNS child anaemia figure was not verified in this search.
- No paper testing NFHS-5 survey-team or device effects on Hb was found. The gap may be real, or the search may have missed it.
- The ICMR Diet and Biomarkers Survey timeline is unknown.

## Puzzle 3: Philippines vaccine coverage collapsed before the Dengvaxia scare

### Takeaway
The Dengvaxia controversy (late 2017) is the standard explanation for the Philippine immunisation collapse and the 2019 measles outbreak. But DTP1 had already fallen from 98% (2013) to 69% (2015), years earlier. The pre-2017 decline is poorly explained.

### Cited Findings
- **Timing and size:**
  - DTP1 fell from 98% in 2013 to 69% in 2015 and declined further after a 2016 spike.
  - Measles-containing vaccine coverage fell steadily after the 2014 outbreak and fell further after Dengvaxia, preceding the early-2019 outbreak.
  - Vaccine stock-outs are noted as a possible cause of the 2014–2016 DTP/HepB drop.
  - Source: [Human Vaccines & Immunotherapeutics (2020) via Informa](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/21645515.2020.1841541).
- **Confidence:** an LSHTM survey (~1,500 respondents) found "strongly agree vaccines are important" fell from 93% (2015) to 32% (2018), and safety perceptions fell from 82% to 21% — [Asian Scientist](https://www.asianscientist.com/?p=110583); [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/1.4930139).
- **Conflicting measles coverage figures:**
  - 88% (2014) → 55% (2018) per one report.
  - The DOH cited 70% (2017) → 39% (2018).
  - Sources: [Visayan Daily Star](https://visayandailystar.com/?p=11588); [Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/amp/456301).
  - WUENIC (WHO/UNICEF estimates) should be checked; the figures differ.
- **Press framing:**
  - Philstar (2019-03-06), "Beyond the Dengvaxia scare: Complacency, devolution of health system also account for measles outbreak" — [Philstar](https://www.philstar.com/headlines/2019/03/06/1898870/headlines).
  - Manila Bulletin (2019-01-08), "The Dengvaxia issue and the current measles outbreak" — [PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/philippines/manila-bulletin/20190108/282269551535705).
  - Arab Times (2018-12-04), "Measles threat in Philippines as trust in vaccines declines" — [PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/kuwait/arab-times/20181204/282441350159824).
  - The WHO representative noted outbreaks "every three to four years" (2011, 2014) — per Philstar.
- **Existing explanations:**
  - Dengvaxia confidence shock (Larson/LSHTM). Evidence: Descriptive/Partial causal for 2018+.
  - Stock-outs and procurement problems. Evidence: Speculation/Descriptive.
  - Devolution of health services to LGUs and complacency. Evidence: Speculation (press).

### Inferences
- **INFERENCE: why it is a puzzle.** A 29-point drop in DTP1 between 2013 and 2015 is enormous for a middle-income country with a CCT (4Ps, Pantawid) that conditions on child health visits. It predates the confidence shock, so supply-side failure, data artefacts or the Typhoon Haiyan aftermath (Nov 2013) are candidates. None has been tested.
- **INFERENCE: hypotheses and variation.**
  - **(a) National procurement failure/stock-outs (2014–16).** This predicts a nationwide, simultaneous drop across antigens and a quick rebound when supply resumes. Test with antigen-specific and region-specific timing.
  - **(b) A denominator or administrative data artefact** (population projections, a switch in the reporting system). Compare administrative coverage with survey coverage: NDHS 2013, NDHS 2017 and NDHS 2022 card/recall coverage, which are public via DHS.
  - **(c) Dengvaxia shock 2017+.** The vaccination was school-based in specific regions (NCR, Central Luzon, CALABARZON, and later Central Visayas, recalled, **not verified**), which gives geographic treatment intensity for a DiD on routine infant immunisation using NDHS 2017 vs 2022 birth cohorts.
  - **(d) LGU devolution and local political shocks.**
- **INFERENCE: data.** NDHS 2013/2017/2022 are public (DHS). Philippine DOH FHSIS administrative coverage is likely available on request. WUENIC is public.
- **INFERENCE: research promise MEDIUM-HIGH.** The Dengvaxia DiD may already exist (not checked). The pre-2017 collapse appears understudied.

### Gaps
- WUENIC Philippines series for DTP3/MCV1 2010–2024 not retrieved.
- Search not done for existing Dengvaxia DiD economics papers.
- Recovery after 2022 not checked.

## Puzzle 4: Brazil's childhood vaccination coverage started falling in 2015, before COVID

### Takeaway
Brazil kept high, stable coverage for about two decades. Coverage peaked around 2015, then fell across all antigens. By 2021 coverage was 23–39 points below 2015, and measles returned in 2018 (10,346 cases). COVID worsened the decline but did not start it. The 2015–2019 onset has no solid causal explanation.

### Cited Findings
- Coverage stable for nearly two decades, peaking in 2015 and then declining — [Revista Pesquisa FAPESP](https://revistapesquisa.fapesp.br/en/a-drop-in-childhood-vaccination/).
- In 2021, hepatitis A, hepatitis B, meningococcal C, pentavalent, pneumococcal, polio, rotavirus and MMR coverage were 23–39 percentage points lower than in 2015 — [FAPESP](https://revistapesquisa.fapesp.br/en/a-drop-in-childhood-vaccination/); [SciELO RBEpid 2023](https://scielosp.org/pdf/rbepid/2023.v26/e230047/en).
- Third-dose polio coverage fell from 95.4% in 2015; the later value was truncated in the snippet — [MDPI Vaccines 2024](https://mdpi.com/2076-393X/12/12/1434).
- An ecological study of SI-PNI data (2011–2021) finds coverage declining in all regions, except MMR in the South and Midwest — [SciELO RESS 2024](https://scielosp.org/pdf/ress/2024.v33nspe2/e20231219/en).
- Brazil was declared measles-free in 2016. Measles re-emerged in 2018 with 10,346 cases, mainly in the North (Roraima and Amazonas, with Venezuelan migration as context) — [BJID 2026](https://www.bjid.org.br/en-download-pdf-S1413867026003569); [Ciência & Saúde Coletiva 2023](https://scielosp.org/pdf/csc/2023.v28n2/337-337/en).
- A municipality-level study of MMR coverage and deprivation (2006–2020) mentions austerity policies (the 2016 spending cap) as context; its findings were not visible in the snippet — [LSHTM Research Online](https://researchonline.lshtm.ac.uk/id/eprint/4676004/1/Municipality-level%20measles%2C%20mumps%2C%20and%20rubella%20%28MMR%29%20vaccine%20coverage%20and%20deprivation%20in%20Brazil%20A%20nationwide%20ecological%20stud.pdf).
- FAPESP notes the SI-PNI data are "not 100% accurate" — [FAPESP](https://revistapesquisa.fapesp.br/en/a-drop-in-childhood-vaccination/).
- **Existing explanations:** complacency, misinformation, austerity, COVID and migration. Evidence: Speculation/Descriptive. No causal study was found.

### Inferences
- **INFERENCE: why it is a puzzle.** Brazil's SUS and PNI were a global model. The decline began before COVID and before Bolsonaro-era vaccine politics (2019+), and it hit all antigens at once.
- **INFERENCE: hypotheses.**
  - **(a) Registration artefact.** SI-PNI migrated from aggregate dose reporting to individual nominal registration (rolled out ~2013–2019, recalled, **not verified**), and the SINASC denominators changed. Simultaneous drops across antigens are a signature of an artefact. Discriminate with municipality-level timing of the system switch against coverage breaks, and with survey-based coverage (the 2020 national coverage survey "Inquérito de Cobertura Vacinal", recalled, **not verified**).
  - **(b) Fiscal austerity and municipal primary-care cuts** (Mais Médicos exit of Cuban doctors in late 2018). The exit is a sharp municipal-level shock usable in a DiD.
  - **(c) Misinformation and political polarisation.** Use municipality vote shares as heterogeneity.
  - **(d) Bolsa Família conditionality enforcement changes.**
- **INFERENCE: data.**
  - DATASUS SI-PNI coverage by municipality, month and antigen, and SINASC births: public via TabNet/OpenDataSUS.
  - Mais Médicos placement data: public.
  - Election data from the TSE: public.
- **INFERENCE: research promise HIGH.** The data are rich and public, and there are sharp municipal shocks. The data-artefact question alone would be a contribution.

### Gaps
- Whether the nominal-registration migration timing has been tested against coverage is unknown.
- A survey-based coverage benchmark was not retrieved.
- Recovery in 2023–2025 was not checked.

## Puzzle 5: Neonatal deaths and stillbirths lag while under-5 mortality falls (global; Bangladesh as a sharp case)

### Takeaway
The neonatal share of under-5 deaths rose from 41% (2000) to 47% (2022) and is close to 50% in 2024. Stillbirth reduction slowed by 53% after 2015, against 42% for under-5 mortality. In Bangladesh, neonatal mortality stalled or rose (28 → 30 per 1,000) between BDHS 2014 and 2017–18 despite rising facility births. The global pattern is partly expected (post-neonatal causes are easier to fix), but the country-level stalls are poorly explained.

### Cited Findings
- **Global neonatal share:** 41% of under-5 deaths in 2000 vs 47% in 2022, as under-5 deaths fell from 9.9 to 4.9 million — [Down To Earth on UN IGME 2023 report](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/health/almost-5-million-children-died-before-their-fifth-birthday-in-2022-finds-un-report-95007); [World Bank Data Blog](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/opendata/un-report-historic-milestone-global-child-deaths-fall-below-5-million-2022).
- **2024 (UN IGME report of March 2025/2026):** 4.9 million under-5 deaths, 2.3 million of them newborns (nearly 50%). The pace of under-5 reduction has slowed by >60% since 2015 — [UNICEF press release](https://www.unicef.org/romania/press-releases/progress-reducing-child-deaths-slows-49-million-children-under-five-die-2024).
- **Stillbirths:**
  - ~1.9 million in 2023; sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia account for 80%.
  - Since 2015 the stillbirth annual rate of reduction slowed by 53% relative to 2000–2015, against 42% for under-5 mortality.
  - Stillbirths fell by "over a third" since 2000.
  - Sources: [UNICEF/WHO press release, 2025-03-25](https://www.unicef.org/press-releases/decades-progress-reducing-child-deaths-and-stillbirths-under-threat-warns-united); [WHO](https://www.who.int/news/item/25-03-2025-decades-of-progress-in-reducing-child-deaths-and-stillbirths-under-threat--warns-the-united-nations); UN IGME "Standing up for Stillbirth" / "Never Forgotten" reports — [UNICEF data](https://data.unicef.org/resources/never-forgotten-stillbirth-estimates-report/).
- **Bangladesh:**
  - NMR 28 (2014) → 30 (2017–18) per BDHS — [Prothom Alo](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/Infant-mortality-rate-not-decreasing).
  - Another analysis gives 31.9 in 2017 (50.2 in 2000) — [Health Science Reports 2024 (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/8bbd211bc10341b0871408204e7129a3).
  - A Scientific Reports (2024) analysis of BDHS 2011/2014/2017–18 on the under-5 stall finds poverty and low education raise risk, with ANC/PNC less protective than expected — [Scientific Reports (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/6212b44a7c3145ae9f9cb8b85d9f54af).
  - Early neonatal deaths are >80% of neonatal deaths (PLOS ONE, cited in the same snippet set) — [KNAV catalog record](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/cmedm,38770805).
  - BDHS 2022 key indicators: under-5 mortality 43 → 31 (2017 → 2022) — [UNB](https://unb.com.bd/category/Bangladesh/under-5-mortality-rate-marks-sharp-fall-in-bangladesh-report/113572).
  - The 2022 NMR was **not retrieved**; BDHS 2022 NMR ≈20 is recalled, **not verified**.
- Press framing:
  - "Infant mortality rate not decreasing" — Prothom Alo (BDHS 2017–18 release, date not verified) — [link](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/Infant-mortality-rate-not-decreasing).
  - The South First: "UN report credits India for reducing child mortality but flags neonatal challenge" — [link](https://thesouthfirst.com/health/un-report-credits-india-for-reducing-child-mortality-but-flags-neonatal-challenge/).
- **Existing explanations:**
  - Neonatal deaths depend on quality of intrapartum and newborn care, not just access. Evidence: Descriptive (consensus).
  - In Bangladesh: poverty and education (Scientific Reports 2024). Evidence: Descriptive.
  - Survey noise: BDHS NMR confidence intervals are wide. Evidence: an untested caveat.

### Inferences
- **INFERENCE: why it is a puzzle.** The global rise in the neonatal share is predictable from epidemiology. What stands out is a large country with booming facility deliveries (Bangladesh, where C-section rates rose steeply; ~45% in BDHS 2022 is recalled, **not verified**) seeing NMR stall in 2014–2018 and stillbirths stagnate. A facility-birth boom without quality could shift deaths rather than prevent them.
- **INFERENCE: hypotheses.**
  - **(a) Reclassification between stillbirth and early neonatal death** as more births occur in facilities. Discriminate using the *perinatal* mortality rate, which is invariant to reclassification, against NMR and stillbirths separately.
  - **(b) Low-quality private clinic births.**
  - **(c) Heat and arsenic exposure.**
  - **(d) Sampling noise.** Pool the birth histories.
- **INFERENCE: data.** BDHS full birth histories (1993–2022) are public, as are the Bangladesh SVRS (BBS, reports) and MICS 2019. The BDHS 2017–18 and 2022 pregnancy-history modules (2022 introduced a full pregnancy history, recalled, **not verified**) allow stillbirth analysis.
- **INFERENCE: research promise MEDIUM.** The perinatal-reclassification test is cheap and could matter for many countries.

### Gaps
- Bangladesh BDHS 2022 NMR and stillbirth rate not retrieved.
- No country-level stillbirth trend puzzles (rising stillbirths) were identified in snippets.

## Puzzle 6: Cesarean epidemic with flat preterm birth and slow low-birthweight decline (Egypt, Brazil, Dominican Republic; global)

### Takeaway
Egypt's cesarean share went from 52% (2014) to ~72% (2021). Brazil's sits at ~57%, rising in the North and Northeast while falling in the Southeast, and private maternity hospitals in São Paulo run at ~80%. Globally, preterm birth did not fall between 2010 and 2020 (9.8% → 9.9%), and low birthweight fell only 0.3% a year in 2012–2020. Drivers of cesareans (private sector, incentives) are partly described. Whether medicalisation contributes to stagnant preterm/LBW outcomes, through scheduled early-term cesareans, has not been tested in these countries.

### Cited Findings
- **Egypt:**
  - The cesarean share rose from 52% (2014) to 72% (2021) per the Egypt Family Health Survey 2021; a CAPMAS official also put it at ~72% of ~2.1 million births in 2021 — [Egyptian Streets, 2025-10-22](https://egyptianstreets.com/2025/10/22/whats-fueling-egypts-rise-in-cesarean-deliveries/); [IEA World](https://www.iea-world.org/egypts-skyrocketing-c-sections/); [Worldcrunch](https://worldcrunch.com/in-the-news/egypt-c-section-health/amp/).
  - The 72% comes from the EFHS 2021, not a standard DHS; Egypt DHS 2014 is the last DHS-branded round.
  - Private hospitals have higher cesarean odds (BMC Pregnancy & Childbirth 2017) — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s12884-017-1591-2.pdf).
  - Policy response: since August 2025 the Ministry of Health has required private facilities to file monthly reports of births and cesareans by Robson class — [Egyptian Streets](https://egyptianstreets.com/2025/10/22/whats-fueling-egypts-rise-in-cesarean-deliveries/); [Al-Ahram Weekly, "Cracking down on C-sections"](https://english.ahram.org.eg/NewsContent/50/1201/552382/AlAhram-Weekly/Egypt/Cracking-down-on-Csections.aspx).
  - Commentary: Rana Hendy and Lobna Shaheen, Project Syndicate (Sept 2024), "Egypt's rise in cesarean births driven by economic turmoil, private health" — [Project Syndicate](https://www.project-syndicate.org/commentary/egypt-rise-cesarean-births-driven-by-economic-turmoil-private-health-by-rana-hendy-1-and-lobna-shaheen-2024-09).
- **Brazil (SINASC):**
  - National cesarean rate: 55.4% (2016), 55.7% (2017), 55.9% (2018), 56.3% (2019), 57.2% (2020), 57% (2021, preliminary) — [RBGO editorial 2023](https://journalrbgo.org/?p=27117).
  - Time-series analysis: national stabilisation from 2012, rising in the North and Northeast, falling in the Southeast; projected 57.4% in 2030 — [SciELO C&SC](https://scielo.br/j/csc/a/VPqfbsmvX65cHQDhvzXDVgF/?lang=en).
  - São Paulo supplementary (private-insurance) births in 2015–2021: cesarean rate 80%, 71% higher than public services. An earlier SINASC study found 85% in private hospitals — [PMC11560135](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11560135/); [CSP 2024 evaluation of Parto Adequado](https://scielosp.org/pdf/csc/2024.v29n1/e00512023/pt).
  - Parto Adequado (ANS) produced "stabilization and even a slight decrease", with an uptick in 2022 — [RBGO](https://journalrbgo.org/?p=27117).
- **DR:** cesarean rate 58% — [UNFPA CPD](https://www.unfpa.org/sites/default/files/portal-document/DP.FPA_.CPD_.DOM_.7%20-%20Dominican%20Republic%20CPD%20-%20ENG.pdf).
- **Global preterm (Ohuma et al., Lancet 2023):**
  - 13.4 million preterm births in 2020 (9.9%) vs 13.8 million (9.8%) in 2010.
  - Annual rate of reduction −0.14%; "no measurable change".
  - No region significantly reduced preterm rates.
  - Sources: [BVS abstract](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/sms/resource/pt/mdl-37805217); [WHO news, 2023-10-06](https://www.who.int/news/item/06-10-2023-1-in-10-babies-worldwide-are-born-early--with-major-impacts-on-health-and-survival).
  - (The snippet named "Moran"; the lead author is Ohuma, recalled, **not verified**.)
- **Global LBW (Okwaraji et al., Lancet 2024):**
  - 19.8 million (14.7%) in 2020 vs 22.1 million (16.6%) in 2000.
  - Annual rate of reduction 2012–2020: 0.3% worldwide (0.85% southern Asia, 0.59% sub-Saharan Africa).
  - Off track for the 30% target.
  - Sources: [BVS abstract](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/brasil/resource/pt/mdl-38430921); [LSHTM](https://researchonline.lshtm.ac.uk/id/eprint/4667148).
- **Existing explanations:**
  - Cesarean drivers: private-sector financial incentives, convenience and scheduling, fear of litigation, and maternal demand. Evidence: Descriptive. Brazil has stronger partial-causal literature (e.g., Parto Adequado evaluations).
  - The cesarean → early-term/preterm/LBW link in these countries: Speculation; no study found in snippets.

### Inferences
- **INFERENCE: why it is a puzzle.**
  - Egypt's 20-point rise in 7 years, with no proportional rise in income or in clinical indications, is extreme.
  - In Brazil the regional *divergence* (Southeast falling, North and Northeast rising) is unexplained.
  - Globally, the stagnation of preterm birth and LBW despite better maternal nutrition and ANC is a big unexplained fact. Scheduled pre-labour cesareans at 37–38 weeks, or iatrogenic preterm deliveries, are a candidate offsetting force in high-cesarean middle-income countries.
- **INFERENCE: discriminating variation.**
  - In Brazil's SINASC (microdata public: gestational week, Robson group, hospital CNES code, mode of delivery, weekday/hour of birth), test whether the cesarean rate rises with the share of births at 37–38 weeks and with preterm births. Births spike on weekdays at private hospitals.
  - Shocks: Parto Adequado rollout across hospitals (staggered entry), and ANS Resolução 368 (2015), which required disclosure of cesarean rates (recalled, **not verified**).
  - In Egypt: EFHS 2021 and DHS 2005/2008/2014 microdata (DHS is public; EFHS availability via CAPMAS is **not verified**). The August 2025 Robson reporting mandate is a potential policy shock.
  - DR: ENDESA 2013 and ENHOGAR-MICS 2019 are public.
- **INFERENCE: research promise HIGH for Brazil**, where public microdata, staggered programmes and precise gestational age come together. **MEDIUM for Egypt**, where data access is limited.

### Gaps
- No study directly linking national cesarean growth to early-term/preterm trends in Egypt or the DR was found.
- A Turkey figure (60.5% in 2022, from the previous round) was not re-verified here.
- Egypt EFHS 2021 microdata access is unknown.

## Puzzle 7: India's wasting is flat or rising while stunting falls and GDP grows ("South Asian enigma" redux)

### Takeaway
India's child wasting rose from ~17% (around 2000) to ~20–21% (2015–18). Other undernutrition indices fell over the same years, and NFHS-5 (2019–21) puts wasting at ~19% (recalled, **not verified**). South Asia's wasting (14.1%) is more than double the global 6.7%. Evidence on growth's effect is contested, and nothing explains why wasting specifically does not respond.

### Cited Findings
- **Regional level:** South Asia wasting 14.1% vs a global 6.7% — [Spheres of Influence](https://spheresofinfluence.ca/?p=23044); [ALRC](https://alrc.asia/south-asia-asias-children-on-the-verge-of-severe-malnourishment/). These are secondary sources.
- **India's trend:** 17.1% (2000) to >20% (2018), or 17.1% (1998–02) to 21% (2012–16), while other indices fell — [Fox Business (GHI coverage)](https://foxbusiness.com/markets/child-hunger-serious-in-india-while-its-economy-grows-fast); [The Secretariat](https://thesecretariat.in/article/india-s-nutrition-enigma-growing-economy-malnourished-children).
- **Existing explanations:**
  - Subramanyam, Kawachi, Berkman, Subramanian (2011, PLOS Medicine): no strong evidence that state-level economic growth reduced child undernutrition in India. Evidence: Partial (state-panel associations) — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/plo/pmed00/1000424.html).
  - Panda, Kumar and Joe (2024, Springer book chapter): growth has a more robust effect in 2005–06 to 2015–16 — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:spr:isbchp:978-981-97-6753-3_13).
  - Sanitation, maternal nutrition and infant diets: commentary — [Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/opinion/endemic-deprivation-1502909473.html); [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/health/childhood-stunting-affects-future-success-in-life-say-experts-54045).
  - Evidence on wasting specifically: Speculation.
- **Press framing:**
  - Fox Business/AP: "Child hunger 'serious' in India while its economy grows fast" (GHI release) — [link](https://foxbusiness.com/markets/child-hunger-serious-in-india-while-its-economy-grows-fast).
  - The Government of India disputes the GHI (recalled, **not verified**).

### Inferences
- **INFERENCE: why it is a puzzle.** Stunting and wasting usually move together with income. In India, stunting fell (48% → 35% from NFHS-3 to NFHS-5, recalled, **not verified**) while wasting did not. A large share of Indian wasting appears at birth or in early infancy, which points to intrauterine growth and LBW rather than acute food deprivation (this "wasting at birth" literature is recalled, **not verified**).
- **INFERENCE: hypotheses.**
  - **(a) Measurement.** Length-board and weighing errors, and inflation of the weight-for-height z-score variance. Test using SD of WHZ and flagged-data shares by survey team.
  - **(b) Seasonality of fieldwork.**
  - **(c) Heat exposure.** Within-district temperature shocks are linkable to the NFHS geocodes.
  - **(d) Rising birth-weight-adjusted early infant wasting from maternal thinness.**
  - **(e) A genuine body-proportion difference**, the "thin-fat Indian baby" hypothesis.
- **INFERENCE: data.** NFHS-1 to NFHS-5 microdata with GPS clusters are public (DHS); NFHS-6 is upcoming; CNNS is available on request.
- **INFERENCE: research promise MEDIUM.** It is a well-known enigma with many papers, but the wasting-specific mechanism (heat, seasonality, measurement) is still open.

### Gaps
- NFHS-5 and NFHS-6 wasting figures were not retrieved.
- The papers on "wasting at birth" were not verified in this search.

## Puzzle 8: Venezuela's infant mortality turned up around 2009, before the economic collapse

### Takeaway
Indirect estimates put Venezuela's infant mortality rate (IMR) at 21.1 per 1,000 in 2016, up from 15.0 in 2008. The long-run decline stopped *around 2009*, before the oil-price collapse (2014) and hyperinflation. Official 2016 data showed infant deaths +30% and maternal deaths +65% year-on-year, followed by a data blackout. The crisis explains post-2014, but the 2009 inflection is unexplained.

### Cited Findings
- **García, Correa & Rousset (2019), Lancet Global Health:**
  - Methods: census plus ENCOVI 2016, indirect estimation.
  - IMR 21.1 per 1,000 (90% CI 17.8–24.3) in 2016, ~1.4× the 2008 rate (15.0).
  - "Around 2009 the long-term decline ... stopped and a new pattern of increase was observed."
  - Sources: [PubMed 30686723](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30686723/); [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/2cc057d733f44e9eb059bfbe2b410bc7).
- **Ministry bulletins:** the ministry stopped publishing after July 2015. The 2016 data released in May 2017 showed infant deaths up 30.12% to 11,466 and maternal deaths up ~65% — [VOA](https://www.voanews.com/amp/venezuela-health-data-soaring-infant-mortality-malaria/3845006.html); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/latino/venezuela-sees-steep-rise-maternal-infant-mortality-malaria-n757046); [RTÉ, 2017-05-10](https://www.rte.ie/news/world/2017/0510/874000-venezuela-infant-mortality).
  - The health minister was dismissed after the release (recalled, **not verified**).
- Press framing: Caracas Chronicles (2017-05-11), "The Tragedy Hidden Away in the Epidemiology Bulletins" — [link](https://www.caracaschronicles.com/2017/05/11/tragedy-hidden-away-epidemiology-bulletins/); AS/COA "Weekly Chart: Venezuela's Infant and Maternal Health Crisis" — [link](https://as-coa.org/articles/weekly-chart-venezuelas-infant-and-maternal-health-crisis).
- Venezuela has the highest maternal mortality rate per 100,000 women in Latin America in 2019 (12.0) — [PMC12291397](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12291397).
- **Existing explanations:** the economic collapse, health-system breakdown and shortages. Evidence: Descriptive/Partial for 2014+; None for the 2009 inflection.

### Inferences
- **INFERENCE: why it is a puzzle.** 2009–2013 was an oil-boom period with Misión Barrio Adentro and rising social spending, yet infant mortality began rising.
- **INFERENCE: hypotheses.**
  - (a) Hospital-sector deterioration masked by primary-care missions.
  - (b) The 2009–10 electricity crisis and dengue epidemics.
  - (c) Estimation artefact from indirect methods.
  - (d) Rising adolescent fertility.
  - Discriminate with the official death registry by cause (WHO Mortality Database has Venezuela ICD-10 through ~2014–2016, **not verified**) and with timing by state.
- **INFERENCE: data.** ENCOVI 2014–2023 (UCAB; microdata partially public, **not verified**); INE vital statistics up to ~2014; WHO Mortality Database; Venezuelan migrant births in Colombia, Brazil and Peru registries (Colombia's must be excluded per scope; Brazil's SINASC records mother's nationality).
- **INFERENCE: research promise MEDIUM.** Data access is poor, but the pre-crisis inflection is a clean question.

### Gaps
- No study explaining the 2009 turning point was found.
- Data after 2016 rely on ENCOVI and modelled UN IGME estimates.

## Secondary puzzles and discarded seeds

### Takeaway
Stunting stalls in Indonesia and Guatemala, and Egypt's double burden, are real but either partly artefactual (survey switches) or partly explained by inequality. Peru's stunting decline is well documented, though its causal attribution remains contested. Paraguay's maternal-mortality rise was not confirmed.

### Cited Findings
- **Indonesia (MEDIUM-LOW):**
  - Stunting 24.4% (2021, SSGI) → 21.6% (2022, SSGI) → 21.5% (2023, SKI, "cenderung stagnan") → 19.8% (2024, SSGI), against a 2024 target of 14% — [Kemkes Badan Kebijakan](https://www.badankebijakan.kemkes.go.id/en/ssgi-2024-prevalensi-stunting-nasional-turun-menjadi-198/); [Kompas, 2024-05-09](https://lestari.kompas.com/read/2024/05/09/170000786/10-provinsi-dengan-prevalensi-stunting-tertinggi-2023); [Profil Kesehatan Masyarakat 2023](https://kesprimkom.kemkes.go.id/assets/uploads/contents/others/Profil_Kesehatan_Masyarakat_Tahun_2023.pdf).

A better puzzle is the variation across 23 provinces above the national mean — [Kompas](https://lestari.kompas.com/read/2024/05/10/140000786/23-provinsi-punya-prevalensi-stunting-di-atas-nasional).

  - DHS 2014: stunting ~21%, wasting rising to 8%, overweight (WHZ>+2) 14.9% (vs a WHO database figure of 20.5% for 2008, possibly a reference-standard difference) — [EKB journal](https://journals.ekb.eg/article_68620_f8cb6b8f1931fd158f398d96b375bb54.pdf).
