## Puzzle 1 — Syphilis surge in Latin America (Brazil, Argentina, Chile), and Brazil's split between acquired and congenital syphilis

### Takeaway
Notified acquired syphilis has risen steeply since about 2010 in Brazil, Argentina and Chile, reaching all-time highs in 2024. In Brazil, gestational and acquired syphilis keep rising while congenital syphilis has been flat or falling since about 2019. Within countries the variation is very large: Córdoba's rate was about 2.4× Argentina's national rate in 2024. Existing explanations are ecological or speculative. The best-evidenced causal piece is the 2014–2017 benzathine penicillin shortage, and that evidence covers only congenital syphilis in Rio de Janeiro.

### Cited Findings
**Facts and figures**
- Argentina, notified syphilis cases: 26,647 (2022), 32,293 (2023), 36,917 (2024). 2024 is the highest on record, about 39% above 2022. Source: Boletín Epidemiológico Nacional (BEN), via press — [Tiempo de San Juan](https://www.tiempodesanjuan.com/el-pais/sifilis-record-los-contagios-aumentaron-casi-un-40-n417952); [El Economista AR](https://eleconomista.com.ar/actualidad/alerta-sifilis-argentina-crecen-40-casos-ya-quintuplican-vih-n90647/amp)
- Argentina, national notification rate: 56.1 per 100,000 (2019) to 93 per 100,000 (2024), +65.8%. The Centro region (Buenos Aires, CABA, Córdoba, Entre Ríos, Santa Fe) reported 61.3% of 2024 cases. Córdoba had the highest provincial rate, 220.3 per 100,000. About 76% of cases were aged 15–39 — [La Capital (Rosario)](https://www.lacapital.com.ar/informacion-general/record-contagios-sifilis-santa-fe-es-el-tercer-distrito-mas-casos-n10151740.html/amp); [El Litoral](https://www.ellitoral.com/informacion-general/sifilis-epidemiologia-registro-argentina-rosario-recaida-controles-prevencion_0_RuXf6dejxQ.html). **Discrepancy:** outlets give the age band as either 15–34 or 15–39.
- Argentina 2025: one outlet reports 36,702 cases in the first 44 weeks of 2025, implying the 2024 record will be exceeded. The page's date metadata is inconsistent, so treat this as **not verified** — [Todo Jujuy](https://www.todojujuy.com/jujuy/jujuy-supera-la-media-nacional-casos-sifilis-n282372)
- Brazil 2024: about 256,000 acquired, 89,000 gestational and 24,000 congenital syphilis cases. The acquired-syphilis detection rate was about 120 per 100,000 (one source gives 120.8), the highest in the historical series. Source: Ministry of Health bulletin released 16 Oct 2025, via [Metrópoles](https://www.metropoles.com/saude/sifilis-avanca-no-brasil-saiba). Official PDFs: [Boletim Sífilis 2025](https://www.gov.br/aids/pt-br/central-de-conteudo/boletins-epidemiologicos/2025/boletim_sifilis_2025.pdf); [Boletim especial out-2026](https://www.gov.br/saude/pt-br/centrais-de-conteudo/publicacoes/boletins/epidemiologicos/especiais/2026/be-da-sifilis-no-especial-out-2026.pdf) (not read).
- Brazil 2023: 25,002 congenital syphilis cases and 196 deaths. The Ministry of Health presents this as a decline consistent with its 2030 elimination plan — [TCM Notícia, citing MoH 2024 bulletin](https://tcmnoticia.com.br/?p=63928); [Boletim Sífilis 2024](https://www.gov.br/aids/pt-br/central-de-conteudo/boletins-epidemiologicos/2024/boletim_sifilis_2024_e.pdf)
- Brazil's congenital syphilis incidence rose steadily until 2018 and has fallen since 2019 — [CIDRAP summary / literature](https://www.cidrap.umn.edu/resilient-drug-supply/penicillin-shortages-tied-more-congenital-syphilis-rio)
- The split shows clearly at subnational level:
  - São Paulo city: gestational syphilis rose from 15.4 per 1,000 live births (2014) to 71.4 (2024), yet the 724 congenital cases in 2024 were the lowest in 11 years. Acquired syphilis was 259.5 per 100,000 in 2024 — [SMS São Paulo Boletim 2025](https://prefeitura.sp.gov.br/documents/d/saude/boletim-epidemiologico-sifilis-2025)
  - Espírito Santo: acquired syphilis reached 184.5 per 100,000 (2024), and congenital syphilis fell slightly to 14.2 per 1,000 live births — [SESA-ES](https://saude.es.gov.br/Media/sesa/vigilancia-epidemiologica/boletins/Boletim%20Epidemiologico%20Estadual%20Sífilis.pdf)
  - Paraíba (2020–24): acquired syphilis rising, gestational and congenital falling — [SES-PB Boletim 02/2024](https://paraiba.pb.gov.br/diretas/saude/arquivos-1/vigilancia-em-saude/boletim-02-siflis-2024.pdf/@@download/file/Boletim%2002%20Síflis%202024.pdf)
- Chile: syphilis rate 19 per 100,000 (2010) to 31.4 (2019), +65%. Syphilis made up about 50% of STI notifications in 2017–2021 — [Confluencia (UDD)](https://revistas.udd.cl/index.php/confluencia/article/download/637/558/1879). Congenital syphilis rose from 2018 and peaked in 2021, which the authors attribute to pandemic disruption of prenatal care. The 2016 rate was 0.1 per 1,000 births — [Andes Pediatría](https://andespediatrica.cl/index.php/rchped/article/download/5737/5490/51277). No ISP figures for 2023 were found.
- Regional picture (PAHO): congenital syphilis cases in Latin America and the Caribbean doubled after 2010 ([PAHO 2017](https://www.paho.org/en/news/24-5-2017-reduction-mother-child-transmission-hiv-and-syphilis-slows-down-latin-america-and)). There were 29,147 reported cases in 2020 and more than 30,000 (preliminary) in 2021. Antenatal screening in Latin America fell to 57% in 2018 and recovered to 70% in 2021. In 18 of 26 countries with data, syphilis prevalence among tested pregnant women increased — [PAHO 2023 slide deck](https://paho.org/sites/default/files/2023-01/2023-cde-3-etmi-plus-prev-elim-cong-syphilis.pdf); [Caribbean Today on PAHO warning](https://caribbeantoday.com/sections/health/paho-warns-against-increasing-cases-of-syphilis-and-congenital-syphilis)

**Press framing**
- Argentina's "record" framing, with Córdoba singled out (2025) — [La Capital](https://www.lacapital.com.ar/informacion-general/record-contagios-sifilis-santa-fe-es-el-tercer-distrito-mas-casos-n10151740.html/amp); [El Ancasti, "pico histórico"](https://www.elancasti.com.ar/informacion-general/sifilis-salud-advierte-un-pico-historico-el-pais-n562856/amp)
- Brazil: "Sífilis avança no Brasil", Metrópoles, Oct 2025 — [link](https://www.metropoles.com/saude/sifilis-avanca-no-brasil-saiba). SBMT president calls the congenital syphilis outbreak "a shame for the country" — [SBMT](https://en.sbmt.org.br/?p=21274)
- Chile: a physician describes the situation as "desbordada" ("overwhelming") — [El Dínamo, 3 Aug 2023](https://www.eldinamo.cl/pais/2023/08/03/la-explicacion-detras-del-aumento-de-casos-de-vih-y-la-desbordada-situacion-de-la-sifilis-en-chile/). The rise is attributed to a lack of sex education — [El Mostrador, 6 Jan 2023](https://www.elmostrador.cl/braga/2023/01/06/alertan-que-aumento-de-sifilis-se-debe-a-la-ausencia-de-educacion-sexual-con-enfoque-inclusivo-y-libre-de-prejuicios/)

**Existing explanations**
- **Benzathine penicillin shortage, 2014–2017.** In Rio de Janeiro, shortage was associated with a 2.23-fold higher risk of congenital syphilis (sociodemographic model). Shortages "at least doubled" congenital syphilis incidence from 2013 to 2017. Braga et al., *Clinical Infectious Diseases* (DOI 10.1093/cid/ciaa1716) — [CIDRAP](https://www.cidrap.umn.edu/resilient-drug-supply/penicillin-shortages-tied-more-congenital-syphilis-rio). **Evidence: Partial causal at best** (ecological, one city). A Fortaleza maternity study found the shortage did not change treatment there (same CIDRAP summary). The shortage cannot explain acquired syphilis continuing to rise after 2017.
- **Expanded rapid testing (detection rather than incidence).** Among Brazilian municipalities with more than 100,000 residents, rising acquired-syphilis trends were associated with more rapid-test availability, less condom availability, less penicillin availability and weaker primary care — [PMC9595568 / IDEAS PLoS ONE 0275253](https://ideas.repec.org/a/plo/pone00/0275253.html). Primary-care teams doing rapid tests increased detection and notification in pregnant women — [RESS 2024](https://scielosp.org/article/ress/2024.v33/e2023398). Mato Grosso authors also cite testing, falling condom use and primary-care reluctance to give penicillin — [PMC8621623](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8621623/). **Evidence: Descriptive / ecological.**
- **Behaviour: PrEP and less condom use, dating apps, changing sexual networks, migration, stigma.** These come from global reviews that mostly draw on high-income MSM data — [Pathogens 2025 review](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-0817/14/11/1148); [PMC12655136](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12655136/). **Evidence: Speculation or descriptive for Latin America.**
- **No sex education.** Chilean experts in the press — [El Mostrador](https://www.elmostrador.cl/braga/2023/01/06/alertan-que-aumento-de-sifilis-se-debe-a-la-ausencia-de-educacion-sexual-con-enfoque-inclusivo-y-libre-de-prejuicios/). **Evidence: Speculation.**

**Why it is a puzzle:** The rise is synchronised across countries with very different health systems: Brazil's tax-financed SUS, Argentina's fragmented system and Chile's high-income system. It continues years after the penicillin shortage ended. Within-country dispersion is extreme (Córdoba 220 vs a national rate of 93 per 100,000). In Brazil, detected maternal infection keeps rising while congenital cases fall, which is either a real prevention success or an artefact of changed case definitions or notification. No paper separates the share due to detection from the share due to true incidence.

### Inferences
- **INFERENCE (hypotheses).** (H1) Detection: the rapid-test rollout through primary care after about 2011–12 raised notified cases with little change in incidence. (H2) True incidence: lower perceived HIV risk (treatment-as-prevention, PrEP rollout in Brazil from 2018), app-mediated partner networks, and more men infected outside MSM networks, with bridging to women. (H3) Supply side: periodic penicillin stockouts and primary-care nurses' reluctance to inject benzathine penicillin raise reinfection and the duration of infectiousness. (H4) Brazil's congenital-syphilis decline after 2019 may partly reflect the 2017 change in the national congenital-syphilis case definition (adequate maternal treatment no longer required partner treatment). **This definition change is recalled, not verified.** A definitional break would mechanically lower congenital counts.
- **INFERENCE (discriminating variation).**
  - Staggered municipal timing of rapid-test arrival and of Rede Cegonha adhesion. Under H1, gestational detection jumps and then levels off, while H2 predicts a sustained rise.
  - The ratio of tertiary/latent to primary/secondary cases. Under pure detection, the latent share rises.
  - Municipal penicillin stockouts, from Ministry/CONASS purchasing records, as shocks to reinfection.
  - PrEP-dispensing site openings (SICLOM) as shocks for male acquired syphilis.
  - The 2017 case-definition break, which allows a regression discontinuity in time.
  - Argentina: provincial differences in Córdoba versus neighbours. One check is whether Córdoba's 220 per 100,000 reflects a provincial testing programme.
- **INFERENCE (data).**
  - Brazil: SINAN (acquired, gestational and congenital syphilis, with individual records via DATASUS TabNet and microdata); SINASC for denominators and prenatal visits; SIA-SUS for outpatient production, i.e. rapid tests performed and penicillin administered; SICLOM for PrEP; CNES for facilities. All public.
  - Argentina: SNVS 2.0 and BEN. Aggregates are public; microdata likely need a request.
  - Chile: DEIS/ISP. Aggregates are public.
- **Puzzle-ness: HIGH. Research promise: HIGH.** Brazil's microdata are rich and the variation is staggered, which suits applied micro (DiD, event studies).

### Gaps
- I could not extract Brazil's official national series of gestational and congenital rates for 2010–2024 from the Boletim PDFs (search-only).
- Paraguay was not covered. No figures were found.
- Whether the 2017 change in Brazil's congenital-syphilis definition affects trends: not verified.
- No study was found that decomposes the rise into detection and incidence for Argentina or Chile.

## Puzzle 2 — Mexico after Seguro Popular: coverage collapse, a "recovery" that may be a measurement change, and measles returning

### Takeaway
After Seguro Popular was replaced by INSABI (2020) and then IMSS-Bienestar, the share of Mexicans lacking health-care access more than doubled, from 16.2% (2018) to 39.1% (2022). The 2024 figure of 34.2% (INEGI) coincides with a questionnaire change. Under CONEVAL-consistent criteria it would be 44.1%. Measles returned in 2025–26 with more than 9,000 cases. Causal evidence on health outcomes is thin and only now appearing.

### Cited Findings
**Facts and figures**
- Lack of access to health services ("carencia por acceso a servicios de salud"): 16.2% (2018, 20.1 million people), 28.2% (2020), 39.1% (2022, 50.4 million). Source: CONEVAL — [Expansión, 10 Aug 2023](https://politica.expansion.mx/mexico/2023/08/10/coneval-se-duplica-la-poblacion-sin-acceso-a-salud); [Senado PAN point of agreement](https://infosen.senado.gob.mx/sgsp/gaceta/65/2/2023-08-24-1/assets/documentos/PA_PAN_legisladores_Coneval.pdf); 2020 figure from [Tlaxcala gazette](https://publicaciones.tlaxcala.gob.mx/indices/Ex27022024.pdf). The rate was 15.6% in 2016 — [México ¿cómo vamos?](https://mexicocomovamos.mx/publicaciones/2025/08/medicion-de-pobreza-2024/)
- 2024 (INEGI took over the measurement): 34.2% (44.5 million). INEGI changed the ENIGH 2024 health-access questions and response options. It no longer counts as deprived people who report access to federal or state public health centres. Under CONEVAL-consistent criteria the figure would be 44.1%. Chiapas: 63.3% — [México ¿cómo vamos?](https://mexicocomovamos.mx/publicaciones/2025/08/medicion-de-pobreza-2024/); [Expansión, 27 Aug 2025: CSOs ask INEGI to clarify](https://politica.expansion.mx/mexico/2025/08/27/organizaciones-piden-al-inegi-aclarar-cambios-en-medicion-de-pobreza-y-acceso-a-salud)
- OOP share of current health expenditure was 41.2%, with catastrophic spending at about 4.4% (2020) — [P4H Mexico profile](https://p4h.world/en/countries/mexico/)
- Measles, 2025–Feb 2026: 9,074 cases and 28 deaths (21 of them in Chihuahua) since February 2025 — [Anadolu Agency](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/americas/mexico-reports-28-measles-deaths-since-outbreak-in-2025/3827591). About 90% of cases were unvaccinated. Between 2018 and 2019 the share receiving a second measles dose "dropped almost by half" (WHO data, per [Think Global Health](https://www.thinkglobalhealth.org/article/measles-takes-root-mexico)). Only about 40–50% of Chihuahua Mennonites are vaccinated (same source). **Discrepancy:** other counts are 8,889 cases ([Mexico Business News](https://mexicobusiness.news/health/news/measles-cases-rise-8889-across-mexico)) and 5,231 at an earlier date.

**Press framing**
- "Coneval: se duplica la población sin acceso a salud" — [Expansión, 10 Aug 2023](https://politica.expansion.mx/mexico/2023/08/10/coneval-se-duplica-la-poblacion-sin-acceso-a-salud)
- "Organizaciones piden al INEGI aclarar cambios en medición de pobreza y acceso a salud" — [Expansión, 27 Aug 2025](https://politica.expansion.mx/mexico/2025/08/27/organizaciones-piden-al-inegi-aclarar-cambios-en-medicion-de-pobreza-y-acceso-a-salud)
- "US-originating measles outbreak has now reached every state in Mexico" — [Mexico News Daily](https://mexiconewsdaily.com/news/us-measles-outbreak-every-state-mexico/)

**Existing explanations and studies**
- Ending Seguro Popular and creating INSABI during early COVID reduced coverage. Catastrophic and excessive OOP rose more in uninsured households (2018–2020, ENIGH) — [JOGH 2023, 13:06044](https://jogh.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/jogh-13-06044.pdf). **Evidence: Descriptive / Partial causal**, because COVID and the reform are confounded.
- Long-run equity of OOP spending across 11 ENIGH waves (2000–2020) — Garcia-Diaz et al. — [PMC10666565](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10666565/). **Descriptive.**
- The pandemic, the abolition of Seguro Popular and OOP in 2020 (ENIGH 2014–2020) — Colchero et al., *Salud Pública de México* 2026 — [medigraphic PDF](https://www.medigraphic.com/pdfs/salpubmex/sal-2026/sal263b.pdf). **Descriptive / Partial.**
- The repeal as a natural experiment for infant mortality, comparing uninsured infants with Seguro Popular or INSABI enrollees — [AEA 2025 conference paper](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2025/program/paper/ByH9i3bT). **Partial causal (working paper).** Authors and results not visible.
- Earlier work: Seguro Popular reduced catastrophic expenditure in ENSANUT 2006 but not in ENIGH 2006 — [York HEDG WP 08/12](https://www.york.ac.uk/media/economics/documents/herc/wp/08_12.pdf).

**Why it is a puzzle:** It is a rare reversal of UHC in a middle-income country. Measured coverage halved while health spending was not cut in nominal terms (spending figures **not verified** in this session). It is unclear whether "lack of access" reflects a real loss of services or the loss of an enrolment label (Seguro Popular enrolment became meaningless once INSABI promised free care to everyone). The 2024 "recovery" is confounded by a questionnaire change. Distributional and health effects are not established.

### Inferences
- **INFERENCE (hypotheses).** (H1) The label effect: coverage loss is mostly survey semantics, and service use is unchanged. (H2) A real supply shock: centralised drug and vaccine purchasing in 2019 caused stockouts, which pushed people to private pharmacy-attached consultorios and raised OOP. The procurement story is **not verified** here; no source was found. (H3) Heterogeneity by state, depending on whether the state joined IMSS-Bienestar (federalised) or kept its own system. The adherence dates are staggered, and the list of states that did not join is **recalled, not verified**. (H4) Measles: the second-dose collapse in 2018–19 coincides with the purchasing change, while Mennonite clustering points to community hesitancy.
- **INFERENCE (discriminating variation).**
  - Staggered state adherence to IMSS-Bienestar (2022–2024) versus non-adherent states, as a DiD.
  - Pre-reform Seguro Popular enrolment intensity by municipality.
  - The ENIGH 2022→2024 question change. The 2024 microdata can be recoded to CONEVAL criteria (44.1%), so the measurement effect is directly measurable.
  - Outcomes: OOP from ENIGH; use of private pharmacy consultorios from ENSANUT; facility production from SINBA/SIS (DGIS); infant and neonatal mortality from SEED/INEGI deaths and SINAC births; vaccination from the SINAVE measles line list.
- **INFERENCE (data).** ENIGH 2016–2024 (public microdata); ENSANUT Continua 2020–2024 (public); DGIS "datos abiertos" for egresos, deaths and births (public); SINAVE notifications (aggregates public).
- **Puzzle-ness: HIGH. Research promise: HIGH.** The causal literature is just beginning (AEA 2025), so there is room but a risk of being scooped.

### Gaps
- No ENSANUT 2022/2024 OOP or catastrophic-spending figures were found.
- No source was found on the 2019 vaccine procurement or shortages.
- Total public health spending trends under INSABI were not checked.

## Puzzle 3 — The Philippines' HIV surge against the global decline

### Takeaway
UNAIDS estimates new HIV infections in the Philippines rose about 543% from 2010 to 2023. WHO cites about 4,400 (2010) to 29,600 (2024), the fastest growth in Asia-Pacific, while global infections fell. Explanations are mostly advocacy-based (parental-consent laws, local condom bans, stigma, the Church). No causal study was found.

### Cited Findings
- Estimated new infections rose 543% (2010–2023), with 33% among 15–24-year-olds — [UNAIDS Asia-Pacific, 4 Jun 2025](https://unaids-ap.org/2025/06/04/unaids-calls-for-urgent-action-as-philippines-faces-fastest-growing-hiv-epidemic-in-asia-pacific/)
- WHO: about 550% increase, from 4,400 (2010) to 29,600 (2024). The Philippines is grouped with Fiji and Papua New Guinea as having the sharpest rises — [The Star, 24 Oct 2025](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2025/10/24/who-sounds-alarm-over-sharp-increases-in-hiv-cases-in-philippines-fiji-papua-new-guinea)
- An average of 61 diagnoses a day in 2025, up 22% on 2024 — [Global Filipino Magazine](https://theglobalfilipinomagazine.com/?p=56666). Q1 2025: 5,101 new cases, +50% year on year (press, via search result). More than 5,000 cases in July–Sept 2025 — [PhilstarLife](https://philstarlife.com/amp/article/816497-doh-logs-more-than-5000-cases-hiv-q3-2025)
- About 252,800 people living with HIV (2025 estimate). Nearly 40% are undiagnosed — [Manila Bulletin, 20 Apr 2026](https://mb.com.ph/2026/04/20/how-can-the-ph-deal-with-hiv)
- In June 2025 the DOH, backed by WHO and UNAIDS, urged that HIV be declared a national public health emergency — [Manila Bulletin, 11 Jun 2025](https://mb.com.ph/2025/06/11/unaids-who-support-dohs-call-for-immediate-action-to-combat-rising-hiv-cases-in-the-philippines)
- MSM: 81% of about 35,000 cases recorded from 1984 to June 2016 — [HRW, 2016](https://www.hrw.org/report/2016/12/08/fueling-philippines-hiv-epidemic/government-barriers-condom-use-men-who-have-sex)
- Peer-reviewed epidemiology: *Sexually Transmitted Infections* 102(1):54 — [sti.bmj.com](https://sti.bmj.com/content/102/1/54) (content not read).

**Press framing:** "fastest-growing" ([UNAIDS AP](https://unaids-ap.org/2025/06/04/unaids-calls-for-urgent-action-as-philippines-faces-fastest-growing-hiv-epidemic-in-asia-pacific/)); "HIV surge amid poor sex education, policy gaps" (*Straits Times*, via [Magzter](https://www.magzter.com/hi/stories/newspaper/The-Straits-Times/HIV-SURGE-IN-PHILIPPINES-AMID-POOR-SEX-EDUCATION-POLICY-GAPS)); "500% increase among young people" ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2603198/world)).

**Existing explanations**
- Several laws and ordinances block access. Parental consent was required for minors under 18 to obtain condoms (RH Law) and HIV tests. At least two cities banned contraceptive and condom sales. School HIV education is weak. There is no MSM-targeted condom promotion. Church opposition plays a role — [HRW 2016](https://www.hrw.org/report/2016/12/08/fueling-philippines-hiv-epidemic/government-barriers-condom-use-men-who-have-sex); [Philstar 2016](https://philstar.com/headlines/2016/12/09/1651844/hrw-despite-rh-law-policies-deter-condom-use). **Evidence: Descriptive / advocacy.** HRW's account is contested by the government. The 2018 HIV/AIDS Policy Act (RA 11166) lowered the age of consent for testing to 15. **This is recalled, not verified.** If true, it is a testable policy shock.
- Low condom use: 13% among male youth in paid sex (2021 study, via the [Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/949378/condom-use-msm-aids-hiv-rontgene-solante-manilamed-hospital-anal-sex-multiple-sex-partners/amp)). **Descriptive.**

**Why it is a puzzle:** The Philippines diverges from peers. Thailand, Cambodia and Vietnam all saw falling incidence with similar regional MSM networks and income. The epidemic was "low and slow" until about 2007–2010 and then took off, and no specific shock is identified. The press offers many explanations, but none is causally tested.

### Inferences
- **INFERENCE (hypotheses).** (H1) A shift in transmission networks from heterosexual or injecting use to MSM, mediated by smartphones and apps from about 2010. (H2) Legal and supply barriers to condoms and testing for youth. (H3) Detection: much of the rise in diagnoses reflects expanded community-based screening. This cannot explain the rise in UNAIDS modelled incidence, but it does matter for HARP counts. (H4) Underfunding after the Global Fund's role declined, plus the effects of Duterte-era drug-war policing on harm reduction (Cebu PWID). **This is speculation.**
- **INFERENCE (discriminating variation).**
  - Variation across cities and provinces in local ordinances: condom bans versus pro-HIV local ordinances (e.g., Quezon City).
  - RA 11166 (2018) lowering the testing age to 15 (**not verified**), as a cohort- or age-discontinuity design on testing and diagnoses.
  - Staggered openings of PrEP and treatment hubs.
  - Regional broadband and mobile rollout timing (H1).
- **INFERENCE (data).**
  - HARP (HIV/AIDS & ART Registry of the Philippines, DOH-EB). Monthly aggregates are public; microdata are restricted.
  - IHBSS (Integrated HIV Behavioral and Serologic Surveillance) and the YAFS youth surveys (UP Population Institute, available by request).
  - NDHS for knowledge and condom-use modules.
- **Puzzle-ness: HIGH. Research promise: MEDIUM-HIGH.** The data are aggregate-heavy, and identification would rely on local policy variation.

## Puzzle 4 — Insurance expansion without out-of-pocket relief (India PM-JAY, Philippines UHC/PhilHealth, Indonesia JKN)

### Takeaway
In three large UHC expansions, OOP shares remain high or reductions are not detectable:
- **India:** enrolment in PM-JAY or other publicly funded insurance is not associated with lower OOP or catastrophic spending (Garg et al.).
- **Philippines:** households still pay about 44% of current health expenditure, more than double the UHC Act's 20% target.
- **Indonesia:** OOP remains above 30% of current health expenditure despite near-universal JKN enrolment. Studies that find JKN reductions are mostly cross-sectional.

### Cited Findings
- **India.** In Chhattisgarh (NSS 2004 and 2014, plus a 2019 primary survey), OOP and catastrophic spending did not fall with PM-JAY or earlier publicly funded insurance enrolment — Garg et al., *BMC Public Health* 2020 — [PMC7298746](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7298746). Nationally (NFHS-5, 2019–21), enrolment was not associated with lower OOP or distress financing for institutional deliveries — *BMC Research Notes* 2023 — [PDF](https://bmcresnotes.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s13104-023-06335-w.pdf). The government claims PM-JAY "saved crores of families"; critics say there are no independent evaluations and that providers upsell services not covered by the scheme — [Janata Weekly](https://janataweekly.org/articles/a-prime-ministerial-scheme-to-stop-poverty-due-to-health-crises-is-helping-poorest-indians-the-least). **Evidence: Descriptive/associational (null).** No national causal estimate was found.
- **Philippines.** OOP was 44.4% of current health expenditure in 2023 (P550.19 billion, +8.5% in pesos). Health spending was 5.9% of GDP. Government and compulsory schemes made up 42.6% — PSA via [BusinessWorld](https://www.bworldonline.com/?p=613871) and [Philstar, 25 Aug 2024](https://philstar.com/business/2024/08/25/2380286/health-care-spending-climbs-59-gdp-2023). **Discrepancy:** the 2022 figure is given as 44.6% or 44.7% depending on the outlet. The UHC Act's 20% OOP limit is cited in the [Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1675802/filipinos-shoulder-up-to-44-7-of-medical-costs-even-with-philhealth). PhilHealth targets 25–35% of current health expenditure by 2028 — [The Freeman via Magzter](https://www.magzter.com/en/stories/newspaper/The-Freeman/PHILHEALTH-REAFFIRMS-COMMITMENT-TO-UHC).
- **Indonesia.** OOP is above 30% of current health expenditure. Earlier Susenas-based studies may mismeasure JKN's OOP effect because the OOP question was added only in 2018–19 — Maulana et al. — [PMC10021284](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10021284/). Catastrophic spending fell to 0.59% of households in 2015 versus 2013 (OR 2.29 pre-JKN), from a regional-journal before-after study — [JMPK UGM](https://journal.ugm.ac.id/v3/JMPK/article/view/4479). A PSM study on older households (Susenas 2021) found OOP reductions of 63–67% — same search; source not individually linked, so **not verified**. **Evidence: Descriptive / weak causal.**

**Press framing:** "Filipinos shoulder up to 44.7% of medical costs even with PhilHealth" — [Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1675802/filipinos-shoulder-up-to-44-7-of-medical-costs-even-with-philhealth); "Households hit hardest by healthcare costs" — [Manila Bulletin, 17 Aug 2024](https://mb.com.ph/2024/8/17/households-hit-hardest-by-healthcare-costs); "A 30-year failure" — [Inquirer Opinion](https://opinion.inquirer.net/?p=175991).

**Why it is a puzzle:** Standard theory says insurance lowers OOP at the point of use. Here, the aggregate OOP share is sticky. Possible reasons:
- Supply-induced demand and balance billing in private hospitals.
- Outpatient drugs are not covered: PM-JAY covers hospitalisation only, and PhilHealth outpatient benefits were limited until the Konsulta package (Konsulta details **not verified**).
- Moral hazard raises total spending.
- Low awareness or take-up.

The relative weight of each is unknown.

### Inferences
- **INFERENCE (hypotheses).** (H1) Benefit-design gap: OOP is mostly outpatient medicines, which the schemes exclude. (H2) Extensive margin: insurance induces new utilisation, so OOP per episode falls while total OOP rises. (H3) Provider capture: empanelled private hospitals add unpaid charges. (H4) Take-up frictions: eligible people do not know they are enrolled.
- **INFERENCE (discriminating variation).**
  - India: staggered state adoption and the states that opted out (e.g., Odisha, Delhi and West Bengal for some period; **recalled, not verified**), plus the SECC-2011 deprivation eligibility cutoffs as an RD.
  - Philippines: the Konsulta rollout timing and the 2023–24 PhilHealth benefit-package expansions (**not verified**).
  - Indonesia: the PBI (subsidised) eligibility threshold in the DTKS/Unified Database.
- **INFERENCE (data).**
  - India: NSS 75th round health (2017–18), NFHS-4/5, Household Consumption Expenditure Survey 2022–23 (public); PM-JAY claims are restricted (NHA).
  - Philippines: FIES (PSA, public), the National Health Accounts, PhilHealth claims (restricted).
  - Indonesia: Susenas (BPS, purchase), IFLS, BPJS Kesehatan sample data (available on request; **not verified**).
- **Puzzle-ness: MEDIUM (the fact is known in health policy circles). Research promise: MEDIUM-HIGH,** especially for India's PM-JAY, where clean causal work is scarce.

### Gaps
- No national causal PM-JAY evaluation was found, and no NSS post-2018 health round results.
- Peer-reviewed analysis of the Philippine OOP decomposition was not found.

## Puzzle 5 — Brazil's childhood-vaccination decline beginning about 2015–16, before COVID

### Takeaway
Brazil's childhood vaccination coverage fell from about 96% (2015) to 84% (2017) according to one aggregate series, and further during COVID. One child-under-2 series falls from 88.85% (2015) to 62.35% (2021). Brazil's PNI had been a global model, and the drop began before COVID and before anti-vaccine politics became salient. Explanations range from hesitancy to deprivation to data artefacts (denominators, unregistered private-clinic doses). No study cleanly separates real decline from measurement.

### Cited Findings
- Coverage: 96.3% (2015), 89.3% (2016), 83.8% (2017) — cited in [PMC8594206](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8594206). A time series for children under 2 gives 88.85% (2015) and 62.35% (2021) — [PMC11146285](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11146285/). **Discrepancy:** the two series differ in vaccine basket and age group.
- The decline began around 2012 and steepened from 2016 — [RESS 2024](https://scielosp.org/article/ress/2024.v33nspe2/e2023539)
- MMR first-dose coverage fell 17% between 2015 and 2020 in the more deprived North and Northeast. Declines were steeper in deprived municipalities (municipal ecological study) — [LSHTM Research Online 4676004](https://researchonline.lshtm.ac.uk/id/eprint/4676004)
- Data quality: the SI-PNI denominator relies on intercensal population estimates. Private-clinic doses are under-reported, and some local systems are not integrated with SI-PNI. Survey coverage (ICV 2020) differs from SI-PNI in São Paulo and Campinas — [RESS 2024](https://scielosp.org/article/ress/2024.v33nspe2/e2023539); [RESS 2023](https://scielosp.org/pdf/ress/2023.v32n3/e2023117/en)
- FAPESP feature: the pandemic is "unlikely to be the only contributor" — [Revista Pesquisa FAPESP](https://revistapesquisa.fapesp.br/en/a-drop-in-childhood-vaccination/)
- Measles was re-established after the 2018 outbreaks in Roraima and Amazonas, linked to Venezuelan migration (same literature: [PMC8594206](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8594206)).

**Existing explanations and evidence quality:**
- Hesitancy: Speculation/Descriptive.
- Deprivation: Descriptive (ecological).
- COVID: Descriptive.
- Data artefact (SI-PNI's move to individual nominal registration around 2013–2017 and denominator error): Speculation. I found **no source** attributing the 2016 drop to an information-system change, although the papers flag denominator problems.

**Why it is a puzzle:** Coverage was very high and stable for decades. The fall started in 2015–16, which is not explained by the pandemic or by documented hesitancy surges at that date, and it is steepest in poor areas. That pattern sits oddly with a hesitancy story, which usually concentrates in educated and wealthier groups.

### Inferences
- **INFERENCE (hypotheses).** (H1) Measurement: the transition to individual-level registration (SI-PNI web) under-counted doses during roll-out, and the denominator from SINASC and IBGE projections was overstated before the 2022 census. (H2) Primary-care supply: Mais Médicos exits (Cuban doctors left in Nov 2018; **date from background knowledge**), vaccine stockouts, and opening hours of salas de vacina. (H3) Recession and austerity after 2015 (constitutional amendment EC 95/2016 spending cap; **background, not verified here**). (H4) Changes to Bolsa Família conditionality monitoring.
- **INFERENCE (discriminating variation).**
  - Municipal timing of SI-PNI nominal-system adoption (H1). The 2022 Census population revision provides a denominator correction test.
  - Cuban doctor exit intensity by municipality in Nov 2018 (H2).
  - Bolsa Família conditionality-monitoring coverage (H4).
  - Comparing SINASC-cohort-based coverage with administrative dose counts.
- **INFERENCE (data).** SI-PNI (DATASUS, public aggregates; microdata via the RNDS/OpenDataSUS COVID-era release), SINASC, CNES, the ICV 2020 national coverage survey (restricted/by request), CadÚnico/Bolsa Família health-conditionality files (restricted).
- **Puzzle-ness: MEDIUM-HIGH. Research promise: MEDIUM-HIGH,** with a clear measurement-vs-real research design.

### Gaps
- No evidence was found on the SI-PNI system change as a cause, so this is unresolved.
- No study was found linking Bolsa Família beneficiaries to the 2015 decline.

## Puzzle 6 — Tuberculosis rising in Latin America while it falls globally

### Takeaway
According to a 2024 *Lancet Public Health* modelling paper, Latin American TB incidence rose 19% from 2015 while global incidence fell 8.7%. The same model attributes about 30% of TB among people over 15 in six countries to incarceration growth. This is a strong partial explanation, but it rests on a model.

### Cited Findings
- Global TB incidence fell 8.7% since 2015, while Latin American incidence rose 19%. The six-country model (Argentina, Brazil, Colombia, El Salvador, Mexico, Peru; 1990–2023) estimates incarceration's population attributable fraction in 2019 at about 36.9% for Brazil and 23.3% for Peru. Excess cases relative to no rise in incarceration since 1990: about 23,497 (Brazil) and 4,922 (Peru). A 50% cut in admissions and duration would lower incidence by more than 10% in all countries except Mexico — Liu, Andrews et al., *Lancet Public Health* 2024 — [MIDAS summary](https://midasnetwork.us/papers/mass-incarceration-as-a-driver-of-the-tuberculosis-epidemic-in-latin-america-and-projected-effects-of-policy-alternatives-a-mathematical-modelling-study/); [Stanford FSI](https://fsi.stanford.edu/node/36963); [Fiocruz](https://fiocruz.br/node/190171); table [PMC11602220](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11602220/table/T2). **Evidence: Partial causal (model-based).**
- Press: "Encarceramento é principal causa de tuberculose na América Latina" — [Agência Brasil, Oct 2024](https://agenciabrasil.ebc.com.br/saude/noticia/2024-10/encarceramento-e-principal-causa-de-tuberculose-na-america-latina); [O Povo, 15 Oct 2024](https://www.opovo.com.br/noticias/saude/2024/10/15/encarceramento-e-principal-causa-de-tuberculose-na-america-latina.html)
- Prison TB crisis review — [Amsterdam UMC PDF](https://pure.amsterdamumc.nl/ws/files/217636969/The-escalating-tuberculosis-crisis-in-central-and-south-american-prisons.pdf)

**Why it is (still) a puzzle:** Incarceration explains a share, but the model leaves about two-thirds unexplained. Countries in Asia (Indonesia, the Philippines) also stagnate without comparable prison growth. **Their trends were not checked in this session.**

### Inferences
- **INFERENCE (hypotheses).** Incarceration, the effect of the Bolsa Família and SUS austerity era on treatment completion, diabetes comorbidity, migration (Venezuelan flows into Peru and Brazil), and detection changes (GeneXpert rollout) inflating notifications.
- **INFERENCE (discriminating variation).** Prison-level openings and overcrowding shocks, through spillovers to surrounding municipalities. Brazil's 2006 drug law (Lei 11.343) as an incarceration shock (**background, not verified here**). Staggered GeneXpert municipal rollout from 2014 (**not verified**).
- **INFERENCE (data).** Brazil: SINAN-TB (public microdata, which includes a "population deprived of liberty" flag; **recalled, not verified**) and SISDEPEN prison statistics (public). Peru: SIGTB (restricted) and INPE prison census.
- **Puzzle-ness: MEDIUM. Research promise: MEDIUM-HIGH** for quasi-experimental tests of the model's mechanism.

### Gaps
- WHO Global TB Report country series for Peru, Indonesia and the Philippines were not retrieved.

## Puzzle 7 — Chile's HIV rise, a high-income outlier in Latin America

### Takeaway
Chile had the largest percentage increase in new HIV infections in Latin America from 2010 to 2018: +34% by UNAIDS, and +66% in Ministry of Health counts according to advocates. That is despite high income and universal ART. Migration explains part, as migrant incidence rose from 22.6 to 115.6 per 100,000 in 2013–2022. Rising Chilean-born incidence, and the mix of detection and true transmission, are less explained.

### Cited Findings
- Chile had the highest percentage rise in new infections in Latin America since 2010 — [UNAIDS 2019 Global AIDS Update, LatAm](https://www.unaids.org/sites/default/files/media_asset/2019-global-AIDS-update_latin-america_en.pdf). +34% (2010–2018) per UNAIDS — [Meganoticias](https://www.meganoticias.cl/nacional/284006-vih-aumento-onusida-minsal-jaime-manalich.html). An advocate says Health Department data show +66% — [WTOP/AP, Sept 2017](https://wtop.com/news/2017/09/chile-battles-an-alarmingly-high-rate-of-new-hiv-infections)
- Adjusted incidence among international migrants rose from 22.6 to 115.6 per 100,000 (2013–2022). The migrant-to-Chilean incidence ratio rose from 1.0 to 7.6 (*Journal of Migration and Health*) — [PMC11759548 PDF](https://rcastoragev2.blob.core.windows.net/3d65a77c8a38bdb45f43df4ae080af0f/main.PMC11759548.pdf)
- A 2024 review cites a 35% increase in 10 years, deficits in prevention policy and migration — [BVS biblio-1559680](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/gim/resource/ar/biblio-1559680)
- Newer counts: 5,401 cases are attributed to 2022 or 2023 depending on the source — **discrepancy** ([El Dínamo 2023](https://www.eldinamo.cl/pais/2023/08/03/la-explicacion-detras-del-aumento-de-casos-de-vih-y-la-desbordada-situacion-de-la-sifilis-en-chile/); [Prensa Latina](https://www.plenglish.com/?p=205212)). A 2024 report says HIV figures are decreasing while other STIs rise — [El Austral de Temuco, 5 Sep 2024](https://www.australtemuco.cl/impresa/2024/09/05/full/cuerpo-principal/5)

**Existing explanations and evidence quality:**
- Migration: Descriptive (cohort/surveillance).
- Prevention-policy deficits: Speculation.
- Expanded testing (the 2018 rapid-test rollout; **not verified**): Speculation.

**Why it is a puzzle:** This is a high-income, universal-ART country going against its regional peers. Migrant incidence may reflect infections acquired in Chile after arrival, which would be a health-system access puzzle, rather than imported prevalence. That split is not resolved.

### Inferences
- **INFERENCE (hypotheses and variation).**
  - Infection acquired after arrival versus imported. Test this with CD4 at diagnosis by time since arrival, and with the timing of migration waves across comunas.
  - Migrants' access frictions in FONASA, using regularisation episodes (2018 and 2021 processes; **not verified**).
- **INFERENCE (data).** Minsal/ISP notification microdata (by request), DEIS, the Censo 2017 migrant population, and the Servicio Nacional de Migraciones permit data.
- **Puzzle-ness: MEDIUM. Research promise: MEDIUM.**

### Gaps
- No UNAIDS post-2019 Chile incidence series was retrieved, and recent trends are possibly reversing.

## Puzzle 8 — Dengue's record year in the Americas and Argentina (2024), then a sharp drop

### Takeaway
2024 set records: 11.7 million cases in the Americas by week 36, 2.5× all of 2023. Argentina had about 577,000–581,000 confirmed cases. A sharp drop followed in 2025. Climate (heat, El Niño) is the dominant explanation and is well researched elsewhere. The leftover puzzles are the temperate expansion and the boom-bust pattern, including the role of serotype immunity.

### Cited Findings
- 11,732,921 cases by EW36 of 2024 (47 countries and territories), versus 4,594,823 in all of 2023. Cases in the first half of 2024 exceeded any previous full year — [PAHO alert, 7 Oct 2024](https://www.paho.org/en/documents/epidemiological-alert-increase-dengue-cases-americas-region-7-october-2024); [PAHO news, 8 Oct 2024](https://www.paho.org/en/news/8-10-2024-record-year-dengue-cases-paho-urges-countries-strengthen-response-seasonal). A "13 million" figure was **not found**.
- Argentina: 576,728 cases by EW39 of 2024 ([The Star, 8 Oct 2024](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2024/10/08/dengue-cases-in-argentina-surpass-576000-in-2024)). 581,402 confirmed out of 771,818 notified by EW48 (ministry, via search snippet). The national peak was the week of 17 March 2024, with 55,476 cases. Early 2025 counts were below 2024 levels in all jurisdictions ([Los Andes](https://www.losandes.com.ar/sociedad/desde-salud-afirman-que-los-casos-de-dengue-estan-por-debajo-de-los-registrados-en-el-mismo-periodo-del-2024)). The full 2024/25 season is ambiguous: 77,045 suspected cases by EW29 of 2025 ([Outbreak News Today](https://outbreaknewstoday.substack.com/p/argentina-dengue-in-the-2024-2025)).
- The *Guardian* links the 2024 spike to record temperatures (via [PressReader, 13 Apr 2024](https://www.pressreader.com/usa/the-guardian-usa/20240413/281711209686195)).
- Argentina's "change in epidemiological pattern," per Fernán Quirós — [El Cronista](https://www.cronista.com/informacion-gral/dengue-el-cambio-de-patente-epidemiologica-que-pone-en-alerta-al-sistema-de-salud-segun-fernan-quiros/)

**Existing explanations:** Climate and El Niño: Partial causal to well explained in the epidemiology literature (not searched in depth). Serotype shifts (DENV-3 re-emergence in Brazil; **not verified**): Descriptive.

**Why it is a (weaker) puzzle:** The causal climate literature is extensive, so the remaining puzzle is mainly the size of the 2025 collapse and municipal-level heterogeneity.

### Inferences
- **INFERENCE.** The applied-micro angle is the economic and health-system response, such as the TAK-003 (Qdenga) vaccine rollout. Brazil's SUS added it in Feb 2024 for selected municipalities (**background, not verified**), which creates eligibility discontinuities. Use SINAN-dengue (Brazil, public microdata) and Argentina's SNVS.
- **Puzzle-ness: LOW-MEDIUM. Research promise: MEDIUM** (vaccine-rollout evaluation).

### Gaps
- Brazil's 2024 national dengue totals were not retrieved in this session.

## Discarded or well-explained seeds, related items, and search log

### Takeaway
Thailand's UCS is well explained, so it is discarded. Venezuela's malaria resurgence is substantially explained (health-system collapse plus gold mining). Mais Médicos is well studied with modest and heterogeneous effects. The Philippines' Dengvaxia confidence collapse is descriptively well documented. Its causal link to the 2019 measles epidemic is less established, and it could be folded into Puzzle 5 as a comparator. Cholera re-emergence and AMR hotspots were **not researched** for lack of tool budget.

### Cited Findings
- **Thailand 30 Baht / UCS: Well explained → discard.** The 2001 reform raised utilisation among the poor. It reduced their infant mortality by at least 6.5 per 1,000 births (about 2 per 1,000 nationally) and removed the poor-rich provincial IMR gradient, with placebo checks — Gruber, Hendren & Townsend, *AEJ: Applied* 6(1):91–107, 2014 — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/aea/aejapp/v6y2014i1p91-107.html); [NBER w17739](https://www.nber.org/papers/w17739.pdf)
- **Brazil Mais Médicos: studied → low puzzle value.** DiD on 2007–2018 municipal data found no aggregate effect on infant or neonatal mortality. There was a reduction (−0.21; 95% CI −0.38 to −0.03) in municipalities with the highest pre-programme IMR — [PMC8363855](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8363855/). Fontes et al. found fewer ambulatory-care-sensitive admissions (via [PMC7491024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7491024)). **Evidence: Partial causal / well studied.**
- **Venezuela malaria: substantially explained.** +359% in cases from 2000 to 2015, with a further rise in 2017. Imported cases into Brazil rose from 1,538 (2014) to 3,129 (2017) — Grillet et al., *Lancet Infectious Diseases* 2019 ([LSHTM](https://researchonline.lshtm.ac.uk/id/eprint/4654956)). Miners account for 47–80% of cases in some municipalities ([PLoS NTD 2021](https://0-doi-org.brum.beds.ac.uk/10.1371/journal.pntd.0008211)). **Evidence: Partial causal → discard as a "puzzle".**
- **Philippines Dengvaxia and vaccine confidence: descriptively explained.**
  - Strong agreement that vaccines are safe fell from 82% (2015) to 21% (2018). Importance fell from 93% to 32%, and effectiveness from 82% to 22%. Larson et al. attribute this to a politicised response — [Philstar, 13 Oct 2018](https://philstar.com/headlines/2018/10/13/1859769/philippines-highly-politicized-response-dengvaxia-mess-erodes-public-trust-vaccines-study); [LSHTM 4649821](https://researchonline.lshtm.ac.uk/id/eprint/4649821). **The publishing journal is inconsistently reported** (*Human Vaccines & Immunotherapeutics* vs *Int J Technol Assess Health Care*).

  - **INFERENCE:** the large 2018 case count suggests immunity gaps built up before the Dengvaxia announcement (Nov 2017). The attribution of the 2019 epidemic to Dengvaxia is therefore under-identified, which leaves a residual research question. Pre-2017 MCV coverage trends were **not verified**.
- **Not researched (gaps):** cholera re-emergence (Haiti 2010 origin is well explained by UN-peacekeeper introduction (**background, not verified here**); Malawi 2022–23, Zambia, Sudan, Yemen); AMR hotspots; HIV in Eastern Europe (Russia and Ukraine are not typically "developing"; skipped); Paraguay syphilis; Peru, Indonesia and Philippines TB country series.
