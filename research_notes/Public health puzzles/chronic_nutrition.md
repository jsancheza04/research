## Puzzle 1 — India's anaemia among women rose between NFHS-4 and NFHS-5 while every standard determinant improved; NFHS-6 (2026) then omitted anaemia

### Takeaway
Anaemia among Indian women 15–49 rose from about 53% (NFHS-4, 2015–16) to about 57% (NFHS-5, 2019–21). Over the same period sanitation, women's empowerment, prenatal care, iron-folic acid (IFA) uptake and iron-rich food intake all improved. In May 2026 the NFHS-6 fact sheets left out haemoglobin entirely, citing concerns about the capillary method. Whether the rise is real or an artefact of measurement is still unresolved. **Puzzle-ness: HIGH. Research promise: HIGH.**

### Cited Findings
- Women's anaemia went from about 53% (NFHS-4) to about 57% (NFHS-5). The rise came despite wider distribution of IFA tablets to pregnant women — [The Quint](https://www.thequint.com/opinion/anaemia-in-india-among-women-children-on-a-rise); [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/news/health/anaemia-in-women-and-children-on-the-rise-assam-fares-worst-nfhs-5-80357)
- Water and sanitation, women's empowerment, folic-acid intake in pregnancy, prenatal-care access, and the share of women and children eating iron-rich food groups all improved between NFHS-4 and NFHS-5. Commentary therefore calls the rise a "mystery" — [IASbaba summary, "India's great anaemia mystery"](https://iasbaba.com/2022/09/indias-great-anaemia-mystery/) (secondary aggregator of an op-ed); [Deccan Herald op-ed](https://www.deccanherald.com/opinion/why-are-women-and-children-in-india-becoming-more-anaemic-1126584.html); [ThePrint op-ed](https://theprint.in/opinion/how-is-indias-economy-affecting-health-look-at-rise-in-anaemia-among-women/572904)
- The change was very uneven across states: 22 states/UTs reported increases in women's anaemia and 12 reported declines of at least 1 percentage point. Assam had the largest rise in one dataset — [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/news/health/anaemia-in-women-and-children-on-the-rise-assam-fares-worst-nfhs-5-80357); [Factly NFHS-5 data](https://factly.in/data-nfhs-5)
- Measurement issue: a paired-sample study of about 1,000 women in Uttar Pradesh found capillary Hb about 1 g/dL lower than venous Hb. Anaemia prevalence was 59.2% by capillary vs 35.2% by venous sample — [Indian Pediatrics, Nov 2022](https://indianpediatrics.net/nov2022/837.pdf)
- A multi-institution study led by Anura Kurpad reported venous-based anaemia prevalence well below NFHS figures in eight states — [Science Chronicle, 2025-05-05](https://sciencechronicle.in/2025/05/05/anaemia-prevalence-in-eight-states-found-to-be-overestimated/)
- The NFHS-6 fact sheets (released 2026-05-29) omitted haemoglobin and anaemia. The Health Ministry pointed to concerns about the capillary method and said anaemia estimates would come from ICMR's Diet and Biomarkers Survey (DABS). It also said the fact sheets are not the final report — [The Wire](https://thewire.in/health/nfhs-key-indicators-missing-government-clarifies)
- Counter-view: dropping anaemia is a mistake, because NFHS was the only source of district-level anaemia data. NFHS-5 already carried a footnote warning against comparing it with venous surveys — [The Quint, "Dropping anaemia questions from NFHS-6 a mistake"](https://www.thequint.com/fit/citizen-fit/anaemia-nfhs-6-questions-dropped-a-mistake)
- Press framing: "Anaemia in women and children on the rise, Assam fares worst: NFHS-5" — [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/news/health/anaemia-in-women-and-children-on-the-rise-assam-fares-worst-nfhs-5-80357) (date not captured); "NFHS key indicators missing; government clarifies" — [The Wire](https://thewire.in/health/nfhs-key-indicators-missing-government-clarifies) (2026)

**Existing explanations and evidence quality**
- Capillary vs venous measurement bias (Kurpad et al.; Indian Pediatrics 2022 study) — **Partial causal / Descriptive** for the *level*. A bias in the level does not by itself explain a *rise* between two rounds that both used capillary methods, unless field protocols, devices or sampling changed between rounds (not verified).
- Economic distress after demonetisation and GST, affecting diet quality (ThePrint op-ed) — **Speculation**
- Changes in survey methodology between rounds (Quint commentary) — **Speculation** (details not confirmed)

### Inferences (INFERENCE)
- Candidate hypotheses: (a) round-specific changes in measurement such as HemoCue model, cuvettes, enumerator training, or ambient temperature during fieldwork (NFHS-5 Phase 2 was fielded during COVID); (b) a real decline in diet quality or iron bioavailability, for example a PDS shift toward rice/wheat and away from coarse grains, or rising tea consumption; (c) other causes of anaemia unrelated to iron, such as haemoglobinopathies, inflammation and air pollution; (d) changes in sample composition.
- Discriminating variation: NFHS-5 was fielded in two phases (pre-COVID 2019 and 2020–21), which allows within-state comparisons across phases. District-level roll-out of fortified rice in the PDS (from 2019 pilots, scaled 2021–24) is a candidate design. Enumerator/team fixed effects in the DHS microdata can test for measurement artefacts. Men's anaemia serves as a placebo for women-specific mechanisms.
- Data: NFHS-4/5 microdata (public via DHS Program); NFHS-6 microdata when released; DABS (restricted, ICMR); the CNNS 2016–18 survey, which used venous samples (public on request — not verified).

### Gaps
- I could not verify in the search what exactly changed in haemoglobin protocols between NFHS-4 and NFHS-5.
- I found no peer-reviewed decomposition of the NFHS-4 to NFHS-5 rise (none appeared in the search).
- NFHS-6 anaemia status beyond the fact sheets is unclear.

## Puzzle 2 — Colombia: CKD counts in the high-cost registry jumped 20–26% a year while recorded diabetes control fell. Real epidemic, reporting incentives, or health-system collapse?

### Takeaway
The Cuenta de Alto Costo (CAC) registry shows CKD cases rising from 790,117 (2022) to 1,251,930 (2024, +26.3%) to 1,502,681 (2025, +20.0%). Audits flagged 580,517 inconsistencies in the supporting diagnoses for CKD, hypertension and diabetes. Over the same period the share of diabetics who are "controlled and monitored" fell from 30.9% (2022) to 24.2% (2023) to 23.1% (2024). A true epidemic cannot plausibly grow this fast. These years coincide with the financial crisis and state interventions of the EPS insurers. **Puzzle-ness: HIGH. Research promise: HIGH** (administrative data, policy shocks, incentive design).

### Cited Findings
- CKD: 1,251,930 people in 2024, +26.3% over the prior period (CAC, via El Tiempo) — [El Tiempo](https://www.eltiempo.com/mas-contenido/davita-cumple-una-decada-llevando-cuidado-renal-a-mas-colombianos-3576170)
- CKD: 1,502,681 reported cases in 2025, +20.03% year on year. Audits identified 580,517 inconsistencies in the documentation behind CKD, hypertension and diabetes diagnoses — [Consultorsalud](https://consultorsalud.com/?p=344637)
- 2024 partial-year figure (cut-off October): 1,014,594 diagnosed, +25.45%. This conflicts with the full-year 1.25M figure because it covers a different period — [El Universal, 2025-04-25](https://www.eluniversal.com.co/colombia/2025/04/25/insuficiencia-renal-en-colombia-una-amenaza-silenciosa-y-de-alto-costo/)
- 2022: 790,117 people with diagnosed CKD. The figure of about 5 million "true" cases rests on assuming 10% prevalence, so it is illustrative only — [El Colombiano](https://www.elcolombiano.com/tendencias/como-cuidar-los-rinones-dia-mundial-del-rinon-KP23973584)
- The share of diabetes "controlled and monitored" (HbA1c <7%) was 30.9% in 2022, 24.2% in 2023 and 23.1% in 2024, below the 30% target (ACEMI bulletin, via Consultorsalud) — [Consultorsalud](https://consultorsalud.com/enfermedades-endocrinas-colombia/)
- Hypertension (CAC 2025 report): 44% of hypertensives do not know their diagnosis, and only 23% of those diagnosed are controlled — [Consultorsalud](https://consultorsalud.com/riesgo-cardiovascular-colombia-millones-hta-d/)
- Local example (Cali, Red de Salud Ladera, 2023–24): 34.8% of hypertensives and 56% of diabetics not adequately controlled. Small, non-representative sample — same Consultorsalud search results ([UTP Revista Médica](https://revistas.utp.edu.co/index.php/revistamedica/article/download/25861/17783/92823))
- Press framing: "Enfermedad renal crónica en Colombia: casos reportados aumentaron 20% y superaron 1,5 millones en 2025" — [Consultorsalud](https://consultorsalud.com/?p=344637); "Insuficiencia renal en Colombia, una amenaza silenciosa y de alto costo" — [El Universal, 2025-04-25](https://www.eluniversal.com.co/colombia/2025/04/25/insuficiencia-renal-en-colombia-una-amenaza-silenciosa-y-de-alto-costo/)

**Existing explanations and evidence quality**
- Better case-finding and screening (implicit in CAC communications) — **Speculation**
- Underlying diabetes and hypertension, plus ageing — **Descriptive** (the standard epidemiological narrative; it cannot generate +20–26% a year)
- Reporting inconsistencies (CAC audits) — **Descriptive**, and acknowledged by the CAC itself
- I found no study linking the decline in control to the EPS crisis (searched; none found).

### Inferences (INFERENCE)
- Hypotheses: (a) **reporting incentives**. The CAC runs a risk-adjustment/redistribution mechanism among EPSs based on reported high-cost cases (*recalled, not verified* for CKD stages specifically), so insurers gain from reporting more, earlier-stage cases. (b) Reclassification toward early CKD stages 1–2 through eGFR/albuminuria screening mandates. (c) Disruption of care after 2023–24, with EPS interventions (Nueva EPS, Sanitas and others — *recalled, not verified* as to exact dates), unpaid IPS networks and medicine shortages lowering monitoring and control. (d) Population mobility between EPSs causing double counting.
- Discriminating variation: stage composition of the new cases (stage 5 dialysis counts are hard to inflate; stages 1–2 are easy). EPS-level timing of government interventions as an event study on control and on reporting. A placebo using outcomes hard to manipulate (dialysis starts, CKD deaths in DANE vital statistics). Comparing the contributory and subsidised regimes.
- Data: CAC public reports and aggregated datasets by EPS and department (public); SISPRO/RIPS (restricted via MinSalud, partial public cubes); DANE EEVV mortality microdata (public); Supersalud intervention dates (public).

### Gaps
- I could not access the CAC 2024/2025 reports directly (search-only), so the stage breakdown is unknown.
- Whether the decline in control reflects fewer HbA1c tests (a monitoring denominator problem) or worse true control is unknown.
- The exact design of the CAC redistribution mechanism for CKD is not verified.

## Puzzle 3 — Colombia's 2023 UPF and sugary-drink tax plus Ley 2120 warning labels: early consumption drops, revenue below projections, ongoing "high consumption" concern, and almost no rigorous evaluation

### Takeaway
Colombia implemented one of the world's broadest UPF taxes. Ley 2277/2022 set an ad valorem tax on UPFs of 10% (from November 2023), 15% (2024) and 20% (2025), followed by UVT indexation. A per-unit tax on sugary drinks applied alongside it. Octagonal warning labels under Ley 2120/2021 came at roughly the same time. Market-research snapshots show 5–20% sales drops in the first months, and revenue came in below official projections. Yet by May 2026 the press still frames consumption as "worrying". The only rigorous ex post study I found is on labour-market effects (null). I found no peer-reviewed study of purchases or intake. **Puzzle-ness: MEDIUM. Research promise: HIGH** (new policy, rich scanner and survey data, staggered rates).

### Cited Findings
- Rate schedule 10% → 15% (2024) → 20% (2025) under Ley 2277/2022 — [Portafolio](https://www.portafolio.co/economia/impuestos/impuesto-a-los-ultraprocesados-en-colombia-impacta-a-los-consumidores-621034); [Forbes Colombia, 2025-01-05](https://forbes.co/2025/01/05/actualidad/estos-alimentos-subiran-de-precio-este-ano-por-impuesto-a-ultraprocesados-que-quedo-en-20/). **Discrepancy:** [Valora Analitik](https://www.valoraanalitik.com/impuestos-colombia-galguerias-gaseosas-aumeto-precio/) says 10% in 2024, 15% in 2025 and 20% in 2026. This conflicts with the other sources and probably refers to a different product group or is an error. Check the statute.
- Kantar observed sales-volume declines of 5–20% in the first two months of implementation — [El Tiempo](https://www.eltiempo.com/economia/sectores/alimentos-y-bebidas-que-mas-se-han-dejado-de-comprar-por-impuesto-a-ultraprocesados-3327986); [Portafolio](https://www.portafolio.co/negocios/comercio/el-efecto-que-han-tenido-los-impuestos-saludables-en-la-dinamica-de-consumo-601719)
- A report cited by El Tiempo concluded the taxes discourage consumption but are not an "efficient" solution — [El Tiempo](https://www.eltiempo.com/economia/sectores/se-ha-reducido-el-consumo-de-las-bebidas-azucaradas-y-de-los-alimentos-ultraprocesados-con-nuevo-impuesto-3474414). Anif judged it too early to determine the effect on consumption — [El Colombiano](https://www.elcolombiano.com/negocios/impuestos-saludables-colombia-anif-cambios-consumo-recaudo-GN28252136)
- Revenue: by May 2025, COP 1.6 trillion in total healthy-tax revenue (COP 288bn from SSBs and COP 1.3tn from UPFs). Initial projections were COP 3.8tn for 2025 and 4.1tn for 2026 — [El Tiempo](https://www.eltiempo.com/economia/sectores/se-ha-reducido-el-consumo-de-las-bebidas-azucaradas-y-de-los-alimentos-ultraprocesados-con-nuevo-impuesto-3474414). By May 2026, COP 1.9tn from the UPF tax "amid concern over high consumption of junk food" — [El Colombiano, 2026-05-20](https://www.elcolombiano.com/negocios/impuesto-ultraprocesados-recaudo-colombia-consumo-comida-chatarra-2026-HE36763499); [Portafolio](https://www.portafolio.co/negocios/empresas/impuesto-a-ultraprocesados-ya-recauda-1-9-billones-en-colombia-494548). **Discrepancy:** reported totals and periods do not reconcile. The 1.9tn may be UPF-only or cumulative, and El Tiempo cites 2.9tn for 2024 in total.
- MinHacienda estimated the inflation effect at 0.21 pp (2023), 0.11 pp (2024) and 0.12 pp (2025). The tax covers 21 of the 443 items in the CPI basket — [Cambio](https://cambiocolombia.com/economia/impuesto-los-alimentos-ultraprocesados-subiria-la-inflacion-021-por-ciento-segun); [El Heraldo, 2023-10-25](https://www.elheraldo.co/colombia/2023/10/25/minhacienda-indico-que-el-impuesto-saludable-afectara-a-21-de-los-443-productos-de-la-canasta-basica-1043823/)
- Ex ante label design: in an online RCT (n = 1,997), octagon warnings lowered intention to purchase (mean 2.59 → 1.99–2.01) and worked similarly across education levels (Mora-Plazas, Taillie et al., Nutrients 2020) — stated intentions only — [MDPI Nutrients 12(10):3124](https://mdpi.com/2072-6643/12/10/3124)
- Ex post evaluation: a 2025 working paper (Taillie a co-author) found no significant short-run effects on employment, income or informality from the combined tax and label reforms (ITS, DiD and synthetic control on GEIH) — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/p/col/000092/022160.html)
- Timeline: Ley 2120 passed July 2021; the tax took effect November 2023 — [Global Food Research Program](https://www.globalfoodresearchprogram.org/?p=170)

**Existing explanations and evidence quality**
- Industry and market-research snapshots — **Descriptive** (short window, no counterfactual)
- Labour effects — **Partial causal** (null)
- Purchases and intake — **None** peer-reviewed found

### Why it is a puzzle
Chile's labelling law (−23.7% in "high-in" beverage volume) and Mexico's SSB tax (−6%, rising in year 2) are well studied. Colombia combines a *broad UPF ad valorem tax* with labels, which is rare worldwide. Its effects on purchases, substitution toward informal or untaxed products (tiendas, artisanal goods, contraband) and reformulation remain unmeasured. The revenue shortfall against projections is consistent with a large demand response, with avoidance or reformulation below label thresholds, or with weak enforcement. These mechanisms have opposite welfare implications.

### Inferences (INFERENCE)
- Hypotheses: (a) reformulation to fall under the warning-label thresholds that define the taxable base (*not verified* that the base is tied to label thresholds; check Ley 2277 art. 54); (b) substitution toward untaxed artisanal or informal snacks; (c) pass-through below 100% in tiendas de barrio; (d) inflation in 2022–23 masking the relative-price effect.
- Discriminating variation: the staggered rate path (10/15/20%), tax incidence across product codes (taxed vs untaxed close substitutes), and the different timing of labels vs tax. Pass-through can be measured in DANE CPI micro-prices. Kantar or NielsenIQ household panels can show purchases. EMC/EMMET give retail and manufacturing output.
- Data: DANE CPI micro-prices (restricted/sala de procesamiento), EMC retail survey (public aggregates), Kantar Worldpanel Colombia (proprietary; UNC GFRP holds access, *not verified*), DIAN tax revenue by item (public aggregates), ENSIN 2015 baseline.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed evaluation of purchases or intake (search-only; there may be unindexed 2025–26 preprints).
- The exact taxable-base rule (whether it uses label nutrient thresholds) is not verified.
- The revenue figures conflict across press outlets.

## Puzzle 4 — Rural BMI rising faster than urban BMI in LMICs (NCD-RisC 2019)

### Takeaway
Against the "urbanisation causes obesity" narrative, more than 55% of the global rise in mean BMI from 1985 to 2017 came from rural areas, and more than 80% in some LMIC regions. Rural mean BMI rose about 2.1 kg/m², compared with about 1.3 (urban women) and 1.6 (urban men). Sub-Saharan African women are the exception. The pattern is well documented, but the mechanisms are barely tested causally. **Puzzle-ness: MEDIUM-HIGH. Research promise: MEDIUM-HIGH.**

### Cited Findings
- NCD-RisC (2019), "Rising rural body-mass index is the main driver of the global obesity epidemic in adults", *Nature* 569:260–264. It pools 2,009 studies and 112 million adults. More than 55% of the global rise in mean BMI from 1985 to 2017, and more than 80% in some LMIC regions, came from rural areas. Rural BMI is rising at least as fast as urban BMI in LMICs except among sub-Saharan African women. The urban–rural gap has closed and in some countries reversed — [UPF repository](https://repositori.upf.edu/items/d0d0ac9e-2f66-4f5d-9f18-97fcdd0ea281); [Southampton eprints](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/431250)
- Rural +2.1 kg/m² vs urban +1.3 (women) and +1.6 (men) — [Luxembourg Institute of Health summary](https://events.lih.lu/blog/our-news-1/post/global-burden-study-obesity-rising-faster-in-rural-areas-than-in-cities-253)
- Authors' explanation: rising rural incomes, mechanisation, car use and processed food reaching rural areas, which leads them to call for rural nutrition policy — **Speculation / Descriptive** (same sources)
- Press framing: "obesity rising faster in rural areas than in cities" — [LIH news](https://events.lih.lu/blog/our-news-1/post/global-burden-study-obesity-rising-faster-in-rural-areas-than-in-cities-253) (May 2019, date recalled, not verified)

### Inferences (INFERENCE)
- Hypotheses: (a) agricultural mechanisation lowering energy expenditure; (b) rural roads and retail integration bringing UPFs (the "last-mile" Coca-Cola/Nestlé distribution networks); (c) cash transfers (Familias en Acción, Prospera, Bolsa Família) raising calorie purchases among the rural poor; (d) selective migration, as leaner and younger people leave for cities and older people remain rural; (e) reclassification of rural areas as they urbanise.
- Discriminating variation: road paving and electrification roll-out (Colombia's Vías Terciarias programme, India's PMGSY); staggered entry of supermarkets or distributors; CCT phase-ins; cohort and age decomposition to test migration composition.
- Data: DHS/NFHS women's anthropometrics with GPS clusters (public); Colombia ENSIN 2005/2010/2015 by zone (public on request from ICBF/MinSalud); Mexico ENSANUT (public); India PMGSY road data (public, SHRUG).

### Gaps
- I found no causal study isolating a mechanism (search-only, limited queries).
- Colombia-specific rural vs urban trends were not checked in ENSIN tables.

## Puzzle 5 — Mexico's exceptionally high and rising diabetes mortality relative to Latin American peers

### Takeaway
Mexico's diabetes mortality roughly doubled from 43.4 to 80.1 per 100,000 between 1998 and 2018. In 2024 diabetes accounted for about 112,577 deaths, roughly 13.7% of all deaths. Diabetes years of life lost per person were 1.13 in Mexico vs 0.18 in Chile (2000–2011), even though both countries have high obesity. Homicide drove the pre-COVID fall in male life expectancy, and diabetes is a secondary contributor. The large *cross-country* divergence in diabetes mortality is poorly explained. **Puzzle-ness: MEDIUM-HIGH. Research promise: MEDIUM-HIGH.**

### Cited Findings
- Diabetes mortality rate 43.4 → 80.1 per 100,000 (1998–2018). Diabetes took about 1.30 (men) and 1.24 (women) years off life expectancy between ages 60 and 85 in 2016–18 — [Vega-López & González-Pérez, Rev Saúde Pública 2021, PMC8522739](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8522739/)
- Among Argentina, Chile, Colombia and Mexico (2000–2011), Mexico had the highest and accelerating diabetes mortality, with YLL per person of 1.13 vs 0.18 in Chile — [Gaceta Sanitaria 2015, 29(3):172–177](https://scielosp.org/pdf/gs/2015.v29n3/172-177/es)
- 2024: 112,577 diabetes deaths, about 13.7% of all deaths (INEGI, cited in) — [BMJ Global Health](https://gh.bmj.com/content/11/3/e019891)
- Male life expectancy 71.8 → 71.1 (2015–2019) while female life expectancy held at 77.6. Violence at ages 20–39 accounts for 54.3% of the male loss, with diabetes and amenable causes also contributing — [medRxiv preprint / Int J Epidemiol](https://medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.05.07.24306982v1.full.pdf+html); press: [Oxford Demography news, "Violence and COVID-19 drive decline in Mexico's life expectancy"](https://www.demography.ox.ac.uk/news/violence-and-covid-19-drive-decline-mexicos-life-expectancy) (date not captured)
- Homicides reversed male life-expectancy gains in 2000–2010 (Aburto, Beltrán-Sánchez et al., Health Affairs 2016) — [MPIDR](https://www.demogr.mpg.de/en/publications_databases_6118/publications_1904/journal_articles/homicides_in_mexico_reversed_life_expectancy_gains_for_men_and_slowed_them_for_women_2000_10_6151/). Beltrán-Sánchez: diabetes costs months of life expectancy, homicide costs years — [UCLA CCPR](https://ccpr.ucla.edu/?p=8214)
- Obesity → diabetes → 2–3 years of life expectancy lost at older ages (Palloni, Beltrán-Sánchez et al., Salud Pública Méx 2015) — [UTMB](https://researchexperts.utmb.edu/en/publications/adult-obesity-disease-and-longevity-in-mexico/) — **Partial causal / simulation**

**Existing explanations and evidence quality**: obesity — Descriptive/simulation; homicide for life expectancy — Well explained (*for the life-expectancy stall itself → that part is discarded*). The divergence in diabetes mortality relative to Chile — **Speculation**. Candidate explanations raised in the literature (*recalled, not verified* in this session) are genetic susceptibility (SLC16A11 variant, SIGMA consortium, Nature 2014), poor glycaemic control in IMSS/Seguro Popular, and death-certificate coding practices.

### Inferences (INFERENCE)
- Hypotheses: (a) certification artefact, since Mexico may code diabetes as the underlying cause more often than Chile does; (b) a gap in treatment and control quality (Seguro Popular → INSABI disruption in 2020); (c) high SSB consumption predating the tax; (d) Indigenous-ancestry genetic susceptibility interacting with diet.
- Discriminating variation: multiple-cause-of-death files (diabetes anywhere on the certificate vs as the underlying cause) compared across Mexico, Chile and Colombia; state-level timing of the Seguro Popular roll-out (2004–2010) as an event study on diabetes mortality; the 2020 INSABI transition; within-Mexico variation by ancestry proxies (Indigenous-language municipalities).
- Data: INEGI death microdata (public, multiple causes); DEIS Chile (public); DANE EEVV (public); ENSANUT HbA1c biomarkers (public); Seguro Popular affiliation by municipality (public).

### Gaps
- I could not confirm a clean Chile vs Mexico obesity comparison, or any study comparing death certification between the two.
- Peer-reviewed status of the medRxiv preprint not confirmed beyond the search snippet.

## Puzzle 6 — Colombia's double burden: stunting stuck near 30% among indigenous children while child and adult excess weight rise, and no new ENSIN results since 2015

### Takeaway
Between ENSIN 2010 and 2015, national stunting among children under 5 fell from 13.2% to 10.8%. Among indigenous children it stayed at about 29.5–29.6%, nearly three times the national rate. Excess weight rose among children under 5 (4.9% → 6.3%) and adults (51.2% → about 56.4–56.5%). Departments such as Vaupés, La Guajira, Amazonas, Guainía and Cauca had stunting above 20% in 2010. I found no published ENSIN round after 2015 in searches up to November 2026, so Colombia's flagship nutrition series is about 11 years old. **Puzzle-ness: MEDIUM** (the double burden is well described, and the indigenous gap is not obviously puzzling). **Research promise: MEDIUM**, larger if microdata from a new ENSIN appear.

### Cited Findings
- Stunting 13.2% (2010) → 10.8% (2015); excess weight among children under 5 4.9% → 6.3% — [ICBF ENSIN page](https://www.icbf.gov.co/nutricion/ensin-encuesta-nacional-de-situacion-nutricional)
- Stunting in 2015: 29.6% among indigenous children vs 10.8% nationally and 10.0% among children with no ethnic affiliation. ## Puzzle 7 — Sub-Saharan Africa: hypertension treatment and control barely moved in 30 years while prevalence rose

### Takeaway
NCD-RisC (Lancet 2021) finds that hypertension treatment and control improved in most countries between 1990 and 2019 but changed little in most of sub-Saharan Africa. In some SSA countries treatment rates are below 25% for women and below 20% for men. Over the same years SSA built some of the developing world's largest chronic-care delivery platforms for HIV, which is the puzzle: why did these platforms not spill over? **Puzzle-ness: MEDIUM. Research promise: MEDIUM-HIGH** (HIV platform roll-out gives quasi-experimental variation).

### Cited Findings
- NCD-RisC 2021 (Lancet 398:957–980; 1,201 studies, 104 million participants): SSA, Oceania and South Asia have the lowest detection, treatment and control. Treatment rates are below 25% for women and below 20% for men in some SSA countries, with little change in most SSA countries since 1990 — [Southampton eprint of NCD-RisC paper](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/451199/1/NCD_RisC_hypertension_paper.docx)
- SSA prevalence is "high and increasing", with poor detection, treatment and control — [J Hum Hypertens 2024](https://link.springer.com/10.1038/s41371-024-00913-6)
- Prevalence and awareness vary *within* as well as between SSA countries (H3Africa six-site study) — [PMC9771997](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9771997)
- A figure of about 48% (women) / 34% (men) SSA prevalence in 2019 appeared in the search but is **not verified** against the Lancet paper.

**Existing explanations and evidence quality**: weak primary care, drug supply, and the cost of lifelong therapy — **Descriptive**. Integration of HIV and NCD care has been trialled (SEARCH trial in Uganda/Kenya, *recalled, not verified*) — **Partial causal** for integration effects, but nothing explains the 30-year stagnation.

### Inferences (INFERENCE)
- Hypotheses: (a) donor funding earmarked for HIV crowded out NCD care (vertical programmes); (b) pharmaceutical supply chains not covering antihypertensives; (c) low perceived need for an asymptomatic condition; (d) measurement, since early surveys had few biomarkers.
- Discriminating variation: district-level PEPFAR intensity and timing; national essential-medicines-list changes; WHO STEPS rounds (multiple per country); DHS rounds with blood pressure (Kenya, Lesotho, Namibia, South Africa and others).
- Data: WHO STEPS microdata (on request); DHS biomarker modules (public); PEPFAR site-level spending (public); NIDS South Africa panel (public).

### Gaps
- I did not obtain SSA-specific prevalence trend figures or a rural/urban split from the primary paper.

## Puzzle 8 — Colombia's gastric cancer geography (the "Colombian enigma"): highland vs coastal Nariño

### Takeaway
Gastric cancer is Colombia's leading cause of cancer death. It is concentrated in Andean and mountainous departments (Nariño, Boyacá, Cauca, Cundinamarca, Tolima, Huila, Santander), with sharp contrasts within Nariño between its highland and coastal zones. H. pylori infection is nearly universal in precursor lesions, but prevalence alone does not explain the geography. Explanations based on H. pylori strain ancestry interacting with host ancestry exist (*recalled, not verified*: Kodaman et al., PNAS 2014). Their causal status is partial. **Puzzle-ness: MEDIUM. Research promise: MEDIUM-LOW** for a development economist, except through the eradication/screening policy angle.

### Cited Findings
- High-mortality regions: Nariño, Boyacá, Cundinamarca, Tolima, Huila, Bogotá, Viejo Caldas and Santander. Mountainous zones have the highest rates. Some sources rank Cauca and Nariño highest — [Encolombia guide](https://encolombia.com/medicina/guiasmed/enfermedades-neoplasticas/cancer-gastrico/); [Redalyc](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/3555/355558415003/355558415003.pdf)
- Nariño's age-adjusted male gastric cancer mortality was 19.3 per 100,000. Gastric cancer is the leading cause of cancer death nationally (Nariño government, 2017 report) — [Gobernación de Nariño](https://2020-2023.narino.gov.co/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/CANCER-2017.pdf)
- H. pylori is present in 73.5–100% of precursor lesions and cancers in Nariño. The Urkunina 5000 project tests eradication as primary prevention. Nariño has "three defined gastric-cancer zones" — [Encolombia/Rev. Cirugía](https://encolombia.com/medicina/revistas-medicas/cirugia/vc334/helicobacter-pylori-cancer-gastrico/)
- Caveat: several sources use old data (1959–1990 for the national series; 2013–2018 for Nariño).

**Existing explanations and evidence quality**: H. pylori (necessary cofactor) — **Well explained** for the individual-level risk factor. Diet (salt, nitrosamines) — **Descriptive**. Phylogeographic mismatch between H. pylori and host ancestry (Kodaman et al., 2014, *not verified*) — **Partial causal**. The geographic contrast as a whole — **Partial causal**.

### Inferences (INFERENCE)
- The interesting development angle is the *policy* response rather than the aetiology: rolling out H. pylori test-and-treat or endoscopy screening across EPSs and regions, and why mortality has (or has not) fallen. A panel of department-level mortality trends from DANE EEVV for 2001–2024 would show whether the highland–coast gap is closing.
- Data: DANE EEVV (public), INC cancer registries (Pasto and Cali population-based registries; restricted/aggregate), CAC cancer reports (public aggregates).

### Gaps
- No recent (2015–2025) department-level gastric cancer mortality figures were captured. Kodaman et al. is not verified in this session.
