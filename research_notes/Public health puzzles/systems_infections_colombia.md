# Public-health puzzles in Colombia, 2001-2026: health system & infectious disease

Method note (applies throughout): about 30 web searches (Spanish + English). WebFetch was not used, so every figure below comes from **search-result snippets/summaries ("search-only")** and was not read in the full source document. Before citing anything, check it against the primary PDF (INS informe de evento / BES, Defensoría report, Supersalud communiqué). Anything recalled from memory and not confirmed in this session is marked **"not verified"**. Inferences are labeled **INFERENCE**. Evidence-quality scale for existing explanations: None / Speculation / Descriptive / Partial causal / Well explained.

Ranking (puzzle-ness × research promise for an applied development micro PhD):
1. Gestational syphilis up roughly 4x while congenital syphilis stays flat, far above the elimination target (HIGH)
2. Health tutelas hit record highs despite near-universal coverage and low out-of-pocket (OOP) spending; huge variation by insurer and department (HIGH)
3. 2024-2026 wave of EPS interventions and liquidations covering about 24M people: unstudied patient outcomes (HIGH; the SaludCoop precedent is well identified)
4. TB incidence rising since 2019, and drug-resistant TB nearly doubling (MEDIUM-HIGH)
5. 2025 pertussis resurgence concentrated in Bogotá/Antioquia, the richest and best-covered areas (MEDIUM-HIGH)
6. Wave of obstetric/neonatal/pediatric service closures (2023-2025) during a collapse in births (MEDIUM-HIGH)
7. Vector-borne volatility: record dengue in 2023-24 at higher altitudes; malaria +81% in 2024, then -46% (MEDIUM)
8. Subsidized regime majority (≈52-54%) and regime mobility vs formal employment (MEDIUM)
Discarded/weak seeds are listed in the final section.

## Puzzle 1 — Gestational syphilis quadrupled, congenital syphilis flat (and 5x over the elimination target)

### Takeaway
Reported gestational syphilis (GS) rose from about 5 to about 22-24 per 1,000 live births + stillbirths (2014→2024/25). Congenital syphilis (CS) stayed around 2.4-2.9 per 1,000 for years, about 5x the 0.5 elimination target. This is either a detection improvement (rising GS with stable CS is what better screening would produce) or a real epidemic that treatment is only partly absorbing. Venezuelan migration is the default press explanation, but the evidence is thin and partly contradictory. No study decomposes the trend.

### Cited Findings
**Facts (INS/SIVIGILA; search-only):**
- GS prevalence ratio 2024: **21.8 per 1,000 NV+MF, 9,748 cases**. 2025 through epi-week 40: **23.8, 7,876 cases**. CS 2024: **1,097 cases, 2.5 per 1,000 NV+MF**, vs national goal 0.5 — [INS news](https://www.ins.gov.co/Noticias/Paginas/Colombia-refuerza-acciones-para-prevenir-la-sifilis-gestacional-y-congenita.aspx)
- CS 2025 to 4 Oct: 825 cases, 2.5/1,000. Top entities: Antioquia, Bogotá, Cartagena, Norte de Santander, Cundinamarca — [INS news](https://www.ins.gov.co/Noticias/Paginas/Colombia-refuerza-acciones-para-prevenir-la-sifilis-gestacional-y-congenita.aspx)
- INS ecological analysis 2014-2021: GS rates rose from **5.1 (2014) to 17.1 per 1,000 NV (2021)** — [Redalyc/journal article](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/1952/195282748003/195282748003.epub)
- INS 2023 event report: CS incidence rose 2018-2021 to a peak of **2.9 per 1,000 NV+MF** — [INS Informe de evento 2023](https://www.ins.gov.co/buscador-eventos/Informesdeevento/SIFILIS%20GESTACIONAL%20%20Y%20CONGENITA%20INFORME%20DE%20EVENTO%202023.pdf). Another article reports CS at 1.5 (2017) → 2.4 (2023) — [Redalyc](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/1952/195282749003/195282749003.epub)
- **Discrepancy:** a Bogotá-published BES (SIVIGILA data through week 38, 2024) gives national CS of 1.85 (2019) → 2.11 (2024), +16.7%. The same bulletin also states a **25% reduction 2020-2024** for weeks 1-38 and credits strengthened GS surveillance with lowering CS — [BES 40, 2024](https://www.saludcapital.gov.co/CTDLab/Publicaciones/2024/BES_40.pdf). The 2.11 (partial year) vs 2.5 (full year) gap is unexplained (cut-off dates, or numerator/denominator definitions).
- Bogotá GS prevalence ratio: **3.5 (2009) → 9.1 (2023) → 11.4 per 1,000 births (H1 2024)** — [Infobae, 28 Sep 2024](https://www.infobae.com/colombia/2024/09/28/alerta-por-aumento-de-sifilis-congenita-y-gestacional-en-bogota-alto-riesgo-de-mortalidad-en-los-recien-nacidos/); [Observatorio de Salud de Bogotá](https://saludata.saludcapital.gov.co/osb/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/OSB_SaludSexual-Sifilis-Gestacional.pdf)
- Migration: a MinSalud table (SIVIGILA) lists GS events by nationality. Venezuelan nationals had **1,806 (2022) and 1,645 (2023)** GS cases, but the cut-off appears to be **epidemiological period VIII**, i.e. a partial year — [MinSalud, eventos salud pública migrantes](https://www.minsalud.gov.co/sites/rid/Lists/BibliotecaDigital/RIDE/DE/PS/eventos-salud-publica-migrantes.pdf). Earlier MinSalud table of GS events "procedentes del exterior": 11 (2017), 106 (2018), 489 (2019) — [MinSalud 2019](https://www.minsalud.gov.co/sites/rid/Lists/BibliotecaDigital/RIDE/VS/ED/GCFI/2019-09-20-msps-migrantes.pdf)
- A UNFPA document cites a Rev Panam Salud Pública 2023 study. In it, late diagnosis (3rd trimester/postpartum) occurred in **61.4%** of Venezuelan women vs 27.1% in a comparison group, and in 2022 **90.1% (225)** of women whose children had syphilis had Venezuelan origin or residence. This appears to be a border/pendular-population subsample, not national — [UNFPA ToR](https://colombia.unfpa.org/sites/default/files/submissions/tdr_evaluacion_acceso_servicios_de_ssr.docx.pdf). Not verified in the original article.
- Venezuelan migrant survey (6,221 adults, 4 cities, Lancet Reg Health Americas 2024): weighted syphilis prevalence of **4.6% among women**, correlated with irregular status and no use of Colombian health care (search-snippet summary; exact article URL not captured — not verified).
- Brazil comparison (Roraima): Venezuelan migration contributed to CS counts but was **not the main driver**; Brazilian mothers had higher rates — [Rev Epidemiol UNISC](https://seer.unisc.br/index.php/epidemiologia/article/view/18537)

**Press framing:**
- "Aumentan casos de sífilis gestacional y congénita en frontera entre Colombia y Venezuela" — [Voz de América](https://www.vozdeamerica.com/a/aumentan-casos-sifilis-gestacional-frontera-colombia-venezuela-/7325003.html) (date not captured)
- "Alerta por aumento de sífilis congénita y gestacional en Bogotá" — [Infobae, 2024-09-28](https://www.infobae.com/colombia/2024/09/28/alerta-por-aumento-de-sifilis-congenita-y-gestacional-en-bogota-alto-riesgo-de-mortalidad-en-los-recien-nacidos/)

**Existing explanations:**
- Migration contact: Ibáñez, Rozo & Urbina, "Forced Migration and the Spread of Infectious Diseases" (IDB DP-00834; J. Health Econ. 2021). Using a municipal-month panel with shift-share predicted inflows, a 1 SD increase in predicted migrant inflow raises **syphilis by 0.09 per 100,000 (mean 1.73)**. Natives did not relocate. Mechanism: contact with infected arrivals — [IDB](https://publications.iadb.org/en/forced-migration-and-spread-infectious-diseases). Quality: **Partial causal** (it covers *acquired* syphilis notifications, not GS/CS vs detection, and the data end before the post-2021 rise).
- Better surveillance/screening: the INS BES 40 itself credits strengthened GS surveillance for lower CS — [BES 40](https://www.saludcapital.gov.co/CTDLab/Publicaciones/2024/BES_40.pdf). Quality: **Speculation/Descriptive**.
- Implementation gaps: 23% of health workers in Bolívar do not use rapid tests — [Rev Colomb Obstet Ginecol/DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/8a135286b6a340109660f60a159d15c3). The clinical guideline dates from 2014 and needs updating — [Rev UdeA Ginecología](https://revistas.udea.edu.co/index.php/ginecologia_y_obstetricia/article/download/346860/20808519/253891). Quality: **Descriptive**.
- Antioquia migrant mothers 2020-22 (EAFIT undergraduate thesis linking incidence to affiliation status) — [EAFIT](https://repository.eafit.edu.co/server/api/core/bitstreams/31b6e6b3-795e-45af-8c7e-7caef2ca8d59/content). Quality: Descriptive.
- Cúcuta qualitative study (20 mothers, medRxiv 2024) — [medRxiv](https://medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.08.30.24312781.full.pdf). Quality: Descriptive/qualitative.

### Inferences
- **Why it is a puzzle (INFERENCE):** a roughly 4x rise in GS with a flat CS ratio means the GS→CS "conversion rate" fell sharply. That fits better detection and treatment. But CS remains 5x the target in a system with ~95% insurance and free prenatal care, and Bogotá (the best-resourced city) shows the steepest GS rise. Migration can't be the whole story: Venezuelan GS counts (~1,600-1,800 per partial year) are a minority of ~9,700 national cases.
- **Candidate hypotheses (INFERENCE):** (a) detection: rollout of point-of-care rapid treponemal tests under the 2014 guideline raises GS notifications mechanically; (b) a real rise in heterosexual transmission (paralleling LAC/Brazil and the US); (c) migration: direct importation plus spillovers to native women via sexual networks; (d) the uninsured/irregular-migrant prenatal-care gap (late first visit); (e) penicillin-benzathine supply disruptions (no Colombian evidence found); (f) insurer quality: CS conditional on GS may vary by EPS, especially intervened ones.
- **Discriminating variation (INFERENCE):** CS/GS ratio by department × year (a detection proxy); GS among Colombian-national women only vs Venezuelan inflow intensity (shift-share à la Ibáñez et al.); timing of rapid-test adoption by municipality; trimester at diagnosis (INS variable); EPS of affiliation of the mother (SIVIGILA records the insurer) → insurer fixed effects, plus the 2024 intervention shock.
- **Data:** SIVIGILA microdata (event codes 740 GS / 750 CS) are partially public via INS "microdatos" (anonymized; check availability by year). Annual informes de evento are public. Births/stillbirths come from DANE EEVV (public). RIPS prenatal visits are restricted (SISPRO cubes give aggregates). Migration: Migración Colombia / RUMV/PEP registries (aggregates public).
- **Puzzle-ness: HIGH. Research promise: HIGH.** Clean outcome; policy-relevant (PAHO elimination initiative); several identifying shocks.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm the share of GS/CS cases among Venezuelan nationals for full years 2023-2025.
- Not found: any Colombian study separating detection from true incidence; any evidence on penicillin shortages.
- The discrepancy between the 2.11 and 2.5 CS incidence figures for 2024 is unresolved.

## Puzzle 2 — Tutela explosion despite near-universal coverage and low OOP spending

### Takeaway
Health tutelas reached historic highs: ~198k (2023), >265k (2024), ~312.5k (2025). They are about 34% of all tutelas, 17 years after T-760/2008 and 12+ years after benefit-package unification. Coverage is ~95%+ and OOP spending is among the lowest in Latin America. Tutela rates per affiliate vary several-fold across EPS and departments, and the highest per-capita departments are not the poorest. No causal explanation exists.

### Cited Findings
**Facts (Defensoría del Pueblo; search-only):**
- 2023: **197,765** health tutelas, +26.44% vs 2022, the third highest in 32 years — [El Tiempo](https://www.eltiempo.com/justicia/investigacion/con-197-765-registros-cifra-de-tutelas-por-salud-en-2023-fue-la-tercera-mas-alta-en-32-anos-defensoria-3338119)
- 2024: **>265,000**. 2025: **~312,500 (+17.92%)**, described by Defensora Iris Marín as a historic maximum. Health is **34%** of all tutelas — [El País Cali](https://www.elpais.com.co/colombia/defensoria-alerta-por-aumento-de-tutelas-en-salud-en-18-312500-se-presentaron-en-2025-2304.html); [El Espectador](https://www.elespectador.com/salud/tutelas-en-salud-aumentaron-en-un-1792-en-2025-defensoria/); [Blu Radio](https://www.bluradio.com/salud/maximo-historico-de-tutelas-en-salud-en-colombia-312-000-entre-2024-y-2025-rg10). **Discrepancy:** La Silla Vacía reports +34.10% for 2024 vs 2023, inconsistent with 197.8k→265k (+34% is consistent; the +42% headline in [Vanguardia, Aug 2024](https://www.vanguardia.com/colombia/2024/08/16/aumentan-en-un-42-las-tutelas-por-mala-atencion-en-el-sistema-de-salud/) is a partial-year figure).
- Pre-T-760 baseline: **328,191** health tutelas in 1999-2005, ~80% granted — [PLoS Med 2009 / Yamin & Parra-Vera](https://dx.doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1000032)
- 2023 by insurer: EPS were the defendant in **80.72%** of cases. Nueva EPS had 44,323 (highest absolute). The highest rates per 10,000 affiliates: **Savia Salud 85, Asmet Salud 68, Comfaoriente 57, SOS 50, Emssanar 46**. By department (share): Antioquia 18.79%, Valle 10.83%, Bogotá 9.61%, Norte de Santander 7.35%, Santander 5.75%. **Per-capita highest: Arauca, Caldas, Norte de Santander, Risaralda, San Andrés** — [La República](https://www.larepublica.co/economia/defensoria-denuncia-cerca-de-198-000-tutelas-en-2023-por-violacion-al-derecho-de-la-salud-3850254); [Vanguardia](https://www.vanguardia.com/santander/2024/04/28/santander-el-quinto-departamento-con-mas-tutelas-presentadas-por-vulneracion-a-la-salud-en-2023/)
- 2022 (MinSalud report to Constitutional Court): average **23 tutelas per 10,000 affiliates**; Savia Salud 50/10k (highest); Antioquia 25,708 — [MinSalud informe tutelas 2022](https://www.minsalud.gov.co/sites/rid/Lists/BibliotecaDigital/RIDE/DE/DIJ/presentacion-informe-tutelas-salud-2022.pdf)
- Measurement caveat: Defensoría rates can be inflated for shrinking EPS (Medimás, Coomeva) when historical enrollment is the denominator — [Asuntos Legales](https://www.asuntoslegales.com.co/actualidad/por-cada-10-000-afiliados-medimas-y-coomeva-presentan-la-mayor-cantidad-de-tutelas-3188552)
- Low OOP context: OOP spending was 14.8% (2012) and 15.9% (2011) of total health spending per MinSalud-cited estimates — [MinSalud boletín gasto de bolsillo](https://www.minsalud.gov.co/sites/rid/Lists/BibliotecaDigital/RIDE/VS/MET/boletin-gasto-bolsillo.pdf). Icesi/PROESA: 20.6% vs a LatAm average of 42.7% (year not confirmed) — [PROESA](https://www.icesi.edu.co/proesa/images/mesa/mesa-gasto-de-bolsillo-en-colombia.pdf). OOP **grew 57.3% between 2022 and 2025** (Afidro/Algebra Labs, an industry source) — [Portafolio](https://www.portafolio.co/economia/gobierno/gasto-de-bolsillo-en-salud-crecio-57-y-golpea-mas-a-hogares-pobres-y-rurales-en-colombia-493111). WHO GHED figure for 2022 not obtained.

**Press framing:** "Máximo histórico de tutelas en salud" — [Blu Radio](https://www.bluradio.com/salud/maximo-historico-de-tutelas-en-salud-en-colombia-312-000-entre-2024-y-2025-rg10); "Durante 2024 se presentaron cerca de 700 tutelas por salud al día" — [Noticias RCN](https://www.noticiasrcn.com/salud-y-bienestar/durante-2024-se-presentaron-cerca-de-700-tutelas-por-salud-al-dia-836837). Framing ties the rise to the EPS crisis under the Petro government and to interventions.

**Existing explanations:**
- Alzate Mora (Health & Human Rights J., 2014): T-760 compliance was "very reduced"; litigation did not fall after T-760 and "has reached its limit" — [HHR Journal](https://www.hhrjournal.org/2014/09/23/health-litigation-in-colombia-have-we-reached-the-limit-for-the-judicialization-of-health/). Quality: **Speculation/Descriptive** (legal essay).
- Law 100 model does not address institutional barriers to access (Rev. Salud Pública review) — [UNAL](https://revistas.unal.edu.co/index.php/revsaludpublica/article/download/121060/95921/749331). Quality: Descriptive.
- Restrepo-Zea & Palacios-Romaña (2025) track T-760 order compliance to 2024 (abstract only seen) — same search. Quality: Descriptive.
- Effects of insurer exit on complaints: the CAPRECOM liquidation raised complaint rates in receiving EPS in quarters 1-2 only, with **no significant effect on judicial claims** (Uniandes, DiD) — [Minciencias/Uniandes record](https://kujane.minciencias.gov.co/Record/UNIANDES2_c1fe034b64d0649e5d0a29953a882229/Details). Quality: **Partial causal**.

### Inferences
- **Why it is a puzzle (INFERENCE):** standard theory says that once coverage and benefit packages are universal and equalized, litigation should fall. Instead it has risen almost monotonically, with an acceleration in 2023-2025. Cross-insurer dispersion (Savia 85 vs average ~23 per 10k) is far larger than plausible differences in morbidity. The per-capita ranking (Arauca, Caldas, Risaralda, San Andrés) doesn't track poverty or rurality, which suggests supply-side legal-market or judicial-culture factors.
- **Candidate hypotheses (INFERENCE):** (a) insurer rationing in response to the UPC/financial squeeze, so tutelas track EPS liquidity; (b) a "legal-supply" channel: law clinics, personerías, lawyers, and online tutela templates (judiciary digitization post-2020) lower filing costs; (c) learning/peer effects: high grant rates (~80%) create a self-reinforcing equilibrium; (d) presupuestos máximos and non-PBS rationing (see Puzzle 3/discards); (e) intervened EPS (state-run) are less responsive.
- **Discriminating variation (INFERENCE):** EPS × municipality × month tutela panel against EPS financial indicators (Supersalud), dates of intervention/liquidation, and judicial digitization timing (Decreto 806/2020, Ley 2213/2022); judge assignment (random allocation in reparto) for grant rates; border departments (Arauca, Norte de Santander) vs migration.
- **Data:** Defensoría annual reports (public PDFs, aggregate tables by EPS and department). Constitutional Court/Rama Judicial tutela statistics (partly public). Microdata may be obtainable via petición (derecho de petición). Supersalud PQRD (complaint) data: public aggregates. RIPS restricted.
- **Puzzle-ness: HIGH. Research promise: HIGH** (clear panel, policy shocks; closely related to the insurer-exit literature).

### Gaps
- No breakdown by regime (subsidized vs contributory) found for 2023-2025.
- Not found: any causal study of determinants of tutela growth post-2008.
- WHO GHED OOP share for the latest year not retrieved.

## Puzzle 3 — EPS interventions and liquidations, 2015-2026: the next natural experiment

### Takeaway
SaludCoop's closure (2015) is **well identified**: it raised mortality via congestion and network narrowing (Buitrago, Rodríguez-Lesmes, Serna, Vera-Hernández, REStat 2026). The 2024-2026 wave is far larger and its patient effects are unstudied. Nueva EPS (~10.9M), Sanitas (~5.7M), SOS, Famisanar, Asmet, Emssanar, Savia, and Coosalud were intervened, ≈24M affiliates in intervened EPS. The Sanitas intervention was reversed by the Constitutional Court (2025), and Petro ordered liquidation of the "bankrupt" EPS (17 Mar 2026). The novelty here is *state takeover* (intervention) rather than exit, plus the reversal.

### Cited Findings
- SaludCoop: incumbent insurers narrowed networks by ~10%. Mortality rose through **congestion and reduced network breadth**, and insurers dropped providers serving sicker patients — [Serna working paper "Insurer Terminations and Mortality"](https://nsernab.github.io/Insurer_Terminations_and_Mortality.pdf); [CEPR DP 19062 (IDEAS)](https://ideas.repec.org/p/cpr/ceprdp/19062.html). A ~20% mortality increase is reported only in a LinkedIn post — **not verified**. Quality: **Well explained** for SaludCoop.
- Mass transfers from liquidated EPS harm the finances of receiving insurers — [Unisabana Pure](https://pure.unisabana.edu.co/es/publications/effect-of-mass-transfers-of-enrollees-from-liquidated-health-insu/). Quality: Partial causal.
- CAPRECOM liquidation: complaints rose temporarily, no effect on judicial claims — [Uniandes record](https://kujane.minciencias.gov.co/Record/UNIANDES2_c1fe034b64d0649e5d0a29953a882229/Details).
- 2024 interventions: Nueva EPS + Sanitas >17M affiliates (~32% of population) — [Portafolio](https://www.portafolio.co/economia/gobierno/pacientes-colombia-exige-investigaciones-por-crisis-en-nueva-eps-y-sanitas-639577); breakdown of 10.9M Nueva EPS, 5.7M Sanitas, 0.7M SOS — [Valora Analitik](https://www.valoraanalitik.com/?p=384111); six intervened EPS total >24M (El País) / 24.3M (El Heraldo) — [El País](https://www.elpais.com.co/colombia/seis-eps-estan-intervenidas-por-la-supersalud-suman-mas-de-24-millones-de-afiliados-0316.html); [El Heraldo](https://www.elheraldo.co/colombia/duros-cuestionamientos-supersalud-por-la-efectividad-de-intervenciones-eps-1083841)
- Supersalud (Jan 2025): of 9 intervened EPS, 4 were below the national complaint rate of 322.03 (Nueva EPS, Emssanar, Coosalud, Asmet) — [Supersalud communiqué via Ámbito Jurídico](https://ambitojuridico.com/sites/default/files/2025-01/COMUNICADO-Supersaludporcada-2025.pdf)
- Constitutional Court struck down the Sanitas intervention; Petro said he would comply — [El Heraldo, 2025-06-27](https://www.elheraldo.co/colombia/2025/06/27/petro-dice-que-acatara-el-fallo-de-la-corte-constitucional-que-tumbo-la-intervencion-a-la-eps-sanitas/)
- 17 Mar 2026: Petro ordered the Health and Finance ministers to liquidate the "bankrupt" EPS, about 7 entities with ~12M affiliates (likely the intervened ones except Nueva EPS; names not officially specified). A day later he softened ("quedarán las que limpien sus estados financieros"). Supersalud called for calm — [La Silla Vacía](https://www.lasillavacia.com/?p=409710); [Vanguardia 2026-03-17](https://www.vanguardia.com/colombia/2026/03/17/petro-ordena-liquidar-eps-en-quiebra-y-desata-controversia-nacional/); [Vanguardia 2026-03-18](https://www.vanguardia.com/colombia/2026/03/18/supersalud-intenta-calmar-la-alarma-tras-el-anuncio-de-petro-de-liquidar-las-eps-en-quiebra/). Governors warned of hospital collapse — [Vanguardia 2026-03-21](https://www.vanguardia.com/colombia/2026/03/21/gobernadores-prenden-alertas-por-liquidaciones-de-eps-arriesgan-vidas-y-colapsan-hospitales/). Press lists: Coosalud, Famisanar, Asmet, Emssanar, Savia, SOS, Capresoca — [enter.co](https://www.enter.co/?p=582218). **No formal Supersalud liquidation resolution found** as of search.
- An ISPOR 2025 poster claims a "causal analysis" of collateral effects of intervened insurers on financial and perception outcomes, 2023-2025 — [ISPOR](https://www.ispor.org/heor-resources/presentations-database/presentation-cti/ispor-europe-2025/poster-session-5-2/collateral-impact-of-government-intervened-health-insurers-on-the-colombian-health-system-a-causal-analysis-of-financial-health-and-perception-outcomes-2023-2025). Content not seen.

### Inferences
- **Why it is a puzzle (INFERENCE):** intervention replaces management but keeps enrollees, a different treatment from exit. The Supersalud data show some intervened EPS have *below-average* complaint rates, while tutela rates per affiliate are highest at intervened EPS (Savia, Asmet, SOS, Emssanar; Puzzle 2). Whether state management improves or worsens care is unknown. The Sanitas reversal provides an "un-treatment."
- **Hypotheses:** (a) intervention → payment arrears to IPS → service closures (links to Puzzle 6); (b) intervention reduces rent extraction → better care; (c) political targeting of interventions (endogenous timing).
- **Discriminating variation:** staggered intervention dates (Apr 2024 Nueva EPS/Sanitas; others 2024-25); Sanitas reversal mid-2025; municipality-level market shares of intervened EPS (pre-period) as exposure; outcomes from SIVIGILA (maternal/perinatal deaths, CS), DANE deaths (public microdata, cause/municipality/insurance regime), tutelas, and Supersalud PQRD.
- **Data:** DANE EEVV mortality microdata (public; includes regime but no EPS ID — check); BDUA enrollment by EPS-municipality (ADRES, public aggregates); RIPS restricted.
- **Puzzle-ness: MEDIUM (exit is well explained) / HIGH for intervention. Research promise: HIGH.**

### Gaps
- Official status of the 2026 liquidation order after March 2026 not confirmed.
- No peer-reviewed study of the 2024 intervention effects on health outcomes found.

## Puzzle 4 — TB incidence rising after 2019, MDR-TB nearly doubling

### Takeaway
Colombia's TB notifications rose from 14,902 (2019) to ~21,200 (2024) and 22,193 (2025p), with the rate at 38.5/100k, against a national target of reducing cases. Drug-resistant TB rose from 329 to 606 cases (2019→2024). Migration is partly causal (Ibáñez et al.). Post-COVID catch-up detection, prisons and HIV are descriptive candidates. There is no decomposition.

### Cited Findings
- INS BES week 12/2025: preliminary 2024 rate 36.78/100k, **20,832 cases vs 14,902 in 2019** — [INS BES 2025 s12](https://www.ins.gov.co/buscador-eventos/BoletinEpidemiologico/2025_Boletin_epidemiologico_semana_12.pdf)
- INS BES week 11/2026: **2024 = 37.44/100k (21,217 cases); 2025 = 38.52/100k (22,193 cases, preliminary), +2.9%**. The national target for 2025 was not met — [INS BES 2026 s11](https://www.ins.gov.co/BibliotecaDigital/2026-boletin-epidemiologico-semana-11.pdf). - Drug-resistant TB: **606 cases (2024) vs 329 (2019)** (Consultorsalud citing INS) — [Consultorsalud](https://consultorsalud.com/desafio-persistente-de-la-tuberculosis-pulmonar/)

- Existing explanations: migration. - **Why it is a puzzle (INFERENCE):** rising income, universal free TB treatment and near-universal coverage should push incidence down (WHO End-TB targets). The rise outpaces what Ibáñez et al.'s effect sizes imply, assuming roughly 2.8M migrants. Also, MDR doubling suggests treatment-adherence failure, possibly linked to EPS dysfunction and drug shortages.
- **Hypotheses:** (a) post-COVID detection catch-up (2020-21 dip, then a rebound); (b) migration; (c) prison overcrowding (INPEC); (d) treatment interruptions from EPS crises and drug shortages → MDR; (e) diabetes/HIV comorbidity.
- **Discriminating variation:** case nationality and population group (SIVIGILA variables); municipality migration intensity; prisons (INPEC census); new vs. previously-treated cases; EPS of the patient × intervention timing; treatment outcomes (loss to follow-up).
- **Data:** SIVIGILA TB event 813 (aggregates public; microdata partially); INS informes de evento (public); WHO TB country profile (public).
- **Puzzle-ness: MEDIUM-HIGH. Research promise: MEDIUM-HIGH** (MDR × insurer-crisis angle is novel).

## Puzzle 6 — Obstetric, neonatal and pediatric service closures amid a fertility collapse

### Takeaway
2023-2025 saw a wave of closures of obstetric/neonatal units, including at elite private and university hospitals in Bogotá, Cali, Medellín and Bucaramanga. The stated causes are falling births plus EPS arrears. These are two very different stories (efficient consolidation vs. financial rationing), and their consequences for travel distance, maternal/perinatal outcomes, and births of Chocó/Córdoba women referred to Medellín are unknown.

### Cited Findings
- 36,737 health services closed in 2023-2024, 9,325 definitively — [El Colombiano](https://www.elcolombiano.com/colombia/crisis-de-la-salud-por-cierre-de-servicios-en-hospitales-y-clinicas-LG27567905)

- Sicolped: pediatrics/neonatology/obstetrics yield very low or negative margins; ACHC reported debts to 225 IPS of **COP 18.9 trillion (June 2024)** — [Noticias Caracol](https://www.noticiascaracol.com/salud/por-que-se-han-cerrado-tantos-servicios-de-pediatria-en-colombia-rg10)
- Medellín: ~30% of deliveries are to mothers from other municipalities and departments (Chocó, Córdoba) — [El Colombiano](https://www.elcolombiano.com/medellin/camas-partos-nacimientos-medellin-hospitales-cierran-servicios-MG26758589)

- **Puzzle-ness: MEDIUM-HIGH. Research promise: HIGH** (public REPS + DANE birth microdata give a strong design).

## Puzzle 7 — Vector-borne volatility: record dengue at higher altitudes; malaria +81% in 2024 then -46%

### Takeaway
Dengue 2023-24 was the largest epidemic cycle since 2007 (>253k cases by late Aug 2024). Aedes and dengue virus have been documented above the "1,500 m ceiling" (A. aegypti at 2,302 m, infected mosquitoes at 1,984 m in Bello). DENV-3 re-expanded and hit children. Malaria rose 81% in 2024 (Chocó, Antioquia, Córdoba; ~135k cases) and fell 46% by mid-2025. Climate (El Niño) is the default explanation and is **partially studied**. The large between-municipality heterogeneity and the speed of the malaria reversal are not explained.

### Cited Findings
- Dengue: 2023-2024 the cycle with most cases since 2007; 253,200 cases to 24 Aug 2024 (MinSalud); 480 of 825 at-risk municipalities above expected levels; 77.5% of cases from Valle, Santander, Huila, Tolima, Cundinamarca, Norte de Santander, Cauca, Antioquia — [El Colombiano](https://www.elcolombiano.com/colombia/salud/en-un-ano-los-casos-de-dengue-en-colombia-pasaron-de-65-mil-a-237-mil-LE25218605); [Meridiano 70](https://meridiano70.co/2024/09/04/ministerio-salud-medidas-dengue-aumento-casos-colombia); [colombia.com](https://www.colombia.com/vida-y-estilo/salud-y-bienestar/alerta-en-825-municipios-de-colombia-por-casos-de-dengue-449637). Full-year 2024 total and 2019 total: **not verified**.
- 2025: 117,789 cases and 141 deaths to 2 Dec 2025 — [Portafolio](https://www.portafolio.co/economia/regiones/colombia-acumula-117-789-casos-de-dengue-con-141-muertes-notificadas-segun-el-ins-484477)
- DENV-3: PAHO (Feb 2025) notes Colombia expanded DENV-3 circulation in 2024; first isolates in Casanare were associated with severe dengue and child deaths — [PAHO](https://www.paho.org/es/noticias/10-2-2025-ops-alerta-sobre-riesgo-brotes-dengue-por-circulacion-serotipo-denv-3-americas)
- Altitude: A. aegypti at 2,302 m (Bello, Antioquia), DENV-2-infected mosquitoes at 1,984 m; previously believed absent above 1,500 m (Biomédica 2016) — [PubMed 27622492](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27622492). Kache et al. (medRxiv 2024), 1,120 municipalities, 2008-2019: climate extremes bring earlier onset of risk in high-elevation settlements — [medRxiv](https://medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.04.02.24304484v1.full.pdf) (**Partial causal**, preprint).
- Malaria 2024: +81% vs 2023, concentrated in Chocó, Antioquia, Córdoba — [Blu Radio](https://www.bluradio.com/salud/brote-de-malaria-en-colombia-incremento-un-81-en-comparacion-con-el-2023-segun-el-ins-rg10); 110,343 cases to week 44; Quibdó 9.4% (3,771), Bagadó 7.4% (2,949); P. vivax 61.9% — [BES 45 2024](https://www.saludcapital.gov.co/CTDLab/Publicaciones/2024/BES_45.pdf); full year 2024 ~135,290 cases, 28 deaths (search summary; source URL among INS results — verify); 2025 week 17: **-46%** vs same period 2024 — [INS BES 2025 s17](https://www.ins.gov.co/BibliotecaDigital/2025-boletin-epidemiologico-semana-17.pdf). Explanation offered: El Niño breeding conditions — [Cambio](https://cambiocolombia.com/salud-y-bienestar/malaria-colombia-calentamiento-global-cambio-climatico-choco-antioquia) (Speculation/Descriptive). Illegal mining and armed-group control as drivers: my recollection from the literature, **not verified** this session.
- Ibáñez et al. find **no** effect of migration on vector-borne diseases — [IDB](https://publications.iadb.org/en/forced-migration-and-spread-infectious-diseases)

### Inferences
- **Why it is a puzzle (INFERENCE):** climate shocks are national, yet dengue excess is municipality-specific (480/825), and malaria doubles and halves within 18 months, too fast for climate trends. Local control capacity (fumigation contracts run by departmental secretariats, EPS diagnosis/treatment), mining booms, and armed-group restrictions on vector-control teams are plausible, testable modifiers.
- **Discriminating variation:** altitude bands × ENSO phase (municipality-week panel); departmental vector-control budgets and contract timing (SECOP, public); illegal-mining polygons (UNODC SIMCI, public); conflict events (CERAC/ACLED); serotype introductions (INS virology).
- **Data:** SIVIGILA weekly municipal counts (public via BES and Portal SIVIGILA); IDEAM climate (public); SIMCI mining (public).
- **Puzzle-ness: MEDIUM** (climate partly explains it; an active epi literature exists). **Research promise: MEDIUM** (a political-economy-of-vector-control angle is underexplored).

### Gaps
- Final 2024 dengue total, 2019 total, and any evidence of autochthonous dengue in new high-altitude municipalities (vs. vector presence) not retrieved.
- Malaria–mining causal evidence not verified this session.

## Puzzle 8 — Subsidized regime is the majority and growing; regime mobility vs formal employment

### Takeaway
The subsidized regime holds ≈52-54% of affiliates (2024-25) and grew by ~600k in a year while the contributory regime lost members. Law 1438/2011 art. 35 lets subsidized affiliates who get formal jobs remain in the subsidized regime (movilidad). By Jan 2024, 5.28M subsidized affiliates were in EPS not authorized for that regime. Whether the subsidized regime is a disincentive to formality (the Levy "informality trap" argument) is a classic question. Recent dynamics and the mobility rule are understudied.

### Cited Findings
- Subsidized share 52.0% (Q3 2024), 52.4% (Q4 2024), 53.6% (Q1 2025), 52.1% (Q2 2025); subsidized +~600k in 12 months while contributory lost (special regimes -114k); contributory -104k in April 2024 — [U. Externado boletines Seguridad Social](https://uexternado.edu.co/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/boletin-11-ss.pdf); [Boletín 13](https://www.uexternado.edu.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Boletin_13_SS.pdf); [Boletín 15](https://www.uexternado.edu.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Boletin_15_SS.pdf)
- Decreto 0719/2024 cites Ley 1438 art. 35 (mobility) and reports **5,281,467** subsidized affiliates in EPS not authorized for that regime as of 31 Jan 2024 — [JEP normograma, Decreto 719 de 2024](https://jurinfo.jep.gov.co/Normograma/Compilacion/docs/decreto_0719_2024.htm). The Consejo de Estado ordered regulation of who must move by income — [Portafolio](https://www.portafolio.co/economia/gobierno/salud-en-colombia-estos-afiliados-saldran-de-regimen-subsidiado-por-orden-del-consejo-de-estado-565751)
- Existing literature (recalled, **not verified** this session): Camacho, Conover & Hoyos (WBER 2014), on effects of Colombia's subsidized regime on formality using the SISBEN discontinuity, find increases in informality. A Hamilton College note says Conover published in Economic Development and Cultural Change — [Hamilton](https://hamilton.edu/news/story/conover-publishes-paper-in-economic-development-and-cultural-change) (topic not confirmed). Quality: **Partial causal** for the pre-2012 period.

### Inferences
- **Why it is a puzzle (INFERENCE):** after the 2012-13 benefit equalization, the contributory regime offers no extra benefits (except cash disability/maternity leave), so the health-insurance wedge against formality should be larger. Yet formal employment rose in 2022-23 while the subsidized share kept rising. The SISBEN IV re-classification (2021) and movilidad may explain it.
- **Discriminating variation:** SISBEN IV score thresholds (RD); timing of POS unification by age group (2012 children/elderly first, then adults 2013) as staggered treatment; PILA formal contributions (restricted) vs BDUA (ADRES).
- **Data:** BDUA aggregates (ADRES, public); GEIH labor (DANE, public); PILA microdata restricted.
- **Puzzle-ness: MEDIUM. Research promise: MEDIUM** (well-trodden question; the post-equalization and SISBEN IV angles are new).

### Gaps
- No official mobility-flow statistics (subsidized↔contributory) found for 2020-2025.
